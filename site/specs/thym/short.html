Thym 0: the MacroCOSM language, its compiler and its runtime

Version 0 of the language, written 2026-11-01 for enough 0.5.0. It was a draft until checkpoint A of the 0.5.0 round, when the reference compiler and runtime first passed the conformance suite on a real model (2026-11-01, the night of the same day). From then on it is the specification, and enough/thym/ is its reference implementation. Where the two disagree, the reference wins and this text is corrected (section 20.4), as docs/MACROCOSM_OES.md promises for its reader.

The design behind this document, with the owner's rulings, is docs/thym-architecture.md (local). Thym is folded into the MacroCOSM specification, docs/MACROCOSM_OES.md, and its words (item, kind, volume, catalog, module, check, lens, atem) keep their meaning here.

Colophon

How to read this

Must and may. Must is a rule a conforming compiler or runtime keeps. May is a permission. Should is advice the checks do not enforce.

The family of words. The suffix says what kind of subject; the prefix says the form.

the whole, private, held by its subject the verified public duplicate an outsider's guess, unverified the owner's encrypted share the style sheet
a company (Comprise) holotype isotype paratype cryptotype —
a person (Ascribe) holograph isograph — (never) cryptograph holomorph (private) · isomorph (shared)

holo- the whole; iso- an equal, verified; para- beside, by another hand; crypto- hidden, by the owner. Crypto- means encrypted everywhere, and there is no outsider's graph of a person.

The words of a text. A text's meaning has MacroCOSM's three densities: the fabula (the EMF twin: order-free material with stances and spans), the short (a telling) and the atem (one line, with focus, push and pull). The author's text is a telling too, and is never called a short. Thym counts the text as a density as well, so a text has four (section 5.2). Thym adds three nouns and three verbs:

  • a reading is a telling of someone else's fabula in your morph, local and labelled (section 14);
  • the null morph is the plainest telling that conforms, Thym's reference (section 13);
  • a package is the self-contained export of a text with its fabula, morph, context and released graph (section 15);
  • to transcode is to move a value mechanically: a deterministic move, the same answer every time (enumerate, compile, cut, project, band);
  • to transcognize is to re-represent a value by choices a mind makes: a move that asks the model, behind a contract (derive, tell, atem, expand, null, morph, read); the heavy lifting of re-cognition;
  • to transconceptualize is to transcognize with a change of frame: another morph, language, audience or occasion.

Move, program, plan, trace and coprocessor are the words of the model itself; section 2 says what each one is.

Words on screen. As in the MacroCOSM specification, the word a person reads comes first, and the word the files use follows in parentheses where they differ. Thym's words are mostly the files' own: a statement of a program is a step (P:step), a part of a plan is a node (R:node), and an entry of a trace is a record (R:record). A plan's node is an item of kind node; it is not MacroCOSM's file word for an item.

Blocks. A block marked thym is one complete program file: it starts with its version line, it compiles under the reference compiler with the standard moves, and its plan is well formed. A block marked cosm is one complete .cosm file that loads and lints clean. A block marked yaml is a fragment and may point outside itself. A block marked python sketches an interface. A block marked text is grammar, a transcript or a picture.

1. Provenance and the name

1.1 Lineage

MacroCOSM OES 0.0.1 gave enough one grammar for volumes of items: Comprise's Holotypes, Canon's catalogs, EMF twins, documents. Everything a reader does with them (load, check, draw) is generic. Everything that moves meaning between them was not: deriving a twin, compiling a short, writing an atem and projecting an isotype were each a Python function or a model prompt standing outside the format. Thym makes those moves first-class (section 2).

Three precedents shaped it.

  • DocOES (MacroCOSM section 18.5). A document is enumerated into items and compiled back byte for byte, so the written file stays the source of truth. A Thym program is enumerated the same way.
  • Lossy codecs. A codec specifies the stream and the reference decoder, and leaves the encoder free. Thym specifies what a move must preserve (its contract) and leaves the model free.
  • The Ideal State Artifact (Daniel Miessler, 2026). A thing is described by its ideal state in prose, its constraints and its decisions, with criteria that can be checked. A program's why, must and decided heads are that, and its contracts are the criteria.

1.2 The name

Thym is said THIGH-m: thigh with an m on the end, after Fukuyama's thymos, the part of the soul that wants to be recognised. Time passes, if the speaker aspirates the T. The word is not an acronym and has no expansion. It names re-cognition: a text re-known at another density, a company re-known in public, a person re-known without a name.

1.3 Stewardship

The owner of enough keeps this specification. A program's version line, thym 0, pins the grammar of this document: a program written under version 0 must compile under every later reference compiler. The language grows by moves, which MiniCOSMs declare (section 17.6), and never by syntax within a version.

2. The model on one page

   author's side                   the three densities                  interpreter's side
   context · graph · morph        fabula · short · atem                 context · graph · morph
        │                               ▲      │                              │
        └── derive ── tell ── atem ─────┘      └── read ── expand ── cut ─────┘
                 (moves, each with a signature and a contract)

   program.thym ──compile──▶ items (thym/1) + plan (R:plan) ──run──▶ outputs + trace (R:trace)
                              ▲ inspectable before anything runs      ▲ replayable from its records
                                     a model-backed step ──▶ coprocessor (behind the contract)
  • A value is a document that knows its type and its provenance: which move made it, from what, in which run. A text's type is its density (section 5).
  • A move is one named step. It has a signature (what it takes and gives), a flag saying whether it asks the model, and a contract: the named checks that must hold on its output. The MiniCOSMs that own the standard moves declare them; a program may define its own.
  • A program composes moves with bindings, choices and loops, and carries its reason in prose heads.
  • The compiler parses, resolves, type-checks, contract-checks, estimates, enumerates the program into items, and plans it. It refuses whole: a program compiles entirely or not at all, and the refusal names the line.
  • A plan is what a program will do, before it does it: a graph of nodes a person can read.
  • The runtime checks the plan against the machine (a model present and large enough; agent-free or not; the inputs present), walks it in order, runs deterministic moves in code, and sends model-backed moves to the coprocessor: the model, behind the move's contract. A run lands all of its outputs or none.
  • A trace is what a run did, one record per node. With the answers it recorded, it replays.
  • Six laws bind every implementation (section 7.2).

3. The design rules

  1. It reads. Every keyword and every standard move is a FEED headword, or an inflected form of one (takes, gives, has, covers). A program can be read aloud to someone who has never seen one, and they follow it. People learn Thym for years the way they learn a craft: more moves, more morph scales, more contexts, never more syntax.
  2. Typed by density. Every meaning value knows its density and its provenance. A move declares what it takes and gives, so an atem of a morph is a compile-time error.
  3. Moves are the primitives, and MiniCOSMs declare them. thym/1 declares the core; emf/1, doc/1 and comprise/1 declare their own (section 6.2). The language grows by MiniCOSM.
  4. Transcode by default; transcognize when marked. A move that transcodes is mechanical and deterministic. A move that transcognizes asks a mind to choose how to re-represent; it is marked asks in its declaration, carries a contract, and is the only place a run can vary. Everything a model produces is checked against the contract before it becomes a value.
  5. Programs are documents. A program carries its ideal state (why), its constraints (must) and its dated decisions (decided). The .thym file is the source of truth; its items, plans and traces are derived from it (section 10).
  6. Nothing partial. A program compiles whole or not at all. A run lands every output or none, and its trace says where it stopped and why.
  7. Portable by design. The grammar, the types, the contracts and the conformance suite are language neutral: .cosm in, .cosm out. The Python reference uses nothing a JavaScript runtime could not mirror (section 12.6).

4. Programs: the language

4.1 The file

A program file has the extension .thym. It is UTF-8 (a byte-order mark is tolerated), with LF or CRLF newlines. Its first line that is neither blank nor a comment is the version line, thym 0. After it come use lines and blocks, in any order. A comment runs from -- to the end of the line. Blank lines are free. Indentation is free too: the examples indent for the eye, and a compiler must read the same file with none.

A complete program, as it reads on the day the owner's drawing works end to end:

thym 0

program publish-story
  why: a story leaves the house with its fabula and its author's morph, so that others
       may read it their way and the author's way stays the author's.
  must: nothing in the package names the author.
  decided 2026-11-01: readings are local and labelled; the text is never replaced.

  takes story: text
  gives out: package

  let f = derive story                       -- text to fabula; asks the coprocessor under emf/1
  assert f covers story else "the fabula does not cover the text"
  let m = measure story against null of f    -- the holomorph: the departure from the null morph
  let c = context of story                   -- the surrounding volume's catalog, read-only
  let g = graph of story                     -- what the holograph released for this text, or nothing
  give package story, f, m, c, g
end

4.2 Blocks

A file holds four kinds of top-level block, each closed by end:

block opens with holds
program program <name> heads, then statements: the unit a person runs
move move <name>(<params>): <type> heads, then statements: a named program with parameters, called like a move (section 8.3)
morph morph <name> heads, then one line per scale, <scale> <1-5>; the formality line also gives its direction (section 13.5)
context context <name> heads, then one line per field, <field> <value> (section 16.2)

A move block may also stand inside a program block, before the program's statements; it is then local to that program. Names are lowercase, [a-z][a-z0-9-]*. A name is unique among the blocks of its file, and differs from every keyword and every move the file can see.

4.3 Heads

A head is a line at the start of a block, before any statement. The prose heads carry the block's reason; the signature heads carry its types.

head form what it is
why: why: <prose> the ideal state the block serves; one per block; its first sentence is the block's atem
must: must: <prose> a constraint the program keeps; stated, not checked (a checked constraint is an assert)
decided decided <YYYY-MM-DD>: <prose> a decision and its date; any number, in date order
takes takes <name>: <type>, <name>: <type> … the inputs; one line, or none for a program that takes nothing
gives gives <name>: <type> the output's name and type; one line; every path must then reach a give

Prose. A prose head begins after its colon and runs to the end of its paragraph. It continues on the following lines until a blank line, a line whose first word is a head keyword (why must decided takes gives), or end. Inside the paragraph any word may begin a line. A blank line, or a takes or gives line, separates the last prose head from the first statement. A compiler warns when a prose paragraph holds a line that begins with a statement keyword, since the writer probably forgot the blank line.

4.4 Statements

statement form meaning
let let <name> = <expr> binds a name once (section 5.4)
assert assert <cond> [else "<message>"] a check the program adds; false refuses the run with the message (section 7.4)
if if <cond> then <stmts> [else <stmts>] end a choice
for each for each <name> in <expr> [where <cond>] <stmts> end a loop (section 8.1)
emit emit [<slot>:] <expr> adds an item to the program's output volume, or, with a slot (emit employees: …), puts a value into that field of the projection; project consumes both (sections 8.2, 18)
give give <expr> gives the block's output; nothing after it on that path runs
withhold withhold <expr> [where <cond>] calls the move withhold: the items leave the output volume without a trace (section 18)
effect <expr> a move run for its effect (lint v, trace); its value is dropped

4.5 Expressions and modifiers

An expression is a move call, a name, a literal, a field read, or an expression in parentheses. A move call is the move's name, then its operands separated by commas, then its modifiers in the order its signature declares them:

derive story
tell f as told
tell f by weight with rosa
expand (atem f) with f to 300 words
measure story against null of f
read f with mine in now
band (count staff) as [none, some] marked verified
project h as isotype
modifier after it used by
as a word or a list of words: an order (told, weight), a kind (isotype, paratype), a marking (verified, stated), the words of a band tell, project, mark, band
by an order tell
with a value: a morph, or a fabula tell, morph, read (a morph); expand (a fabula)
to a number and a unit: 300 words, 12 sentences cut (words or sentences); expand (words)
against a short: the null (null of f) measure
of a value null and the accessors fabula, context, graph, isomorph, which take no operand: of is their argument
in a context read
where a condition over items withhold; and a filter on a volume or a list (h where module is employees)
marked a marking: verified, stated any expression that gives an item or a word; short for mark … as …

A modifier's argument that begins with a move name is itself a call, and takes every modifier after it: measure story against null of f is measure(story, against: null(of: f)). Parentheses group where the eye would otherwise misread. A field is read with a dot: now.medium, o.kind.

Conditions join terms with and, or and not. A term compares two values with is, is not, in (membership in a list), <, > or =; tests a field with has (the field is present); tests coverage with covers (section 7.3); or is a bare value of type bool.

Bare words. A bare word stands for itself, without quotes, wherever only a word can stand: inside a list literal, after as, by and marked, and on the right of is and is not. In a where clause over items, a bare field name is the current item's field: h where visibility is internal; kind and module there are the item's kind and module letter, read from its id, never a field of that name (a Comprise aim has a kind field of its own). Where a word may stand, a bound name of the same spelling means its value, and the binding wins. Everywhere else a bare name must be bound. An unbound name is a compile error, so a typo never becomes a word.

4.7 Keywords

Every keyword is a FEED headword or a form of one (takes, gives, has and covers are forms of take, give, have and cover). The keywords of version 0 are:

thym use program move morph context end
why must decided takes gives
let assert if then else for each in where emit give withhold
as by with to against of marked
is not and or has covers
true false nothing

The standard moves (section 6.3), the densities, the orders, the markings and the stances are reserved too: no block, binding or user move may take one of these names. A few words are both a keyword or a type and a move (morph, context, package, graph, fabula, trace); where the word stands decides which it is.

4.8 The grammar in full

file      := blank* "thym" INT NEWLINE { use | block }
use       := "use" URI NEWLINE
block     := program | move | morph | context
program   := "program" NAME NEWLINE { head } { move } { stmt } "end"
move      := "move" NAME "(" [ param { "," param } ] ")" ":" type NEWLINE { head } { stmt } "end"
param     := NAME ":" type
morph     := "morph" NAME NEWLINE { head } { SCALE INT [ "toward" WORD ] NEWLINE } "end"
context   := "context" NAME NEWLINE { head } { FIELD value NEWLINE } "end"

head      := "why:" prose | "must:" prose | "decided" DATE ":" prose
           | "takes" param { "," param } NEWLINE | "gives" param NEWLINE
prose     := TEXT { NEWLINE TEXT }                  -- to a blank line, a head keyword or "end"

type      := "text" | "fabula" | "short" | "atem" | "item" | "volume" | "morph" | "context" | "graph"
           | "package" | "plan" | "trace" | "report"
           | "number" | "string" | "bool" | "date" | "word" | "list" "of" type

stmt      := "let" NAME "=" expr
           | "assert" cond [ "else" STRING ]
           | "if" cond "then" { stmt } [ "else" { stmt } ] "end"
           | "for" "each" NAME "in" expr [ "where" cond ] { stmt } "end"
           | "emit" [ FIELD ":" ] expr | "give" expr | "withhold" expr [ "where" cond ]
           | expr

expr      := call | filter | primary
filter    := primary "where" cond                  -- the items of a volume, or the members of a list, that satisfy cond
call      := MOVENAME operand { "," operand } { modifier }
operand   := primary
primary   := NAME | literal | URI | primary "." FIELD | "(" expr ")"
modifier  := "as" ( WORD | list ) | "by" WORD | "with" primary | "to" INT UNIT | "against" primary
           | "of" primary | "in" primary | "where" cond | "marked" WORD
             -- a primary after a modifier word that begins with a MOVENAME is a call, to the end of the line

cond      := term { ( "and" | "or" ) term }
term      := [ "not" ] ( primary cmp primary | primary "has" FIELD | primary "covers" primary | primary )
cmp       := "is" | "is" "not" | "in" | "<" | ">" | "="

literal   := INT | STRING | DATE | list | WORD | "true" | "false" | "nothing"
list      := "[" [ primary { "," primary } ] "]"
value     := literal | NAME

NAME      := [a-z][a-z0-9-]*          SCALE := one of the ten scale names (section 13.2)
FIELD     := [a-z][a-z0-9_-]*         UNIT  := "words" | "sentences"
INT       := [0-9]+                   DATE  := [0-9]{4}-[0-9]{2}-[0-9]{2}
STRING    := '"' ( [^"\\] | '\"' | '\\' )* '"'
URI       := "oes:" … | a relative path with no spaces

MOVENAME is a name that a manifest or a move block declares, WORD is a bare word (section 4.5), and TEXT is the rest of a line. Comments count as blank. A statement ends at the end of its line: a line may not hold two statements, and a statement may not span lines except inside parentheses or a list. There are no semicolons, no braces and no indentation rules.

5. Values and types

5.1 The types

type what it is carries
text a markdown text path or bytes, sha256
fabula an emf/1 volume its text's sha256 (spans resolve against it)
short a telling its fabula's sha256, its order, its morph (the null's when none was given)
atem line + focus + push + pull its source's sha256 and span
item, volume any OES item or volume URI, MiniCOSM, kind
morph a thym/1 morph: the ten scales derived-from (text digests) or declared (a morph block)

plan, trace, report sections 11 and 12; a lint report
number, string, bool, date, word, list of T plain values

The nine meaning types are text fabula short atem item volume morph graph package; a context is a meaning value too when it is an occasional context item. Every meaning value carries provenance (section 5.3). The plain types carry none.

5.2 Densities and what fits where

The four densities, text, fabula, short and atem, are types, and each move's signature says which it takes (section 6.3). A value of one density is never silently read as another: tell story, where story is a text, is a type error, and the message gives the fix ("derive it first").

5.3 Provenance

Every meaning value a move makes carries the move, its inputs' digests, the run (its trace's id), the step and the node. Written into a .cosm, this becomes the item's envelope provenance plus one field, made, which thym/1 lists in its envelope and every other MiniCOSM keeps as an unknown field:

provenance: {mode: derived, sources: ["notes.md#sha256:54055e8…", "oes:thym/2026-11-01-1042-7f3a/R/record/3"], confidence: 0.9}
made: {move: tell, run: 2026-11-01-1042-7f3a, step: 3, node: R:node/4, engine: "model:g40-04"}

made is the law of provenance (section 7.2) in one field: Canon never holds a telling that does not say what it is a telling of. A value a person wrote by hand has made: {move: none}, and a value read from disk without made is treated as hand-made.

5.4 Bindings

let binds a name once, in the block where it appears. An inner block (the body of an if or a for each) sees the names bound before it and may not rebind them. A loop variable is bound for the loop's body. A program's takes names are bound for the whole program. A move's parameters are bound for its body, and a move sees no binding of the program that calls it: it is called with values, never with the caller's scope.

6. Moves

6.1 What a move is

A move is one step, a transcoding or a transcognition, with four parts: a signature (its operands and modifiers with their types, and the type it gives); an asks flag (true when the move needs the model); a contract (the named checks that must hold on its output, section 7); and an owner, the MiniCOSM whose manifest declares it (section 17.6). A move transcodes unless its declaration says asks: true, in which case it transcognizes. A model-backed move may have a scaffold: a deterministic stand-in with a weaker output, which a run with --rules uses instead (section 19).

6.2 Who declares what

7. Contracts and the six laws

7.1 What a contract is

A contract is a list of named checks that must hold on a move's output before the output becomes a value. The compiler checks the static part: it refuses a composition that cannot satisfy a contract, such as a tell whose input is a text, or a project … as isotype that emits a bare count. The runtime checks the dynamic part on every output. A model-backed output that fails is repaired narrowly, retried within its budget, and then refuses the run (section 9). Most checks already exist as code in enough and Comprise; Thym names them and attaches them to moves.

7.2 The six laws

  1. Coverage. A telling says nothing its fabula does not hold.
  2. Grounding. Every span exists in its text; every term is in FEED or is a named entity.
  3. Monotone stance. No move strengthens a claim's stance. The ladder, weakest first, is hearsay assumed opinion deduced observed axiom. A telling may say a claim more weakly than its fabula does, never more strongly.
  4. Round trip. A package gives back its text (section 15.4), compile gives back a doc volume's markdown, and compile on a program's items gives back the .thym file: each byte for byte.
  5. Withhold, never falsify. A projection leaves what it hides without a trace: no placeholder, no count of withheld things, no gap in an order that reveals them.
  6. Provenance. Every meaning value says what it came from and which run made it (section 5.3).

7.4 assert

An assert line adds a check of the program's own. Its condition is evaluated at run time with the values at hand, and false refuses the run with the message. assert f covers story is the usual one; its step's contract names the check the condition uses (covers is coverage). A user move's assert lines become contracts of the move (section 8.3).

8. Control flow, bindings, user moves, use

Bindings are section 5.4.

8.1 Choice and loop

if … then … else … end chooses. for each … in … where … end goes through the items of a volume or a fabula (in told order: by span, then by id), the lines of a short, or the members of a list. where filters before the body runs. The loop variable is bound for the body. The body may emit, assert, let new names and run moves for their effect. It may not give: a program gives once, after its loops.

8.2 Three ways out

give gives the block's value, and every path of a block that gives must reach one. emit adds an item to the program's output volume, or, with a slot (emit employees: band …), sets a field the target schema names; only project consumes them, so a program that emits and never projects is refused, because the emitted items go nowhere. withhold marks items of a volume as withheld, and project leaves them out without a mark.

8.3 User moves

A move block is a named program with parameters. It is called like a standard move, with its operands in parameter order. It has a declared signature and inherits the contracts of the moves it uses, and its assert lines become contracts of its own. A move may call other moves. A move that calls itself, directly or through another, is refused in version 0. A move sees only its parameters.

thym 0

program story-in-brief
  why: a story, told plainly and then cut to a paragraph, for a listing.

  takes story: text
  gives brief: short

  move plain-paragraph(f: fabula): short
    let plain = tell f as told
    assert f covers plain else "the telling lost part of the fabula"
    give cut plain to 120 words
  end

  let f = derive story
  give plain-paragraph f
end

8.4 use

A use line stands outside every block, usually just after the version line. It brings another file's moves, morphs and contexts into scope: use oes:thym/house names everything the install's thym/1 volume house declares (its morphs and contexts, rosa among them), and use house.thym names everything a file beside this one declares. Two names that clash are a compile error, and the refusal names both files.

thym 0
use oes:thym/house

program tell-like-rosa
  why: a text told again in the house voice, for the help pages.

  takes t: text
  gives s: short

  let f = derive t
  give tell f as told with rosa
end

10. Programs as documents

The enumerator reads a .thym file into a thym/1 volume of kind program (section 17). Ids name their block: the program's name, or <program>.<move> for a move local to a program.

  • One item per block. A top-level block gives P:program/<name>, P:move/<name>, M:morph/<name> or C:context/<name>, with its heads as fields, its text verbatim in body, its lines, and its order among the blocks. A body runs from the block's first line to the next block, blank lines and comments included. A move inside a program gives P:move/<program>.<name>, with a body and lines but no order, since the program's body already holds it.
  • One item per statement. A P:step holds the statement verbatim, its line, which statement it is, the move it calls and every move its line calls, what it binds, what it uses, and whether it asks. Steps are numbered from 1 in the order they are written, within each block: P:step/<block>.<n>. An if or a for each is one step that lists the steps inside it; else and end are structure, not steps. A block lists only its top-level steps.
  • Names and summaries, by rule. A step's name is its statement without indentation or comment; its summary is the comment's text when the line has one, else the name. A block's summary is the first sentence of its why, capitalised. A contract's summary says what its move must leave true of what it gives; an assert's names the condition. A plan's root summary counts its nodes and the ones that ask ("Seven nodes, three of which ask the model.", with number words up to twenty and "node" or "one of which asks" in the singular); a node's summary is its line's comment, else its name; a block with no why takes its first line; an assert inside a move says "The move's own check". Nothing is looked up by program.
  • One item per contracted call. A P:contract names a call's move and the checks it carries: P:contract/<block>.<n> for the step's own move, and <block>.<n>.<m> for each contracted call nested inside it, marked included. An assert step's contract names the check its condition uses. A call of a user move carries the contracts the move inherits.

compile on the items gives the file back byte for byte: the catalog's front (everything before the first block), then each top-level item's body in order, is the file. The docs viewer shows a program at three densities like any volume: its atem (the first sentence of the why), its short (the program as written, term-linked), and its fabula (steps, moves and contracts as cards, with "mentioned in" across programs).

A program is also a text for every other move: derive on a .thym file gives a fabula of what it says, and an atem of a program is the one line a listing shows.

11. The compiler

11.1 The stages

  1. Parse the file into a tree, by recursive descent over the grammar of section 4.8. The tree keeps each statement's line and source text.
  2. Resolve names, URIs, used files, and the moves every manifest in the registry declares (section 17.6).
  3. Type-check by density. Every expression gets a type, every call is matched to a signature, and the provenance of each value (what it will be made from, by which move) is worked out.
  4. Contract-check the static part (section 7.1).
  5. Estimate: count the model-backed nodes, the sizes of their inputs and the output budgets; work out the largest prompt the plan will build; and ask preflight whether the machine's model holds it.
  6. Enumerate into thym/1 items (section 10), written as <name>.thym.cosm beside the program when asked.
  7. Plan into a directed acyclic graph of nodes, written as a thym/1 volume of kind plan (section 17.3).

The nodes of a plan come in evaluation order, a nested call before the call that takes its value:

  • one node per move call, a marked modifier included (it is a mark node of its own), and one per assert; a call of a user move is one node;
  • one node per if, for its condition; each node of a branch says which branch it is in (then or else) and waits on the condition;
  • for a for each, a node per member for each call in its body when the collection is known at plan time; otherwise one node per call, marked repeats, that takes the collection;
  • no node for a let whose value is not a call; a filter (h where …) folds into the node that takes it.

A node takes names (inputs, bindings, or a temporary t<n> made by the nested call at node n) and gives at most one: its binding, the block's gives name, or a temporary. A node runs after the nodes whose outputs it takes. The node that gives a block's output also runs after every assert, emit and withhold before it, so nothing lands until every check has passed. Its after list leaves out any node that another in the list already runs after.

A plan is inspectable before anything runs: the docs viewer draws it with the graph lens, the cosm viewer lists its nodes with their asks flags and budgets, and agent-free mode refuses it on sight when it asks.

11.2 The package

enough/thym/ is one Python package: parse.py, types.py, check.py, enumerate.py, plan.py, run.py, coprocessor.py, trace.py, contracts.py, moves/ and cli.py. The moves are implemented in moves/, by subject (emf, doc, project, edit, morph, pack), as the manifests' code keys name them. The package imports the standard library, PyYAML and enough.macrocosm, and nothing else of enough. The engines (canon_derive, docoes, llm, preflight) plug in from outside, through the coprocessor and the moves' registration, so the compiler is vendorable, as the reader is.

12. The runtime

The runtime is non-traditional in four ways and ordinary in every other. Its values are documents. One of its instructions is "ask a mind", behind a contract. Every run leaves a readable account of itself. And programs, plans and traces are read in the same viewer as everything else.

12.1 Execution

run takes a plan and the program it was made from (the .thym file or its program volume; the plan's program.sha256 must match), since a filter's condition, a local move's body and an assert's message are read from the program, and checks the plan against the environment: the model present and large enough, agent-free or not, and every input present with the digest the plan recorded. It then walks the nodes in a topological order. Independent nodes may run in parallel or in sequence. A deterministic node calls its move's implementation. A model-backed node builds the coprocessor call from the move's contract and the node's inputs. A failing node refuses the run: outputs already made are discarded, nothing is written to the target paths, and the trace records how far the run got. Outputs are written only when every node has succeeded, all at once, with the trace beside them.

12.2 The coprocessor

One interface; three implementations in enough; any number elsewhere.

class Coprocessor(Protocol):
    def complete(self, contract: Contract, inputs: Inputs, *, budget: Budget) -> Candidate: ...

Contract  = {move, schema, checks, repair}                         # what the move needs back and what must hold
Inputs    = {name: (value, digest)}                                # the node's operands, by name
Budget    = {attempts, output_words, prompt_tokens_max, seconds}   # the node's budget and the machine's limits
Candidate = {output, engine, attempts, tokens, seconds, checks: [{name, ok, why}]}
  • ModelCoprocessor is enough's: llm.complete with canon_derive's machinery, generalised. It asks for schema'd output; makes the narrow repairs the contract allows (drop an item whose span does not resolve, normalise a stance word, re-ask once with the failed checks listed); keeps within its attempts; and applies the lexicon floor and the span checks. An answer that is to be YAML and has prose before, after or instead of it (a narration, a heading, a second block, a block left open) fails schema on that attempt, before any other check, and is asked again for only the YAML; the runtime reads it so too, so a replay fails it again. An answer an engine reads for a move (the whole text's atem under derive) that the engine cannot read fails schema likewise, and never becomes open questions under green checks.
  • RefusingCoprocessor is agent-free's. A plan with model-backed nodes is refused at plan time with their count (section 19), so it never reaches complete.
  • ReplayCoprocessor is the conformance suite's. It answers each call with the candidate recorded for that node and attempt, and refuses when the contract or the input digests differ from the record's. So a trace plus its recorded candidates re-runs to the same outputs on any conforming runtime. That is Thym's answer to non-determinism: contained by contracts, reproduced by replay.

12.3 Traces

A run writes one thym/1 volume of kind trace. Its catalog names the run; the program (name and sha256); the plan's sha256; the environment (enough's version, the model's id and context size, agent-free or not, the machine's memory band); when the run started and ended; and its outcome. One R:record item per node holds the node, the move, the digests of the inputs and outputs, and the candidate's fields: the engine (code for a deterministic node, rules for a scaffold, model:<id>, or skipped for a node on the branch not taken), attempts, tokens, seconds, and every check with its verdict and, for a failed check, why; a node asked more than once also keeps tries, each failed attempt's checks with their reasons, so a refused node explains all its tries. A record's tokens are its requests' and those of the model calls its engines made, as the engine reports them where it does, else estimated; the environment's context size is the model's window, read when a model runs and 0 when none does; and the trace's root item names the engines whose calls the run recorded (engines, section 12.4). The trace also carries a copy of its plan's nodes, so that its records' node references resolve inside the volume and the trace lints clean on its own. Traces live under ENOUGH_THYM_ROOT/runs/ as <run-id>.trace.cosm, with a copy beside the outputs when they land in a volume. A value's made names its run, and so its trace. The docs viewer shows a trace at the fabula density as cards; its atem is one line: "derived 17 items from close.md in 11 s on g40-04, all checks green".

12.4 Replay

run --replay <run-id> runs that run's plan with the ReplayCoprocessor, over the candidates recorded under the run as candidates/<node>[-<k>].<attempt>.cosm (a node that asks more than once records each answer). The outputs must be byte-identical to the recorded run's: they carry the recorded run's id in made, and the replay's own trace takes a new id with replay_of naming the recorded run. A difference is a conformance failure of the runtime, not of the program.

One counter per node keys every call the node makes, so that no two collide: its first request is <node>; every later request, and every model call one of the host's engines makes for it (Canon reads each passage through the coprocessor), is <node>-<k>, k counted from 1 across both; and every call it makes to an engine (FEED's lookup, Canon's derivation, DocOES, the surrounding catalog) is <node>-e<k>, recorded once, at attempt 1, as {engine, inputs: {call}, keys, tokens, seconds, output}: the engine and the function called, the sha256 of the call's arguments in canonical form, the keys of the model calls the engine made while it ran, what they cost, and what the engine answered (raised, in place of output, for a call that raised). Only a node's own engine calls are recorded; an engine that calls another records the outer call, whose answer holds the inner. A replay answers every engine call from its record and runs no engine, so it needs no dictionary and no engine; it refuses at the first input, call or prompt whose digest differs from the record's, before any engine or model work. A run whose trace names no engines was recorded before engine calls were, and replays with the host's live engines.

12.5 Budgets and the 16 GB rule

A plan carries the largest prompt it will build. At plan time the runtime asks preflight whether the machine's model holds it; on a small machine a program may be refused with the number and the fix, as chat is today. Every node has an attempts budget (3 for a model-backed node, 1 otherwise) and an output budget in words (the call's to N words, or else the machine's). The trace records what each node cost.

12.6 Portability

No Python-only semantics. No reliance on mapping order beyond what the COSM YAML subset fixes. No floats in contracts: bands and scales are integers, and the ratios of section 13.3 are integers per hundred or per thousand. Digests are sha256: a text's over its bytes, a volume's over its catalog file, a file's always over its bytes, and a mapping's over the reader's canonical dump (macrocosm.dump: keys in file order, LF newlines, UTF-8); the compiler's own dump writes program volumes for people, with block scalars, and is never a digest's form. File paths are URIs. Time is UTC in traces and local only in file names. A JavaScript runtime that passes the conformance suite is a conforming runtime, and the docs viewer could then make readings in the page.

13. The null morph, the ten scales, morphs

13.1 The null morph

The null morph is Thym's reference telling of a fabula. It tells in told order, one claim per sentence, in the commonest FEED words that still say it. It says every stance in a fixed phrase ("the author holds that", "it is assumed that", "reportedly"). It has no figure, no humour, no allusion and no contraction, and it is in the third person unless the fabula's claims are in the first. It is one paragraph: a paragraph mark the model adds is read as none, never as a departure. It is model-backed, with the tightest contract in the language, null-pinned: every scale is 1. Its scaffold is the ordered outline told in the items' own sentences, which --rules gives (section 19). The null morph exists so that morphs are differences: a morph is what a text does that the null telling of its fabula does not.

13.2 The ten scales

Each scale is an integer from 1 to 5. 1 is the null's value on every scale, and 5 the farthest departure. Seven scales are statistics, six over the text and analytical-depth over its fabula; three are the model's judgement, with examples. Every word a measurement counts is a FEED headword (section 13.3).

The word lists above are version 0's seed; the reference keeps them in enough/thym/moves/morph.py. The statistics read English: the lists, the honorifics, the stance phrases, FEED's bands and the rule of nine letters are English's, so a text in another language is measured as if it were English (its judgement scales still hold, since the model reads the language); a later version may give the lists per language.

13.4 Holomorph and isomorph

A holomorph is measure t against null of f, for a person's text t with fabula f: each scale's value as a departure from the null, with its evidence. It is derived from any text the person writes with Ascribe active, and it sharpens over a corpus: a corpus's morph is the scale-wise median of its texts' morphs, and each scale's confidence rises with the number of texts. A person may pin a scale by hand; the pin wins, and the scale is listed as pinned.

analytical-depth is carried from the fabula rather than measured on the telling: a text's depth is its fabula's. A telling may say fewer of the fabula's reasons than the fabula holds, never more, so the fabula's depth is a ceiling. The null tells the claims without the reasons that link them, and so sits at 1. In a read, the morph's value is the ask, up to that ceiling.

An isomorph is a holomorph the owner chose to share: the same shape, marked shared, released from the holograph by a program, as an isograph is. A publication may ship one in its package so that readers can read it the author's way. A person may keep several, one for letters and one for work.

13.5 Morph blocks and morph items

A morph may be declared by hand in a program file, one scale per line; the unlisted scales sit at 1.

thym 0

morph plain-letter
  why: a letter home: personal, a little hedged, never ornate.

  person-voice 4
  stance-style 3
  formality 2 toward casual
end

program letter-home
  why: a note told the way I write letters.

  takes t: text
  gives s: short

  let f = derive t
  give tell f as told with plain-letter
end

As an item, a morph is M:morph/<slug> in a thym/1 volume (section 17.3). It holds scales (scale → value, all ten); toward, the direction of formality; evidence, basis and confidence, scale by scale, when it was measured; pinned (a list of scale names); derived (from: text digests; at; by) or declared: true; and shared.

14. Readings

A reading is read f with m in c: a telling of a fabula in the reader's morph (their holomorph, or an isomorph they chose), shaped by the reader's occasional context and made on the reader's machine. Its contract is morph's with two more checks. It is labelled: read_through: <morph name> in the file's front matter, and the reading footer at its end. And it never touches the source (never-source): its path is never an input's path, and no input file changes. A reading is saved beside the package or the text as <text-stem>.read-<morph>-<YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM>.md, in local time, taking -2, then -3, on a collision, as shorts do.

The footer (the owner's wording, 2026-11-01):

*a reading of notes.md through plain-letter, made here on 2026-11-01 by g40-04; the text stands unchanged*

The day is the UTC day, so a replay reproduces the line.

On screen a reading is always shown as "read through name", and the label is never hidden. A website that serves packages may offer readings in its own isomorph and let a visitor override it with theirs. Nothing a reader does changes what the author published. A reading of a reading is refused: read takes a fabula, and a reading is a short.

15. Packages

15.1 Folder form

<name>.thym/ (a folder; a program is a file of the same suffix, <name>.thym, and a library tells the two apart by the one being a folder with a package.cosm inside):

text.md            the author's text, exactly as published
fabula.cosm        emf/1; every span resolves into text.md
morph.cosm         thym/1, kind morph (optional: the author's isomorph for this text)
context.cosm       thym/1, kind context (optional: the occasional context, and the surrounding volume's catalog excerpt)
graph.cosm         an isograph or cryptograph excerpt (optional; only what the holograph released for this text)
package.cosm       the catalog: contents with digests, the program and the trace that made it, the notice

<name> is the stem of the text the package was made from, and a package is saved beside that text: notes.md gives notes.thym/, notes.package.cosm and notes.thym.enc. A reader of a library lists each form as one thing (Canon: kind: package, kind: sealed) and never opens the folder up as texts. enough's export makes a package by running a program over the text and its fresh EMF twin (package story, f, m, c, g, with context of story and graph of story), so the package's catalog names that program and run; it derives nothing. Unpacking verifies every digest and every span before it writes anything, then writes the text beside the package as <name>.md and its fabula as that text's twin, <name>.md.cosm, whose source.path and locators say the text's name again, with the morph, context and graph as <name>.morph.cosm, <name>.context.cosm and <name>.graph.cosm; it writes nothing on a failure, and never over a file that differs.

15.3 Single-file and encrypted forms

package lands the single-file form, with the folder form <name>.thym/ beside it. The single-file form, <name>.package.cosm, is the same catalog with each file's bytes as a block scalar under contents[].text, so that one file travels. The encrypted form, <name>.thym.enc, is the single-file form encrypted on the owner's machine and handed, with a key, to a chosen party: this is what a cryptotype and a cryptograph are. The cipher is Comprise's bundle scheme, comprise-bundle/0 (a data key per package, AES-256-GCM over chunks, an encrypted manifest), vendored into enough so that one cipher serves both subjects; the catalog's encrypted: {cipher, key: shown-once} field names it, and the key never leaves the owner (the owner's ruling, 2026-11-01). An index may hold only consented match tokens, hashes of characteristics the owner chose to make matchable, so that a search can match an encrypted package without reading it.

The encrypted form is a thym/1 package catalog that holds nothing in the clear: encrypted: {cipher: comprise-bundle/0, aead, key: shown-once, hid, bundle, keyring, manifest, chunks}, binary values in base64. The key is Comprise's recovery code (fifteen random bytes as twenty-four base32 characters, shown as six groups of four), which wraps the package's data key by PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 at 600,000 iterations, as Comprise's recovery keyring entry does; it is shown once and stored nowhere. enough depends on cryptography for AES-256-GCM (0.5.0, the owner's decision of 2026-11-02), so a package sealed in enough is Comprise's and opens there. Where AES-256-GCM is not available (a vendored runtime without that package), the same layout is sealed with an encrypt-then-MAC AEAD built from HMAC-SHA256 alone (aead: hmac-sha256-ctr: an HMAC keystream in counter mode, then a 16-byte HMAC tag, under keys split from the one key by HKDF), and the file says so; a package sealed with AES-256-GCM opens only where AES-256-GCM is available.

15.4 The laws of a package

The author's text is the package's telling as told: unpack gives it back byte for byte, and what is checked is that every span of the fabula resolves into it and that the fabula covers it. Every file's digest is in the catalog (digests). The catalog names the program and the trace that made the package. Nothing is in graph.cosm without a release recorded in the holograph (released). A package never names its author unless the author put the name in the text.

16. Context

16.1 Surrounding context

The surrounding context is the volume a text sits in: its Canon volume's canon.cosm, the project's catalog, or a site's served catalog when a website serves packages. It is read, never written. context of story gives the catalog excerpt around the text: its neighbours, the volume's name and scope, and the terms the volume defines. A program may use it to link terms, to choose a morph the volume prefers, or to tell a text in the light of its neighbours.

16.2 Occasional context

The occasional context is just-in-time: why this text, for whom, in what medium, at what moment, under what constraints. It is a thym/1 item of kind context with the fields purpose, audience, medium, moment, hurry (a bool), words (a budget), minutes (a reading time), language, and any field a program names. The author's occasion shapes the telling (a note for a friend, a brief for a board); the interpreter's shapes the reading (on a phone, in a hurry: the atem, expanded to three hundred words). A context may be declared in a program file and thrown away:

thym 0

context on-the-train
  why: five minutes on a phone, standing.

  medium "phone"
  hurry true
  words 300
end

program read-my-way
  why: a package is read on the reader's terms: in a hurry, the short way in;
       otherwise a telling in the reader's own morph, for this occasion.

  takes p: package, mine: morph, now: context
  gives r: short

  let f = fabula of p
  if now.medium is "phone" and now.hurry then
    give expand (atem f) with f to 300 words     -- the short way in
  else
    give read f with mine in now                 -- a telling in my morph, labelled a reading
  end
end

17.1 The catalog

A thym/1 volume is one file. Its catalog declares oes: thym/1 and says which of six kinds of volume it is:

morph <name>.morph.cosm one or more M:morph items
context <name>.context.cosm one or more C:context items

Catalog keys: oes, macrocosm, id, name, kind, source, front, built, program, plan, inputs, estimate, environment, started, ended, outcome, contents, made, notice, encrypted, nodes. The id is thym/<slug>.

17.2 The modules and their kinds

Kinds are unique across the modules, as in every MiniCOSM. Every item has the envelope (id, type, name, summary, status, visibility, provenance, pending, links) and may carry made (section 5.3).

Steps are numbered from 1 in the order they are written. In a move local to a program, <program> reads <program>.<move> (P:step/open-house.offer-line.1); a contract for a call nested inside a step adds .<m> (P:contract/publish-story.3.1), as section 10 says.

17.6 The moves: block

Any MiniCOSM's manifest may carry a moves: list. A MiniCOSM whose manifest enough does not edit declares its moves in a sidecar <name>.moves.cosm beside the manifest, a mapping {moves-of: <minicosm>/<major>, moves: [...]}; Comprise's is comprise.moves.cosm. When both exist, the manifest's list wins and the sidecar is reported. The generic reader keeps the block as an unknown key; the Thym compiler reads it. Each entry:

takes a list of accepted types for the operands, in order; alternatives joined by |; empty when the move's argument is its of
gives the type given
modifiers a mapping, modifier word → the type it takes (as: order, to: words, with: morph, of: package)

scaffold the name of the rules-mode stand-in, when one exists
code the reference implementation, informative: enough.thym.moves.emf:tell

18. Projections: the dial as a program

An isotype is a holotype transcoded: project h as isotype, under a program that gives every item a density (fabula, short, atem, or withheld) and a marking (verified, stated). The target schema's rules (comprise-isotype/0: default-deny, assertions not measurements, withhold never falsify, epochs and bands, verified only where derived, the standing gate) are the contract of project (section 6.3), so the schema stops being the ceiling and becomes the law. The isotype's draft step becomes three preset programs (minimal, fuller, house), and a verified owner may write their own. A release carries the digests of the program and the trace that made it. The Comprise side is a specification of its own, for comprise-dev; enough builds none of it and reads what it produces.

The example program, deterministic end to end:

thym 0

program open-house
  why: show what the house sells and what it wants, as the house says them; say how many
       people only in bands; keep everything internal inside.

  takes h: volume                  -- the holotype
  gives i: volume                  -- an isotype: comprise-isotype/0, and no more

  move offer-line(o: item): item
    give mark o as verified        -- an offer is what the house does now: verified
  end

  for each o in h where kind in [product, service, platform]
    emit offer-line o
  end
  for each a in h where kind is aim
    emit mark a as stated          -- an aim is the house's own words: stated
  end
  let staff = h where module is employees
  emit employees: band (count staff) as [none, some] marked verified     -- never a number
  withhold h where visibility is internal
  give project h as isotype
end

A preset that adds atem lines for the offers asks the model, and its plan says so; project itself never does. An unlabelled emit adds an item to the projection; a labelled one (emit employees: …) puts a value into a field the target schema names, and the schema says which fields may be filled. An item neither emitted nor withheld is left out as if withheld, by default-deny, and the plan says how many such items there are, so the author sees what the program never mentioned. The example is an illustration, not Comprise's minimal preset: Comprise emits employees only when the holotype holds people or says it has none, and always fills shape.markets; the preset says so.

19. Agent-free

In agent-free mode the coprocessor is the refusing one: a plan with model-backed nodes is refused at plan time with their count and the first one's line ("3 steps ask a model; agent-free is on"). A plan with none runs. With --rules, each model-backed move that declares a scaffold runs it instead: derive gives the rules twin, atem the labelled scaffold, tell and null the ordered outline, and measure the seven statistics scales with the three judgement scales pending. A move with no scaffold (expand, morph, read) still refuses. The trace says engine: rules for each scaffold, and every output is marked state: pending, as EMF's rules mode marks a twin. The viewer offers only plans that can run.

20. Conformance

20.1 A compiler must

  1. Read the grammar of section 4.8 exactly, and refuse a file whose version line is not thym 0.
  2. Resolve moves from every manifest's moves: block and every *.moves.cosm sidecar in the registry's folders, and refuse an unknown move with the nearest name.
  3. Type every expression by section 5, refuse every type error with its line, and work out provenance.
  4. Refuse the static contract failures of section 7.1 and the other compile errors of section 9, each with its check.
  5. Enumerate into thym/1 items that compile back byte for byte (section 10), and plan into an acyclic graph of nodes by section 11.1.
  6. Return refusals as values with stage, line, check and message.

20.2 A runtime must

  1. Refuse a plan at plan time when the environment cannot run it (section 9), with the count or the size.
  2. Walk the plan in a topological order; run deterministic moves by their reference semantics; send model-backed moves to a coprocessor under their contracts; check every output against its contract before it becomes a value.
  3. Land every output or none; write the trace with every record; name the run in every output's made.
  4. Replay a recorded run to byte-identical outputs with the replay coprocessor.
  5. Keep the six laws.

21. Decisions in this version

  1. Thym is a full procedural language folded into MacroCOSM. The OES gains a verb; thym/1 holds programs, morphs, contexts, plans, traces and packages; every MiniCOSM may declare moves.
  2. Its own text syntax, English-leaning, every keyword a FEED headword or a form of one. Programs read aloud, and moves, not syntax, are how the language grows.
  3. Values are typed by density and carry provenance in made; a value without it is hand-made.
  4. MiniCOSMs declare moves, in a moves: block or a sidecar; the compiler hard-codes none.
  5. Deterministic unless marked asks. The model is a coprocessor behind a contract, and replay by recorded candidates is the guarantee of reproducibility.
  6. Six laws: coverage, grounding, monotone stance (on a fixed ladder), round trip, withhold-never-falsify, provenance.
  7. Plan, then interpret. Nothing runs before the whole program is checked and planned, and a plan is a volume a person can read and refuse.
  8. Nothing partial, at compile time or at run time.
  9. Programs are documents, with why, must and decided, enumerated like a manual; the file is the source of truth.
  10. The null morph is the reference telling, and a morph is a measured departure from it on ten scales; formality alone carries a direction, and analytical-depth is carried from the fabula.
  11. Readings are local, labelled and never the source.
  12. A package is self-contained: a folder, a single file, or encrypted with a key that never leaves the owner.
  13. Context is of two kinds: surrounding (read-only) and occasional (an item, thrown away after use).
  14. The outsider's guess is a paratype. Crypto- means encrypted everywhere; there is no outsider's graph of a person, ever.
  15. The dial is a projection program, and the target schema's rules are the contract of project.
  16. Transcode, transcognize and transconceptualize are the verbs: the mechanical move, the minded move under a contract, and the minded move that changes the frame; transpile is retired.
  17. Python reference, JavaScript-portable; the conformance suite is a folder of .thym and .cosm files.
  18. The version line pins the grammar: thym 0.
