Thym

Edition 0.6.0 · revised 2026-11-06 · cosmoes.org home: https://thym.cosmoes.org/

This edition describes the thym/1 built-in and defines the language in two grammars; a program's version line, thym 0 or thym 1, names the grammar it is written in.

Thym is the transcognition language that connects OESes, with its compiler and its runtime: programs compose named moves over values typed by density, carrying meaning from one volume to another, whether the volume is a text's fabula, a folder of code, a document, a company's Holotype or a person's holograph. A compiler checks and plans a whole program before anything runs, and a runtime walks the plan, asks a model only behind a contract, and traces every run so that it replays. Thym's own volumes, thym/1, are a built-in of the core of MacroCOSM OES (https://macro.cosmoes.org/), specified here, and MacroCOSM's words (item, kind, volume, catalog, module, check, lens, atem) keep their meaning here.

enough (https://github.com/0gsd/enough) is the reference implementation of the language, its compiler and its runtime. Where an implementation and this text disagree, one of them is in error; the Changes appendix (section 25) records which was corrected.

Colophon

How to read this

Must and may. Must is a rule a conforming compiler or runtime keeps. May is a permission. Should is advice the checks do not enforce.

The family of words. The suffix says what kind of subject; the prefix says the form.

the whole, private, held by its subject the verified public duplicate an outsider's guess, unverified the owner's encrypted share the style sheet
a company (Comprise) holotype isotype paratype cryptotype —
a person (Ascribe) holograph isograph — (never) cryptograph holomorph (private) · isomorph (shared)

holo- the whole; iso- an equal, verified; para- beside, by another hand; crypto- hidden, by the owner. Crypto- means encrypted everywhere, and there is no outsider's graph of a person.

The words of a text. A text's meaning has MacroCOSM's three densities: the fabula (the text's twin: order-free material with stances and spans), the short (an entailment of the fabula) and the atem (one line, with focus, push and pull). The author's text arranges the same material too, and is never called a short: the text entails its fabula, and the fabula entails a short. Thym counts the text as a density as well, so a text has four (section 5.2). To decompile is to make a fabula from an input, and to compile is to make a short of a fabula: those are the plain words, and in a program the moves are derive and entail (DocOES's byte-exact enumerate and compile keep their names). Each of these steps is an entailment, the step that expands meaning by reducing data: the whole entails what is made from it (MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.1). A fabula has a flavour, the value of its catalog's flavour: fabulan for prose, fabulap for verse, fabulac for code (MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.4). A fabulac's short is a cshort, source code rebuilt from it and never run, and its atem at depth is a catem, a text saying what a person can do with the program (section 20). Thym adds three nouns and three verbs:

  • a reading is an entailment of someone else's fabula in your morph, local and labelled (section 14);
  • the null morph is the plainest entailment that conforms, Thym's reference (section 13);
  • a package is the self-contained export of a text with its fabula, morph, context and released graph (section 15);
  • to transcode is to move a value mechanically: a deterministic move, the same answer every time (enumerate, compile, cut, project, band);
  • to transcognize is to re-represent a value by choices a mind makes: a move that asks the model, behind a contract (derive, entail, atem, expand, null, morph, read); the heavy lifting of re-cognition;
  • to transconceptualize is to transcognize with a change of frame: another morph, language, audience or occasion.

Transcoding and transcognizing meet in entailment, and the line between them stays where it is. A projection (project, section 18) is an entailment made by rule, and holds by construction. entail makes an entailment by a model under contract, and what it makes holds only because its checks hold it: coverage, no-invention and stance-kept (section 7.3).

Move, program, plan, trace and coprocessor are the words of the model itself; section 2 says what each one is. The second grammar, thym 1, adds a few more: a folder (a directory tree read under one fixed rule, section 20), a line and a change (section 21), a contract block (a check a program defines, section 7.5), and the dialectic's questions and answers (section 22).

Words on screen. As in the MacroCOSM specification, the word a person reads comes first, and the word the files use follows in parentheses where they differ. Thym's words are mostly the files' own: a statement of a program is a step (P:step), a part of a plan is a node (R:node), and an entry of a trace is a record (R:record). A plan's node is an item of kind node; it is not MacroCOSM's file word for an item.

Blocks. A block marked thym is one complete program file: it starts with its version line (thym 0 or thym 1), it compiles under a conforming compiler with the standard moves, and its plan is well formed. A block marked cosm is one complete .cosm file that loads and lints clean. A block marked yaml is a fragment and may point outside itself. A block marked python sketches an interface. A block marked text is grammar, a transcript or a picture.

1. Provenance and the name

1.1 Lineage

MacroCOSM OES (https://macro.cosmoes.org/) gives one grammar for volumes of items: Comprise's Holotypes, the home file system's catalogs (Canon), fabulas, documents. Everything a reader does with them (load, check, draw) is generic. Before Thym, everything that moves meaning between them was not: deriving a twin, compiling a short, writing an atem and projecting an isotype were each a function or a model prompt standing outside the format. Thym makes those moves first-class (section 2), and so connects one OES to another: a program reads a company's Holotype and writes its isotype, or reads a folder of code and writes its fabula.

Three precedents shaped it.

  • DocOES (MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.5, https://macro.cosmoes.org/). A document is enumerated into items and compiled back byte for byte, so the written file stays the source of truth. A Thym program is enumerated the same way.
  • Lossy codecs. A codec specifies the stream and the reference decoder, and leaves the encoder free. Thym specifies what a move must preserve (its contract) and leaves the model free.
  • The Ideal State Artifact (Daniel Miessler, 2026). A thing is described by its ideal state in prose, its constraints and its decisions, with criteria that can be checked. A program's why, must and decided heads are that, and its contracts are the criteria.

1.2 The name

Thym is said THIGH-m: thigh with an m on the end, after Fukuyama's thymos, the part of the soul that wants to be recognised. Time passes, if the speaker aspirates the T. The word is not an acronym and has no expansion. It names re-cognition: a text re-known at another density, a company re-known in public, a person re-known without a name.

1.3 Stewardship

Thym is stewarded by Verroes (Graham Smith). The specification is revised at its home, https://thym.cosmoes.org/; each revision is an edition, and the Changes appendix (section 25) records what each edition changed. A program's version line pins the grammar it is written in, thym 0 or thym 1 (section 4.9): a program written under a grammar version must compile under every later conforming compiler, and compile the same way. The language grows by moves, which the core's built-ins and the MiniCOSMs declare (section 17.6), and never by syntax within a grammar version. thym 1 is the next grammar version, and every thym 0 program compiles under every later compiler exactly as it did.

2. The model on one page

   author's side                   the three densities                  interpreter's side
   context · graph · morph        fabula · short · atem                 context · graph · morph
        │                               ▲      │                              │
        └── derive ── entail ── atem ───┘      └── read ── expand ── cut ─────┘
                 (moves, each with a signature and a contract)

   a folder of code ── derive ──▶ fabula (a fabulac) ── entail ──▶ cshort (code, never run) · catem (what it does)
   a fabula ── question ──▶ open questions ⇢ a person answers ⇢ record · settle ──▶ the fabula, settled
                 (two runs with a person between them; no run waits)

   program.thym ──compile──▶ items (thym/1) + plan (R:plan) ──run──▶ outputs + trace (R:trace)
                              ▲ inspectable before anything runs      ▲ replayable from its records
                                     a model-backed step ──▶ coprocessor (behind the contract)
  • A value is a document that knows its type and its provenance: which move made it, from what, in which run. A text's type is its density (section 5).
  • A move is one named step. It has a signature (what it takes and gives), a flag saying whether it asks the model, and a contract: the named checks that must hold on its output. The manifests that own the standard moves declare them, the core's built-ins and the MiniCOSMs alike; a program may define its own moves, and its own checks.
  • A program composes moves with bindings, choices and loops, and carries its reason in prose heads.
  • The compiler parses, resolves, type-checks, contract-checks, estimates, enumerates the program into items, and plans it. It refuses whole: a program compiles entirely or not at all, and the refusal names the line.
  • A plan is what a program will do, before it does it: a graph of nodes a person can read.
  • The runtime checks the plan against the machine (a model present and large enough; agent-free or not; the inputs present), walks it in order, runs deterministic moves in code, and sends model-backed moves to the coprocessor: the model, behind the move's contract. The model is chosen for the run: a local one, or a cloud model through a broker (section 23). A run lands all of its outputs or none.
  • A trace is what a run did, one record per node. With the answers it recorded, it replays.
  • Six laws bind every implementation (section 7.2).

3. The design rules

  1. It reads. Every keyword and every standard move is a FEED headword, or an inflected form of one (takes, gives, has, covers, keeps, lines). A program can be read aloud to someone who has never seen one, and they follow it. People learn Thym for years the way they learn a craft: more moves, more morph scales, more contexts, never more syntax.
  2. Typed by density. Every meaning value knows its density and its provenance. A move declares what it takes and gives, so an atem of a morph is a compile-time error.
  3. Moves are the primitives, and the built-ins and the MiniCOSMs declare them. thym/1 declares the language's own moves; the built-ins fabula/1 and doc/1 and the MiniCOSMs comprise/1 and ascribe/1 declare theirs (section 6.2). The language grows by moves, never by syntax within a grammar version.
  4. Transcode by default; transcognize when marked. A move that transcodes is mechanical and deterministic. A move that transcognizes asks a mind to choose how to re-represent; it is marked asks in its declaration, carries a contract, and is the only place a run can vary. Everything a model produces is checked against the contract before it becomes a value.
  5. Programs are documents. A program carries its ideal state (why), its constraints (must) and its dated decisions (decided). The .thym file is the source of truth; its items, plans and traces are derived from it (section 10).
  6. Nothing partial. A program compiles whole or not at all. A run lands every output or none, and its trace says where it stopped and why.
  7. Portable by design. The grammar, the types, the contracts and the conformance suite are language neutral: .cosm in, .cosm out. The Python reference implementation uses nothing a JavaScript runtime could not mirror (section 12.6).

4. Programs: the language

4.1 The file

A program file has the extension .thym. It is UTF-8 (a byte-order mark is tolerated), with LF or CRLF newlines. Its first line that is neither blank nor a comment is the version line, thym 0 or thym 1. The compiler reads the version line first and then reads the whole file by that version's grammar; nothing else in the file chooses the version (section 4.9). After it come use lines and blocks, in any order. A comment runs from -- to the end of the line. Blank lines are free. Indentation is free too: the examples indent for the eye, and a compiler must read the same file with none.

A complete program, which packages a story with its fabula and its author's morph (section 15):

thym 0

program publish-story
  why: a story leaves the house with its fabula and its author's morph, so that others
       may read it their way and the author's way stays the author's.
  must: nothing in the package names the author.
  decided 2026-11-01: readings are local and labelled; the text is never replaced.

  takes story: text
  gives out: package

  let f = derive story                       -- text to fabula; asks the coprocessor under emf/1
  assert f covers story else "the fabula does not cover the text"
  let m = measure story against null of f    -- the holomorph: the departure from the null morph
  let c = context of story                   -- the surrounding volume's catalog, read-only
  let g = graph of story                     -- what the holograph released for this text, or nothing
  give package story, f, m, c, g
end

4.2 Blocks

A file holds four kinds of top-level block, each closed by end:

block opens with holds
program program <name> heads, then statements: the unit a person runs
move move <name>(<params>): <type> heads, then statements: a named program with parameters, called like a move (section 8.3)
morph morph <name> heads, then one line per scale, <scale> <1-5>; the formality line also gives its direction (section 13.5)
context context <name> heads, then one line per field, <field> <value> (section 16.2)
contract contract <name>(<params>) thym 1: prose heads, then let, assert and for each lines: a check the program defines (section 7.5)

A move block, and in thym 1 a contract block, may also stand inside a program block, before the program's statements; it is then local to that program. Names are lowercase, [a-z][a-z0-9-]*. A name is unique among the blocks of its file, and differs from every keyword and every move the file can see.

4.3 Heads

A head is a line at the start of a block, before any statement. The prose heads carry the block's reason; the signature heads carry its types.

head form what it is
why: why: <prose> the ideal state the block serves; one per block; its first sentence is the block's atem
must: must: <prose> a constraint the program keeps; stated, not checked (a checked constraint is an assert)
decided decided <YYYY-MM-DD>: <prose> a decision and its date; any number, in date order
takes takes <name>: <type>, <name>: <type> … the inputs; one line, or none for a program that takes nothing
gives gives <name>: <type> the output's name and type; one line; every path must then reach a give. In thym 1 a program may give several outputs, gives code: folder, manual: short, and every path gives each of them once, by name (section 8.2)
keeps keeps <name> [of <output>] [against <name>, …] thym 1: the block's output passes the contract block or named check <name>; of names the output in a program of several (section 7.5)
depth: depth: <1-64> thym 1: how deep a move that calls itself may go (section 8.3)

Prose. A prose head begins after its colon and runs to the end of its paragraph. It continues on the following lines until a blank line, a line whose first word is a head keyword (why must decided takes gives, and in thym 1 also keeps and depth), or end. Inside the paragraph any word may begin a line. A blank line, or a takes or gives line, separates the last prose head from the first statement. A compiler warns when a prose paragraph holds a line that begins with a statement keyword, since the writer probably forgot the blank line.

4.4 Statements

statement form meaning
let let <name> = <expr> binds a name once (section 5.4)
assert assert <cond> [else "<message>"] a check the program adds; false refuses the run with the message (section 7.4)
if if <cond> then <stmts> [else <stmts>] end a choice
for each for each <name> in <expr> [where <cond>] <stmts> end a loop (section 8.1)
emit emit [<slot>:] <expr> adds an item to the program's output volume, or, with a slot (emit employees: …), puts a value into that field of the projection; project consumes both (sections 8.2, 18)
give give <expr> gives the block's output, once on each path; a path ends when its last output is given, and nothing after that runs. In thym 1, give <output>: <expr> names which output it gives (section 8.2)
collecting let let <name> = for each <name> in <expr> [where <cond>] <stmts> end thym 1: a loop that collects; binds the list of what its keep lines kept (section 8.1)
keep keep <expr> thym 1: adds a value to the list the nearest enclosing loop that collects is making
withhold withhold <expr> [where <cond>] calls the move withhold: the items leave the output volume without a trace (section 18)
effect <expr> a move run for its effect (lint v, trace); its value is dropped

4.5 Expressions and modifiers

An expression is a move call, a name, a literal, a field read, or an expression in parentheses. A move call is the move's name, then its operands separated by commas, then its modifiers in the order its signature declares them:

derive story
entail f as told
entail f by weight with rosa
expand (atem f) with f to 300 words
measure story against null of f
read f with mine in now
band (count staff) as [none, some] marked verified
project h as isotype
modifier after it used by
as a word or a list of words: an order (told, weight), a kind (isotype, paratype, isograph), a marking (verified, stated), the words of a band entail, project, mark, band
by an order entail
with a value: a morph, or a fabula entail, morph, read (a morph); expand (a fabula)
to a number and a unit: 300 words, 12 sentences cut (words or sentences); expand (words)
against a short: the null (null of f) measure
of a value null and the accessors fabula, context, graph, isomorph, which take no operand: of is their argument
in a context read
where a condition over items withhold; and a filter on a volume or a list (h where module is employees)
marked a marking: verified, stated any expression that gives an item or a word; short for mark … as …
before, after a line thym 1: insert (exactly one of the two, section 21)

In thym 1, as also takes a flavour's word: derive t as prose, derive t as verse, derive t as code; and entail takes as code and the catem's depths as line, as overview, as manual (section 20). The flavour names themselves are never keywords or moves.

A modifier's argument that begins with a move name is itself a call, and takes every modifier after it: measure story against null of f is measure(story, against: null(of: f)). Parentheses group where the eye would otherwise misread. A field is read with a dot: now.medium, o.kind.

Conditions join terms with and, or and not. A term compares two values with is, is not, in (membership in a list), <, > or =; tests a field with has (the field is present); tests coverage with covers (section 7.3); or is a bare value of type bool. In thym 0 a call inside a condition goes in parentheses: assert (words x) > 0.

In thym 1, three things change in conditions and loops:

  • A call on either side of a comparison needs no parentheses: assert words new < words old, where count parts is 0. A call in a condition ends at is, <, >, =, has, covers, and, or, then, else, a closing parenthesis or the end of the line. in ends it too, unless the move declares an in modifier, in which case in is the modifier (read f with m in now); write membership in parentheses then: (read f with m) in xs. Parentheses still work everywhere. A move's name on a side begins a call only when an operand, or a modifier other than in, where or marked, follows it; otherwise it is a field or a word, so where count is 3 reads the field count.
  • in with a text on its right: "TODO" in l holds when the line, string, short or text on the right contains the characters on the left exactly. Membership in a list is unchanged.
  • A where after a call in a for each header belongs to the loop, unless the move declares a where modifier: in for each l in lines of s where words l > 30, the where filters the lines.

Bare words. A bare word stands for itself, without quotes, wherever only a word can stand: inside a list literal, after as, by and marked, and on the right of is and is not. In a where clause over items, a bare field name is the current item's field: h where visibility is internal; kind and module there are the item's kind and module letter, read from its id, never a field of that name (a Comprise aim has a kind field of its own). Where a word may stand, a bound name of the same spelling means its value, and the binding wins. Everywhere else a bare name must be bound. An unbound name is a compile error, so a typo never becomes a word.

4.7 Keywords

Every keyword is a FEED headword or a form of one (takes, gives, has, covers and keeps are forms of take, give, have, cover and keep). The keywords of thym 0 are:

thym use program move morph context end
why must decided takes gives
let assert if then else for each in where emit give withhold
as by with to against of marked
is not and or has covers
true false nothing

thym 1 keeps every one of them and adds six:

keep keeps contract depth before after

contract opens a block, keeps and depth are heads, keep is a statement, and before and after are modifiers. depth is the head only where a : follows it; anywhere else it is a value, the field of a folder's file (section 20.1), so repo where depth is 0 holds the files at the folder's top. The standard moves a program sees (section 6.3), the densities, the orders, the markings and the stances are reserved too: no block, binding or user move may take one of these names. entail is a standard move from 0.7, and so reserved: a program that used entail as a name before 0.7 must rename it. That is the only incompatibility the move's new spelling brings, and none is known. tell, its earlier spelling (section 6.3), stays reserved. A few words are both a keyword or a type and a move (morph, context, package, graph, fabula, trace); where the word stands decides which it is.

4.8 The grammar in full

The grammar of thym 0:

file      := blank* "thym" INT NEWLINE { use | block }
use       := "use" URI NEWLINE
block     := program | move | morph | context
program   := "program" NAME NEWLINE { head } { move } { stmt } "end"
move      := "move" NAME "(" [ param { "," param } ] ")" ":" type NEWLINE { head } { stmt } "end"
param     := NAME ":" type
morph     := "morph" NAME NEWLINE { head } { SCALE INT [ "toward" WORD ] NEWLINE } "end"
context   := "context" NAME NEWLINE { head } { FIELD value NEWLINE } "end"

head      := "why:" prose | "must:" prose | "decided" DATE ":" prose
           | "takes" param { "," param } NEWLINE | "gives" param NEWLINE
prose     := TEXT { NEWLINE TEXT }                  -- to a blank line, a head keyword or "end"

type      := "text" | "fabula" | "short" | "atem" | "item" | "volume" | "morph" | "context" | "graph"
           | "package" | "plan" | "trace" | "report"
           | "number" | "string" | "bool" | "date" | "word" | "list" "of" type

           | "assert" cond [ "else" STRING ]
           | "if" cond "then" { stmt } [ "else" { stmt } ] "end"
           | "for" "each" NAME "in" expr [ "where" cond ] { stmt } "end"
           | "emit" [ FIELD ":" ] expr | "give" expr | "withhold" expr [ "where" cond ]
           | expr

expr      := call | filter | primary
filter    := primary "where" cond                  -- the items of a volume, or the members of a list, that satisfy cond
call      := MOVENAME [ operand { "," operand } ] { modifier }   -- no operand when the move takes none: fabula of p
operand   := primary
primary   := NAME | literal | URI | primary "." FIELD | "(" expr ")"

           | "of" primary | "in" primary | "where" cond | "marked" WORD
             -- a primary after a modifier word that begins with a MOVENAME is a call, to the end of the line

cond      := term { ( "and" | "or" ) term }
term      := [ "not" ] ( primary cmp primary | primary "has" FIELD | primary "covers" primary | primary )
cmp       := "is" | "is" "not" | "in" | "<" | ">" | "="

list      := "[" [ primary { "," primary } ] "]"

FIELD     := [a-z][a-z0-9_-]*         UNIT  := "words" | "sentences"
INT       := [0-9]+                   DATE  := [0-9]{4}-[0-9]{2}-[0-9]{2}
STRING    := '"' ( [^"\\] | '\"' | '\\' )* '"'
URI       := "oes:" … | a relative path with no spaces

MOVENAME is a name that a manifest or a move block declares, WORD is a bare word (section 4.5), and TEXT is the rest of a line. Comments count as blank. A statement ends at the end of its line: a line may not hold two statements, and a statement may not span lines except inside parentheses or a list. There are no semicolons, no braces and no indentation rules.

4.9 Two grammars

A conforming compiler reads both versions. The version line switches the tables the whole file is read by:

what the version line switches thym 0 thym 1
keywords (section 4.7) as thym 0 lists them adds keep keeps contract depth before after
head words, at which a prose head stops (section 4.3) why must decided takes gives adds keeps and depth
blocks (section 4.2) program move morph context adds contract
types (section 5.1) as thym 0 lists them adds folder, line, change
modifiers (section 4.5) as by with to against of in where marked adds before, after
grammar section 4.8, the first block section 4.8, with the thym 1 productions
the moves a program sees each declaration's thym 0 view each declaration whole
how a for each over a collection known only at run time plans one node per statement (section 11.1) one node for the loop (section 11.1)

A thym 0 file is read by the thym 0 tables exactly, so a thym 0 paragraph whose second line begins with "keeps" stays prose, and every thym 0 program compiles to the same items and the same plan under every later compiler.

This needs thym 1. When a thym 0 file is refused, the compiler compiles the same text again as thym 1. If that second compile succeeds, the refusal becomes a version refusal at the first line of the file that holds a thym 1 feature: "this needs thym 1: a loop that collects (let readings = for each …) at line 14; the first line says thym 0". Otherwise the thym 0 refusal stands unchanged. The second compile never changes what a thym 0 program means: it runs only on a file the first refused, and it only chooses the message.

What a thym 0 program sees. A move declaration may say since: 1, and then only thym 1 programs see the move; and it may carry a thym-1: mapping of what a thym 1 program sees in addition to, or in place of, its own keys (section 17.6). A thym 0 program sees each declaration without its thym-1: mapping and no declaration that says since: 1: its view is frozen. So a thym 0 program that binds a name such as record or lines still compiles, and a thym 0 program that calls a move only thym 1 sees is told it needs thym 1. The language still grows by moves: a later edition or a manifest may add a move to thym 0 by declaring it without since.

use across versions. A used file is read by its own version line, and its blocks mean what they meant there. A thym 1 program may use a thym 0 file, a thym 1 file, a program volume of either version, and any morph or context volume. A thym 0 program may use a thym 0 file and any morph or context volume; a used .thym file or program volume whose version line (in a volume, the first line of its front that is neither blank nor a comment) says thym 1 is refused at the use line, check version.

Enumeration and round trip hold in both versions: compile on a program volume gives the .thym file back byte for byte (section 10). A thym 0 file enumerates to exactly the items edition 0.5.4 gave it; new fields appear only on the new constructs of thym 1.

Relabelling has one cost. Changing a thym 0 program's version line to thym 1 can change the order of the items that a loop of several statements emits when it goes over a collection known only at run time. Under thym 0 each statement of such a loop is a node of its own and runs over every member before the next statement starts, so the items come in statement order. Under thym 1 the loop is one node whose body runs member by member, so they come in pass order. The items are the same; their order in the output volume may differ.

5.2 Densities and what fits where

The four densities, text, fabula, short and atem, are types, and each move's signature says which it takes (section 6.3). A value of one density is never silently read as another: entail story, where story is a text, is a type error, and the message gives the fix ("derive it first").

5.3 Provenance

Every meaning value a move makes carries the move, its inputs' digests, the run (its trace's id), the step and the node. Written into a .cosm, this becomes the item's envelope provenance plus one field, made, which thym/1 lists in its envelope and every other format keeps as an unknown field:

provenance: {mode: derived, sources: ["notes.md#sha256:54055e8…", "oes:thym/2026-11-01-1042-7f3a/R/record/3"], confidence: 0.9}
made: {move: entail, run: 2026-11-01-1042-7f3a, step: 3, node: R:node/4, engine: "model:g40-04", route: local}

engine is model:<id> for a model-backed value, where the id is the model's own: the name a local model is known by, or the provider's resolved id for a cloud model. A model-backed value also says its route, local or cloud, and a cloud one its provider (section 23). A value made before this edition has neither key, and a replay of it writes none.

made is the law of provenance (section 7.2) in one field: the home file system (Canon) never holds an entailment that does not say what it is an entailment of. A value a person wrote by hand has made: {move: none}, and a value read from disk without made is treated as hand-made.

5.4 Bindings

let binds a name once, in the block where it appears. An inner block (the body of an if or a for each) sees the names bound before it and may not rebind them. A loop variable is bound for the loop's body. A program's takes names are bound for the whole program. A move's parameters are bound for its body, and a move sees no binding of the program that calls it: it is called with values, never with the caller's scope.

6. Moves

6.1 What a move is

A move is one step, a transcoding or a transcognition, with four parts: a signature (its operands and modifiers with their types, and the type it gives); an asks flag (true when the move needs the model); a contract (the named checks that must hold on its output, section 7); and an owner, the built-in or the MiniCOSM whose manifest declares it (section 17.6). A move transcodes unless its declaration says asks: true, in which case it transcognizes. A model-backed move may have a scaffold: a deterministic stand-in with a weaker output, which a run with --rules uses instead (section 19).

7. Contracts and the six laws

7.1 What a contract is

A contract is a list of named checks that must hold on a move's output before the output becomes a value. The compiler checks the static part: it refuses a composition that cannot satisfy a contract, such as an entail whose input is a text, or a project … as isotype that emits a bare count. The runtime checks the dynamic part on every output. A model-backed output that fails is repaired narrowly, retried within its budget, and then refuses the run (section 9). Most checks exist as code in enough and Comprise; Thym names them and attaches them to moves. In thym 1 a program may also define a check of its own, as a contract block, and promise that a move's output keeps it (section 7.5).

7.2 The six laws

  1. Coverage. An entailment says nothing its fabula does not hold.
  2. Grounding. Every span exists in its text; every term is in FEED or is a named entity.
  3. Monotone stance. No move strengthens a claim's stance. The ladder, weakest first, is hearsay assumed opinion deduced observed axiom. An entailment may say a claim more weakly than its fabula does, never more strongly.
  4. Round trip. A package gives back its text (section 15.4), compile gives back a doc volume's markdown, and compile on a program's items gives back the .thym file: each byte for byte.
  5. Withhold, never falsify. A projection leaves what it hides without a trace: no placeholder, no count of withheld things, no gap in an order that reveals them.
  6. Provenance. Every meaning value says what it came from and which run made it (section 5.3).

7.4 assert

An assert line adds a check of the program's own. Its condition is evaluated at run time with the values at hand, and false refuses the run with the message. assert f covers story is the usual one; its step's contract names the check the condition uses (covers is coverage). A user move's assert lines become contracts of the move (section 8.3). An assert refuses at once: use it where asking again cannot help, as with an input outside the band a program reads or a coverage law. Where a model's answer can be better on the next try, use keeps (section 7.5).

7.5 Contract blocks and keeps

In thym 1 a program may define a check of its own. A contract block is a named, reusable set of conditions over a value: contract <name>(<value>: T, <other>: U, …), its prose heads, then let, assert and for each lines, then end. It stands at the top level, or inside a program before the program's statements, as a move does. In the terms of section 7.1, it defines one named check.

contract shorter(new: line, old: line)
  why: a reworded line says less in fewer words, never more.

  assert words new < words old else "the reworded line is not shorter than the line it replaces"
end

The head keeps <name> [of <output>] [against <name>, …] stands in a move or a program that gives, and promises that the block's output passes the check. The kept value is the block's output and fills the contract's first parameter; the names after against are the block's own parameters (in a move) or its takes names (in a program), and they fill the second and later parameters, in order. <name> is a contract block the file can see (its own, a program's local one, or one a used file declares) or a named check of section 7.3; for a named check, the against names are its inputs. A program of several outputs says which one it keeps: keeps tidy of manual.

The static rules.

  • A contract's body asks no model, directly or through a move it calls: a contract checks, and never asks.
  • Its body holds let, assert and for each lines only.
  • Its name is unique among the file's blocks, and differs from every keyword, every move the file sees and every check of section 7.3.
  • keeps names a contract or a check that exists; otherwise check unknown-check, with the nearest name.
  • The kept value's type fits the contract's first parameter, and each against name the next one, in count and in type.
  • keeps stands only in a block that gives.

How it is checked. When a user move's body gives its value, each contract it keeps runs: its parameters are bound, its lines run in order, and the first false assert gives the verdict {name, ok: false, why: <the assert's message>}. If the move asks (section 8.3: a move asks when any move it calls asks), a failed kept contract runs the move's body again, within the node's attempts (3, counting the first). On every re-run, each model request the body makes carries the failed contract's verdicts among its repairs, so the model is told which checks its last answer failed and why; nothing else reaches the model. After the attempts, the run is refused with the contract's name as its check. A move that does not ask is refused at once, since running a deterministic body again gives the same answer. A program that keeps a contract has its output checked before anything lands, and a failure refuses the run, without a re-ask: a program is not one node.

A manifest's contract: list names checks the registry implements, and a contract block is a check a program implements. Both kinds of name stand in the same places: a plan node's contract item, a record's checks, a refusal's check. A manifest cannot name a contract block, because a manifest's checks are code.

thym 1

contract plain-enough(s: short)
  why: a help page stays short and never shouts.

  assert words s < 301 else "over three hundred words"
  for each l in lines of s
    assert not "!" in l else "an exclamation mark"
  end
end

program help-page
  why: a guide said plainly for the help pages, asked again until it is plain enough.

  takes guide: text
  gives page: short

  move told-plainly(f: fabula): short
    keeps plain-enough
    keeps no-invention against f
    give entail f as told
  end

  give told-plainly (derive guide)
end

8. Control flow, bindings, user moves, use

Bindings are section 5.4.

8.1 Choice and loop

if … then … else … end chooses. for each … in … where … end goes through the items of a volume or a fabula (in told order: by span, then by id), the lines of a short, or the members of a list. where filters before the body runs. The loop variable is bound for the body. The body may emit, assert, let new names and run moves for their effect. It may not give: a program gives once, after its loops (refused as type, section 9). In thym 1 a loop also goes through the text files of a folder, in path order (section 20.2), and lines of s gives the lines of a text or a short as line values (section 21).

How a loop runs. A loop over a list written in the program runs its statements once per member, as written. Over a collection known only at run time, thym 0 runs each statement of the body over every member before the next statement starts, and thym 1 runs the body member by member, as a unit, as a user move's body runs: a name the body binds, an if inside it and a nested call all take this pass's values (section 11.1 says how each plans). In thym 0, three such loops are refused at run with the fix, write thym 1, where a loop's body runs member by member: one holding an if whose condition reads the loop's variable or a name the body binds (the condition is read once, before the loop, and the refusal keeps the check that failed); one holding a let read later in the same loop (check loop); and one holding a for each nested inside it (check loop).

A loop that collects (thym 1) is a for each that is the value of a let:

let readings = for each t in repo where suffix is py
  keep read-file t, f
end

It reads aloud: let readings be, for each Python file of the repository, its reading kept. keep <expr> belongs to the nearest enclosing loop that collects. It may stand inside an if or a plain for each within that loop's body (an inner loop then keeps several per pass), and never in a user move's body unless that body holds the loop. Every keep of one loop keeps one type T, and the loop binds list of T; a loop that collects has at least one keep. The body may let, assert, emit, withhold, run moves for effect, and nest if and for each, and it may not give. The names the body binds are bound for one pass, and the loop's own name is bound after its end, never inside it. The list is bound when the last pass ends, and a refusal in any pass refuses the run.

thym 1

program aims-in-brief
  why: every aim of a house, told in one line, in the order the holotype keeps them.

  takes h: volume
  gives brief: short

  let lines-of-aims = for each a in h where kind is aim
    let s = atem a
    keep s.line
  end
  give join lines-of-aims
end

8.2 Three ways out

give gives the block's value, and every path of a block that gives must reach one. emit adds an item to the program's output volume, or, with a slot (emit employees: band …), sets a field the target schema names; only project consumes them, so a program that emits and never projects is refused, because the emitted items go nowhere. withhold marks items of a volume as withheld, and project leaves them out without a mark.

Several outputs (thym 1). A program may give several outputs, gives code: folder, manual: short, and gives each with a labelled give, shaped like the labelled emit: give code: entail f as code. Every path gives each output exactly once, by name; a path that misses one is refused (no-give), and a second give of the same output on one path, or a give naming no output of the program, is refused (type). A block with one output may label its give or not. A user move gives one value, since its signature says one type. All of a program's outputs land at once, or none do.

8.3 User moves

A move block is a named program with parameters. It is called like a standard move, with its operands in parameter order. It has a declared signature and inherits the contracts of the moves it uses, and its assert lines become contracts of its own. A move may call other moves. A move sees only its parameters.

Recursion. In thym 0, a move that calls itself, directly or through another, is refused (recursion, section 9). In thym 1 a move may call itself when it says how deep it may go: the head depth: <N>, with N from 1 to 64, under its why. Every move on a cycle of calls says depth:, and a move that says it and never calls itself is refused, as is a depth outside 1 to 64 (type). A program cannot call itself, since programs are not called, and cannot say depth:. A depth is one number that a person can read, a plan can show and every runtime keeps the same way; a rule of "a strictly smaller input" would need a measure the compiler can prove falls, which Thym's values do not have. A recursive move's contract and asks are those of every move on its cycle.

A call of a recursive move is one node, as any call of a user move is (section 11.1): its body is never expanded into the plan, so the plan is as finite as the program text. The node's budget holds the move's depth. At run, the runtime counts the move's calls on its stack, and a call that would nest the move one deeper than its depth refuses the run, check recursion, naming the pass in the person's terms: "give tree-of d (line 17): tree-of went 13 deep; its depth is 12". A depth bounds how deep a move goes, not how many calls it makes, so a plan with a recursive move that asks has an open count of model calls (section 23).

thym 1

program folder-outline
  why: a tree of folders, one line per folder, every folder under its parent.

  takes d: folder
  gives outline: short

  move tree-of(d: folder): short
    depth: 12
    let below = for each sub in folders of d
      keep tree-of sub
    end
    give join [(join [d.path]), (join below)]
  end

  give tree-of d
end

thym 0

program story-in-brief
  why: a story, put plainly and then cut to a paragraph, for a listing.

  takes story: text
  gives brief: short

  move plain-paragraph(f: fabula): short
    let plain = entail f as told
    assert f covers plain else "the short lost part of the fabula"
    give cut plain to 120 words
  end

  let f = derive story
  give plain-paragraph f
end

8.4 use

A use line stands outside every block, usually just after the version line. It brings another file's moves, morphs and contexts into scope: use oes:thym/house names everything the install's thym/1 volume house declares (its morphs and contexts, rosa among them), and use house.thym names everything a file beside this one declares. Two names that clash are a compile error, and the refusal names both files. A used path is relative to the program's folder and stays inside it: an absolute path, a .. step, or a link that leads out of the folder is an unknown URI, and the file is not read. A used file is read by its own version line, and a thym 0 program may not use a thym 1 file (section 4.9).

thym 0
use oes:thym/house

program like-rosa
  why: a text said again in the house voice, for the help pages.

  takes t: text
  gives s: short

  let f = derive t
  give entail f as told with rosa
end

A refusal is a value, not an exception: the CLI prints it and exits non-zero, the server returns it as the response, and the viewer shows it in place of the plan or the outputs. It carries stage (compile, plan or run), line, check and message, and like MacroCOSM's refusal it reads as a plain no.

A refusal speaks in the person's words, at every stage and in both versions. It names the statement as the person wrote it: the line without indentation or comment, cut to 60 characters with "…". It names that statement's line, and, inside a loop, the pass in the person's terms: for o = M:offer/seed-boxes (the item's id), for t = tool/cli.py (the file's path), for l = line 7 (the line's number), for x = the second member (a member of a written list, by its place; the plan's node names the same pass by the member as written, section 11.1). It names a value by the expression the person wrote, such as (count staff). It never shows a temporary (t3), a node's number, a node's id, an implementation's exception or the inside of a value as the implementation prints it. The shape is:

<statement> (line N[, for <var> = <member>]): <what failed, in the program's names>[: the fix]

For example: "let f = derive story (line 11): derive still fails coverage after 3 attempts: sentence 4 is not covered". The one exception is a fault of the runtime itself: that refusal says so, as a fault of the runtime and not of the program, and its record keeps the detail for whoever maintains the runtime. A plan keeps its temporaries in its nodes' names, since plans are for reading the graph; a refusal never quotes a node's name, and quotes the statement, which the runtime has because it runs with the program beside the plan (section 12.1).

10. Programs as documents

The enumerator reads a .thym file into a thym/1 volume of kind program (section 17). Ids name their block: the program's name, or <program>.<move> for a move local to a program.

  • One item per block. A top-level block gives P:program/<name>, P:move/<name>, M:morph/<name> or C:context/<name>, with its heads as fields, its text verbatim in body, its lines, and its order among the blocks. A body runs from the block's first line to the next block, blank lines and comments included. A move inside a program gives P:move/<program>.<name>, with a body and lines but no order, since the program's body already holds it.
  • One item per statement. A P:step holds the statement verbatim, its line, which statement it is, the move it calls and every move its line calls, what it binds, what it uses, and whether it asks. Steps are numbered from 1 in the order they are written, within each block: P:step/<block>.<n>. An if or a for each is one step that lists the steps inside it; else and end are structure, not steps. A block lists only its top-level steps.
  • Names and summaries, by rule. A step's name is its statement without indentation or comment; its summary is the comment's text when the line has one, else the name. A block's summary is the first sentence of its why, capitalised. A contract's summary says what its move must leave true of what it gives; an assert's names the condition. A plan's root summary counts its nodes and the ones that ask ("Seven nodes, three of which ask the model.", with number words up to twenty and "node" or "one of which asks" in the singular); a node's summary is its line's comment, else its name; a block with no why takes its first line; an assert inside a move says "The move's own check". Nothing is looked up by program.
  • One item per contracted call. A P:contract names a call's move and the checks it carries: P:contract/<block>.<n> for the step's own move, and <block>.<n>.<m> for each contracted call nested inside it, marked included. An assert step's contract names the check its condition uses. A call of a user move carries the contracts the move inherits, and, in thym 1, the contracts the move keeps, after the inherited ones.
  • What thym 1 adds. A contract block gives P:check/<name>, or P:check/<program>.<name> for one local to a program, with why, params, body, lines, order and steps (its lines as steps, P:step/<name>.<n>). A keep is a step of form keep; a loop that collects is a step of form for whose binds is the loop's name. A labelled give holds its output's name in slot. A block that keeps a contract lists it in keeps, a move that may call itself holds its depth, and a program of several outputs lists each in gives. A thym 0 file enumerates exactly as before: no field is new on any of its items.

compile on the items gives the file back byte for byte: the catalog's front (everything before the first block), then each top-level item's body in order, is the file. enough's docs viewer shows a program at three densities like any volume: its atem (the first sentence of the why), its short (the program as written, term-linked), and its fabula (steps, moves and contracts as cards, with "mentioned in" across programs).

A program is also a text for every other move: derive on a .thym file gives a fabula of what it says, and an atem of a program is the one line a listing shows.

11. The compiler

11.1 The stages

  1. Parse the file into a tree, by recursive descent over the grammar of section 4.8 for the version its line names. The tree keeps each statement's line and source text.
  2. Resolve names, URIs, used files, and the moves every manifest in the registry declares (section 17.6).
  3. Type-check by density. Every expression gets a type, every call is matched to a signature, and the provenance of each value (what it will be made from, by which move) is worked out.
  4. Contract-check the static part (section 7.1).
  5. Estimate: count the model-backed nodes, the sizes of their inputs and the output budgets; work out the largest prompt the plan will build; and ask preflight whether the machine's model holds it.
  6. Enumerate into thym/1 items (section 10), written as <name>.thym.cosm beside the program when asked.
  7. Plan into a directed acyclic graph of nodes, written as a thym/1 volume of kind plan (section 17.3).

The nodes of a plan come in evaluation order, a nested call before the call that takes its value:

  • outside a loop's body, one node per move call, a marked modifier included (it is a mark node of its own), and one per assert; a call of a user move is one node;
  • one node per if, for its condition; each node of a branch says which branch it is in (then or else) and waits on the condition;
  • for a for each, one node per statement of its body: for each member when the collection is a list written in the program, otherwise once, marked repeats, taking the collection. A call nested inside a statement of a loop's body, marked included, is part of that statement's node and is evaluated with each member; it is never a node of its own. Under thym 1, a loop over a collection known only at run time is one node, and its body runs member by member as a unit; a loop that collects is one node too;
  • no node for a let whose value is not a call; a filter (h where …) folds into the node that takes it.

A statement's node in a loop is named by its statement, with , for each … or , for x = <member> after it; it asks when any of its calls asks, and it takes every name the statement and the loop's where read. Under thym 1, a loop over a run-time collection whose body is one plain statement (neither an if nor a for each) gives exactly the thym 0 node; any other gives a node named by the loop's own line, with move for, repeats: true, members where known, and calls, the moves its header and its body call, in written order, once each. A loop that collects gives such a node, which also gives the loop's name. members: {at_most: n} is given only where the plan alone knows the collection's size: a list written in the program, a folder input (its files, counted before the where), or a list input (its members); a count is never read from a volume or fabula input, so a plan does not depend on where it was planned. A call of a user move that keeps contracts carries keeps, and one that may call itself carries depth in its budget.

A node takes names (inputs, bindings, or a temporary t<n> made by the nested call at node n) and gives at most one: its binding, the block's gives name, or a temporary. A node runs after the nodes whose outputs it takes. The node that gives a block's output also runs after every assert, emit and withhold before it, so nothing lands until every check has passed. Its after list leaves out any node that another in the list already runs after.

A plan is inspectable before anything runs: the docs viewer draws it with the graph lens, the cosm viewer lists its nodes with their asks flags and budgets, and agent-free mode refuses it on sight when it asks.

11.2 The package

In the reference implementation, enough (https://github.com/0gsd/enough), the compiler and the runtime are one Python package, with the moves implemented by subject (emf, doc, project, edit, morph, pack) as the manifests' code keys name them. The package depends on the standard library, PyYAML and the MacroCOSM reader, and on nothing else of enough. The engines (Canon's derivation, DocOES, the model client, preflight) plug in from outside, through the coprocessor and the moves' registration, so another program can carry a copy of the compiler and run it, as it can the reader.

11.3 The command line

enough's command line, as an example of the operations a compiler and a runtime offer:

python -m enough.thym check     <program.thym>                     compile only; prints the refusal or "ok"
python -m enough.thym plan      <program.thym> <inputs…>           compile and plan; prints the plan's atem and cost
python -m enough.thym run       <program.thym> <inputs…> [--rules] run; prints the trace's atem; writes outputs
python -m enough.thym run       --replay <run-id>                  re-run a recorded run (section 12.4)
python -m enough.thym trace     <run-id>                           show a trace
python -m enough.thym enumerate <program.thym>                     write <name>.thym.cosm
python -m enough.thym compile   <name.thym.cosm>                   the file again, byte for byte
python -m enough.thym conform   [<suite folder>]                   run the conformance suite

plan and run also take --model local (the default: the local model), --model cloud (the cloud model the host names) or --model cloud:<the provider's model id>, and --calls <N>, a ceiling on the run's model requests (section 23). An input is given as name=path, a folder's path included (repo=tool/), and a list input by its name once per member, in order (xs=a.md xs=b.md); when the program takes exactly one input, a bare path will do (python -m enough.thym run tidy.thym notes.md), and for a list, one bare path per member. An argument is name=path when the text before its first = is a name the program takes, and a bare path otherwise; a program of several inputs refuses a bare path ("tidy takes story and voice; give each as name=path"). The bare path is the command line's rule, not the grammar's, so it holds for both versions, and the plan it makes is the plan name=path makes.

Every enough command reads ENOUGH_THYM_ROOT (default ~/enough/thym/) for where runs live, and writes nowhere else unless it is told the path.

12. The runtime

The runtime is non-traditional in four ways and ordinary in every other. Its values are documents. One of its instructions is "ask a mind", behind a contract. Every run leaves a readable account of itself. And programs, plans and traces are read in the same viewer as everything else.

12.1 Execution

run takes a plan and the program it was made from (the .thym file or its program volume; the plan's program.sha256 must match), since a filter's condition, a local move's body and an assert's message are read from the program, and checks the plan against the environment: the model present and large enough, agent-free or not, and every input present with the digest the plan recorded. It then walks the nodes in a topological order. Independent nodes may run in parallel or in sequence. A deterministic node calls its move's implementation. A model-backed node builds the coprocessor call from the move's contract and the node's inputs. A failing node refuses the run: outputs already made are discarded, nothing is written to the target paths, and the trace records how far the run got. Outputs are written only when every node has succeeded, all at once, with the trace beside them.

A list input (takes xs: list of T, in either version) is given member by member, in order; an empty list is not an input given. The plan records it as {name, type, path, sha256, members: [{path, sha256}]}: path is the first member's, sha256 is over the members' digests in order, a folder member also has files and bytes, and the list's size is the sum of its members'. The run reads each member as it reads a single input of type T, held to its own digest, names it xs[2] in a refusal (counted from 1), and gives the program the list in the order given. The record of a node that takes the list names each member beside it, as xs[n], with its digest and path.

A run snapshots every folder it takes (section 20.3), a list's folder members each as inputs/<name>.<n>.folder.cosm, so that its replay needs no repository. A program of several outputs lands them all in the same step, or none. A folder output lands as a directory, outputs/<name>/, holding exactly the folder's files, and is renamed into place with every other output in that one step (section 20.4).

12.2 The coprocessor

One interface; three implementations in enough; any number elsewhere.

class Coprocessor(Protocol):
    def complete(self, contract: Contract, inputs: Inputs, *, budget: Budget) -> Candidate: ...

Contract  = {move, schema, checks, repair}                         # what the move needs back and what must hold
Inputs    = {name: (value, digest)}                                # the node's operands, by name
Budget    = {attempts, output_words, prompt_tokens_max, seconds}   # the node's budget and the machine's limits
Candidate = {output, engine, route, provider, attempts, tokens, seconds, checks: [{name, ok, why}]}

A candidate's route and provider say where its model ran (section 23); a candidate recorded before this edition has neither. A kept contract that failed (section 7.5) reaches the next request under repair, as the move's own failed checks do. A move that asks more than once may name under repair.failed what it could not take in its last answer; that reaches the next request too, after the runtime's own verdicts, so that a request asked again says why.

  • ModelCoprocessor is enough's: its model client with the machinery of Canon's derivation, generalised, on the run's route, a local model or a cloud model through a broker. It asks for schema'd output; makes the narrow repairs the contract allows (drop an item whose span does not resolve, normalise a stance word, re-ask once with the failed checks listed); keeps within its attempts; and applies the lexicon floor and the span checks. An answer that is to be YAML and has prose before, after or instead of it (a narration, a heading, a second block, a block left open) fails schema on that attempt, before any other check, and is asked again for only the YAML; the runtime reads it so too, so a replay fails it again. An answer an engine reads for a move (the whole text's atem under derive) that the engine cannot read fails schema likewise, and never becomes open questions under green checks.
  • RefusingCoprocessor is agent-free's. A plan with model-backed nodes is refused at plan time with their count (section 19), so it never reaches complete.
  • ReplayCoprocessor is the conformance suite's. It answers each call with the candidate recorded for that node and attempt, and refuses when the contract or the input digests differ from the record's. So a trace plus its recorded candidates re-runs to the same outputs on any conforming runtime. That is Thym's answer to non-determinism: contained by contracts, reproduced by replay.

12.3 Traces

A run writes one thym/1 volume of kind trace. Its catalog names the run; the program (name and sha256); the plan's sha256; the environment (the implementation and its version, the id of the model that ran and its context size, the route and the provider, agent-free or not, the machine's memory band); when the run started and ended; and its outcome. A record of a loop that runs as one node says how many passes it made, and holds a row <name>.<k> per value a loop that collects kept. One R:record item per node holds the node, the move, the digests of the inputs and outputs, and the candidate's fields: the engine (code for a deterministic node, rules for a scaffold, model:<id>, or skipped for a node on the branch not taken), attempts, tokens, seconds, and every check with its verdict and, for a failed check, why; a node asked more than once also keeps tries, each failed attempt's checks with their reasons, so a refused node explains all its tries. A record's tokens are its requests' and those of the model calls its engines made, as the engine reports them where it does, else estimated; the environment's context size is the model's window, read when a model runs and 0 when none does; and the trace's root item names the engines whose calls the run recorded (engines, section 12.4). The trace also carries a copy of its plan's nodes, so that its records' node references resolve inside the volume and the trace lints clean on its own. In enough, traces live under ENOUGH_THYM_ROOT/runs/ as <run-id>.trace.cosm, with a copy beside the outputs when they land in a volume. A value's made names its run, and so its trace. The docs viewer shows a trace at the fabula density as cards; its atem is one line: "derived 17 items from close.md in 11 s on g40-04, all checks green".

12.4 Replay

run --replay <run-id> runs that run's plan with the ReplayCoprocessor, over the candidates recorded under the run as candidates/<node>[-<k>].<attempt>.cosm (a node that asks more than once records each answer). The outputs must be byte-identical to the recorded run's: they carry the recorded run's id in made, and the replay's own trace takes a new id with replay_of naming the recorded run. A difference is a conformance failure of the runtime, not of the program.

One counter per node keys every call the node makes, so that no two collide: its first request is <node>; every later request, and every model call one of the host's engines makes for it (Canon reads each passage through the coprocessor), is <node>-<k>, k counted from 1 across both; and every call it makes to an engine (FEED's lookup, Canon's derivation, DocOES, the surrounding catalog) is <node>-e<k>, recorded once, at attempt 1, as {engine, inputs: {call}, keys, tokens, seconds, output}: the engine and the function called, the sha256 of the call's arguments in canonical form, the keys of the model calls the engine made while it ran, what they cost, and what the engine answered (raised, in place of output, for a call that raised). Only a node's own engine calls are recorded; an engine that calls another records the outer call, whose answer holds the inner. A replay answers every engine call from its record and runs no engine, so it needs no dictionary and no engine; it refuses at the first input, call or prompt whose digest differs from the record's, before any engine or model work. A run whose trace names no engines was recorded before engine calls were, and replays with the host's live engines.

A replay reads a folder input from the run's snapshot of it (section 20.3), never from the disk, so it needs no repository. A candidate recorded without a route replays without one: the outputs it gives carry no route in made, as they did when they were recorded. Model calls inside a loop or a recursion are keyed on the node's one counter in the order they were made (pass order, depth first), so a replay meets them in the same order.

12.5 Budgets and the 16 GB rule

A plan carries the largest prompt it will build. At plan time the runtime asks preflight whether the machine's model holds it; on a small machine a program may be refused with the number and the fix, as enough refuses a chat the machine cannot hold. Every node has an attempts budget (3 for a model-backed node, 1 otherwise) and an output budget in words (the call's to N words, or else the machine's). The trace records what each node cost. The rule reads the window of the run's model: a local model's, or a cloud model's context length. A recursive move's node carries its depth, and a run may be given a ceiling on its model requests, --calls N, which a cloud plan with an open count of calls must be given (section 23).

12.6 Portability

No Python-only semantics. No reliance on mapping order beyond what the COSM YAML subset fixes. No floats in contracts: bands and scales are integers, and the ratios of section 13.3 are integers per hundred or per thousand. Digests are sha256: a text's over its bytes, a volume's over its catalog file, a file's always over its bytes, a folder's over its files as the tree digest (section 20.1), and a mapping's over the reader's canonical dump (keys in file order, LF newlines, UTF-8). The folder rule is the same in every language and uses no ignore-file semantics; the compiler's own dump writes program volumes for people, with block scalars, and is never a digest's form. File paths are URIs. Time is UTC in traces and local only in file names. A JavaScript runtime that passes the conformance suite is a conforming runtime, and the docs viewer could then make readings in the page.

13. The null morph, the ten scales, morphs

13.1 The null morph

The null morph is Thym's reference entailment of a fabula. It goes in told order, one claim per sentence, in the commonest FEED words that still say it. It says every stance in a fixed phrase ("the author holds that", "it is assumed that", "reportedly"). It has no figure, no humour, no allusion and no contraction, and it is in the third person unless the fabula's claims are in the first. It is one paragraph: a paragraph mark the model adds is read as none, never as a departure. It is model-backed, with the tightest contract in the language, null-pinned: every scale is 1. Its scaffold is the ordered outline said in the items' own sentences, which --rules gives (section 19). The null morph exists so that morphs are differences: a morph is what a text does that the null short of its fabula does not.

13.2 The ten scales

Each scale is an integer from 1 to 5. 1 is the null's value on every scale, and 5 the farthest departure. Seven scales are statistics, six over the text and analytical-depth over its fabula; three are the model's judgement, with examples. Every word a measurement counts is a FEED headword (section 13.3).

scale 1 5 basis
register plain ornate statistics: rare words
sentence-length short long statistics: words per sentence
paragraph-rhythm even varied statistics: sentences per paragraph
analytical-depth claims only every reason statistics on the fabula: reasons per claim (carried, section 13.4)
figurative-density literal figurative judgement
stance-style flat hedged statistics: hedge words
person-voice impersonal personal statistics: first and second person
formality plain marked, toward: formal or toward: casual statistics: contractions, Latinate and casual markers
humour none much judgement
allusion none dense judgement, with the fabula's entities as candidates

formality is the one scale with two directions away from plain, so a morph that departs on it says which: toward: formal or toward: casual.

13.3 Measurement

Ratios are per hundred or per thousand words, compared as integers. The thresholds are this edition's. The conformance suite pins them, and a later edition may move them only with an entry in the Changes appendix (section 25).

scale measure 1 2 3 4 5
register the share of distinct words outside FEED's common band (FEED's bands 4 and up are common; a word with no band is rare when it has nine letters or more and common when it has eight or fewer, the nine-letter rule; names never count; the null short's register also exempts every word its fabula's items use, since it must say them), per hundred < 2 < 5 < 10 < 20 ≥ 20
sentence-length mean words per sentence ≤ 10 ≤ 15 ≤ 20 ≤ 27 > 27
paragraph-rhythm the spread of sentences per paragraph: standard deviation over mean, per hundred; needs three paragraphs, else 1 with confidence 30 < 15 < 30 < 50 < 75 ≥ 75
analytical-depth the fabula's reason links (supports, depends-on, resolves) per claim, per hundred < 10 < 30 < 60 < 100 ≥ 100
figurative-density sentences the model judges figurative (metaphor, simile, personification), per hundred sentences 0 < 5 < 15 < 30 ≥ 30
stance-style hedge words (maybe, perhaps, seems, might, could, arguably, probably, likely, somewhat, rather, I think, I suspect) per thousand words < 5 < 10 < 20 < 40 ≥ 40
person-voice first and second person pronouns and address (I, me, my, we, us, our, you, your) per thousand words < 5 < 15 < 30 < 60 ≥ 60
formality toward casual: contractions and casual markers (gonna, kinda, yeah, ok, stuff, interjections) per thousand words; toward formal: Latinate markers (utilise, commence, prior to, in accordance with, the passive in more than half of sentences, honorifics) per thousand words; the larger decides toward < 5 < 15 < 30 < 60 ≥ 60
humour sentences the model judges to be making a joke or an ironic turn, per hundred sentences 0 < 3 < 10 < 20 ≥ 20
allusion references to works, sayings and figures outside the text's subject, judged by the model with the fabula's E:work, E:person and quotations as candidates, per thousand words 0 < 1 < 3 < 6 ≥ 6

For every scale, measure gives a value; a basis (statistics or judgement); evidence, one to three sentences of the text that moved it; and a confidence as an integer per hundred (no floats reach a .cosm). Below 300 words or three paragraphs, a statistics scale reports a confidence of at most 50, and a judgement scale at most 40. The word lists above are this edition's seed; the reference implementation keeps them beside its measures. The statistics read English: the lists, the honorifics, the stance phrases, FEED's bands and the nine-letter rule are English's, so a text in another language is measured as if it were English (its judgement scales still hold, since the model reads the language); lists per language are reserved for a later edition.

13.4 Holomorph and isomorph

A holomorph is measure t against null of f, for a person's text t with fabula f: each scale's value as a departure from the null, with its evidence. It is derived from any text the person writes with Ascribe active, and it sharpens over a corpus: a corpus's morph is the scale-wise median of its texts' morphs, and each scale's confidence rises with the number of texts. A person may pin a scale by hand; the pin wins, and the scale is listed as pinned.

analytical-depth is carried from the fabula rather than measured on the text or the short: a text's depth is its fabula's. An entailment may say fewer of the fabula's reasons than the fabula holds, never more, so the fabula's depth is a ceiling. The null says the claims without the reasons that link them, and so sits at 1. In a read, the morph's value is the ask, up to that ceiling.

An isomorph is a holomorph the owner chose to share: the same shape, marked shared, released from the holograph by a program, as an isograph is. A publication may ship one in its package so that readers can read it the author's way. A person may keep several, one for letters and one for work.

13.5 Morph blocks and morph items

A morph may be declared by hand in a program file, one scale per line; the unlisted scales sit at 1.

thym 0

morph plain-letter
  why: a letter home: personal, a little hedged, never ornate.

  person-voice 4
  stance-style 3
  formality 2 toward casual
end

program letter-home
  why: a note told the way I write letters.

  takes t: text
  gives s: short

  let f = derive t
  give entail f as told with plain-letter
end

As an item, a morph is M:morph/<slug> in a thym/1 volume (section 17.3). It holds scales (scale → value, all ten); toward, the direction of formality; evidence, basis and confidence, scale by scale, when it was measured; pinned (a list of scale names); derived (from: text digests; at; by) or declared: true; and shared.

14. Readings

A reading is read f with m in c: an entailment of a fabula in the reader's morph (their holomorph, or an isomorph they chose), shaped by the reader's occasional context and made on the reader's machine. Its contract is morph's with two more checks. It is labelled: read_through: <morph name> in the file's front matter, and the reading footer at its end. And it never touches the source (never-source): its path is never an input's path, and no input file changes. A reading is saved beside the package or the text as <text-stem>.read-<morph>-<YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM>.md, in local time, taking -2, then -3, on a collision, as shorts do.

The footer:

*a reading of notes.md through plain-letter, made here on 2026-11-01 by g40-04; the text stands unchanged*

The day is the UTC day, so a replay reproduces the line.

On screen a reading is always shown as "read through name", and the label is never hidden. A website that serves packages may offer readings in its own isomorph and let a visitor override it with theirs. Nothing a reader does changes what the author published. A reading of a reading is refused: read takes a fabula, and a reading is a short.

15. Packages

15.1 Folder form

<name>.thym/ (a folder; a program is a file of the same suffix, <name>.thym, and a library tells the two apart by the one being a folder with a package.cosm inside):

text.md            the author's text, exactly as published
<twin's name>      the text's fabula, fabula/1, under the name its twin had (notes.md.fabulan.cosm); every span
                   resolves into text.md. A package made before this edition holds it as fabula.cosm, emf/1 read as a fabulan
morph.cosm         thym/1, kind morph (optional: the author's isomorph for this text)
context.cosm       thym/1, kind context (optional: the occasional context, and the surrounding volume's catalog excerpt)
graph.cosm         an isograph made for this text (optional; only what the holograph released for this text)
package.cosm       the catalog: contents with digests, the program and the trace that made it, the notice

A package has three forms, each saved beside the text it was made from and named by that text's stem, <name>: the folder form <name>.thym/, the single-file form <name>.package.cosm and the encrypted form <name>.thym.enc (notes.md gives notes.thym/, notes.package.cosm and notes.thym.enc). Section 15.3 is the rule for which of them package lands. A reader of a library lists each form as one thing (the home file system's kind: package and kind: sealed) and never opens the folder up as texts. enough's export makes a package by running a program over the text and its fresh fabula (package story, f, m, c, g, with context of story and graph of story), so the package's catalog names that program and run; it derives nothing. A package finds its fabula by its role in the catalog, role: fabula, never by the file's name, and nothing is renamed on the way in or out. Unpacking verifies every digest and every span before it writes anything, then writes the text beside the package as <name>.md and its fabula under the name the package carries it by (<name>.md.fabulan.cosm; from a package that holds fabula.cosm, the text's twin <name>.md.cosm), whose source.path and locators say the text's name again, with the morph, context and graph as <name>.morph.cosm, <name>.context.cosm and <name>.graph.cosm; it writes nothing on a failure, and never over a file that differs.

15.3 Single-file and encrypted forms

package lands the single-file form, with the folder form <name>.thym/ beside it; it makes the encrypted form only when sealing is asked for. The single-file form, <name>.package.cosm, is the same catalog with each file's bytes as a block scalar under contents[].text, so that one file travels. The encrypted form, <name>.thym.enc, is the single-file form encrypted on the owner's machine and handed, with a key, to a chosen party: this is what a cryptotype and a cryptograph are. The cipher is Comprise's bundle scheme, comprise-bundle/0 (a data key per package, AES-256-GCM over chunks, an encrypted manifest), vendored into enough so that one cipher serves both subjects; the catalog's encrypted: {cipher, key: shown-once} field names it, and the key never leaves the owner. An index may hold only consented match tokens, hashes of characteristics the owner chose to make matchable, so that a search can match an encrypted package without reading it.

The encrypted form is a thym/1 package catalog that holds nothing in the clear: encrypted: {cipher: comprise-bundle/0, aead, key: shown-once, hid, bundle, keyring, manifest, chunks}, binary values in base64. The key is Comprise's recovery code (fifteen random bytes as twenty-four base32 characters, shown as six groups of four), which wraps the package's data key by PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 at 600,000 iterations, as Comprise's recovery keyring entry does; it is shown once and stored nowhere. enough uses the cryptography package for AES-256-GCM, so a package sealed in enough is Comprise's and opens there. Where AES-256-GCM is not available (a vendored runtime without that package), the same layout is sealed with an encrypt-then-MAC AEAD built from HMAC-SHA256 alone (aead: hmac-sha256-ctr: an HMAC keystream in counter mode, then a 16-byte HMAC tag, under keys split from the one key by HKDF), and the file says so; a package sealed with AES-256-GCM opens only where AES-256-GCM is available.

A conforming reader of a sealed package refuses a file larger than its size cap before parsing it, refuses YAML anchors and aliases in the file's clear part, and bounds what its list of chunks may open to. The limits are the implementation's; that they exist is the rule, and each refusal is whole: nothing is opened and nothing is written.

15.4 The laws of a package

The author's text is the package's text as the author told it: unpack gives it back byte for byte, and what is checked is that every span of the fabula resolves into it and that the fabula covers it. Every file's digest is in the catalog (digests). The catalog names the program and the trace that made the package. Nothing is in graph.cosm without a release recorded in the holograph (released). A package never names its author unless the author put the name in the text.

16. Context

16.1 Surrounding context

The surrounding context is the volume a text sits in: its volume's catalog in the home file system (Canon's canon.cosm), the project's catalog, or a site's served catalog when a website serves packages. It is read, never written. context of story gives the catalog excerpt around the text: its neighbours, the volume's name and scope, and the terms the volume defines. A program may use it to link terms, to choose a morph the volume prefers, or to make a text's short in the light of its neighbours.

16.2 Occasional context

The occasional context is just-in-time: why this text, for whom, in what medium, at what moment, under what constraints. It is a thym/1 item of kind context with the fields purpose, audience, medium, moment, hurry (a bool), words (a budget), minutes (a reading time), language, and any field a program names. The author's occasion shapes the entailment (a note for a friend, a brief for a board); the interpreter's shapes the reading (on a phone, in a hurry: the atem, expanded to three hundred words). A context may be declared in a program file and thrown away:

thym 0

context on-the-train
  why: five minutes on a phone, standing.

  medium "phone"
  hurry true
  words 300
end

program read-my-way
  why: a package is read on the reader's terms: in a hurry, the short way in;
       otherwise an entailment in the reader's own morph, for this occasion.

  takes p: package, mine: morph, now: context
  gives r: short

  let f = fabula of p
  if now.medium is "phone" and now.hurry then
    give expand (atem f) with f to 300 words     -- the short way in
  else
    give read f with mine in now                 -- an entailment in my morph, labelled a reading
  end
end

17.4 Checks

The envelope checks the generic reader runs, and six of thym/1's own, which a Thym compiler and runtime run; the manifest states them as prose under checks.own, so a generic reader neither runs nor lists them, and lints a Thym volume on the envelope alone: program-compiles (a program volume's source compiles to these items, by its own version line), plan-acyclic, trace-complete (every node has a record, or the trace is refused with stopped_at), scale-range, digests (a package's files match its catalog), reading-labelled.

17.5 Renderers

program (the file compiled back; lens {kind: short, order: told}), plan (a flowchart of nodes along after), trace (a table of records: id, move, engine, attempts, seconds), and, by code, text-tree and compile.

17.6 The moves: block

Any manifest, a built-in's or a MiniCOSM's, may carry a moves: list. A format whose manifest another specification keeps declares its moves in a sidecar <name>.moves.cosm beside the manifest, a mapping {moves-of: <format>/<major>, moves: [...]}; Comprise's is comprise.moves.cosm. When both exist, the manifest's list wins and the sidecar is reported. The generic reader keeps the block as an unknown key; the Thym compiler reads it. Each entry:

key holds
name the move's name, a FEED headword, unique across the registry but for an entry that extends a move
takes a list of accepted types for the operands, in order; alternatives joined by |; empty when the move's argument is its of
gives the type given
modifiers a mapping, modifier word → the type it takes (as: order, to: words, with: morph, of: package)
asks true when the move needs the model; absent or false otherwise
contract the list of check names (section 7.3)
scaffold the name of the rules-mode stand-in, when one exists
code the reference implementation, informative: enough.thym.moves.emf:entail
says one line, in FEED words, for the viewer and the readvisor
since 1: the move exists for thym 1 programs only (section 4.9)
extends true: the entry adds as kinds to a move another manifest declares, and nothing else (its modifiers are {as: <kinds>}); a call of one of its kinds gives the entry's gives, keeps its contract and runs its code. Ascribe's project … as isograph and project … as allograph are one, an entry whose as lists both (section 6.2)
thym-1 a mapping of the keys a thym 1 program sees in addition to, or in place of, the entry's own: takes, gives, modifiers, contract, scaffold, and the three below; a thym 0 program sees the entry without it
gives-when a list of {as: <words>, gives: <type>} or {takes: <type>, gives: <type>}: the type a call gives when its as word, or its first operand's type, is one of these
contract-when the same shape, with contract: [...]: the call's contract when its as word or first operand's type matches; else contract
scaffold-when the same shape for scaffold; a rule may say scaffold: none, and a call it matches then has no scaffold, so that --rules refuses it at plan time

gives may also say same-as-with or same-as-in: the call gives the type of its with (or in) value.

18. Projections: the dial as a program

An isotype is a holotype transcoded: project h as isotype, under a program that gives every item a density (fabula, short, atem, or withheld) and a marking (verified, stated). The target schema's rules (comprise-isotype/0: default-deny, assertions not measurements, withhold never falsify, epochs and bands, verified only where derived, the standing gate) are the contract of project (section 6.3), so the schema stops being the ceiling and becomes the law. The isotype's draft step is three preset programs (minimal, fuller, house), and a verified owner may write their own. A release carries the digests of the program and the trace that made it. The Comprise side is a specification of its own, Comprise OES (https://comprise.cosmoes.org/); Thym defines none of it, and a Thym implementation reads what Comprise produces. A person's dial is the same move over a holograph, project h as isograph, to the schema of Ascribe's isograph (Ascribe (https://ascribe.cosmoes.org/), its section 14.1), whose rules are then the contract. project h as allograph makes the other projection a holder runs: the allograph, which keeps only the holder's experience and domain expertise and is the one thing of a holograph a registry lists (Ascribe, its sections 14.6 and 14.7). Each MiniCOSM declares the kinds it projects: isotype and paratype are Comprise OES's, and isograph and allograph are Ascribe's. Listing an allograph is the implementation's act after the run; no move sends anything to a registry. Ascribe's cryptograph and isomorph are made by the implementation, and moves for them are reserved for a later edition; it makes match tokens no more, so no move is reserved for them.

A projection is an entailment made by rule: a holotype entails its isotype and its paratype, and a holograph its isograph and its allograph (MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.1). What it makes holds by construction, since the schema's rules are its contract and no model is asked. entail (section 6.3) makes an entailment by a model under contract, and what it makes holds only because its checks hold it. The two kinds stay on the two sides of the line section 1 draws: project transcodes, and entail transcognizes.

The example program, deterministic end to end:

thym 0

program open-house
  why: show what the house sells and what it wants, as the house says them; say how many
       people only in bands; keep everything internal inside.

  takes h: volume                  -- the holotype
  gives i: volume                  -- an isotype: comprise-isotype/0, and no more

  move offer-line(o: item): item
    give mark o as verified        -- an offer is what the house does now: verified
  end

  for each o in h where kind in [product, service, platform]
    emit offer-line o
  end
  for each a in h where kind is aim
    emit mark a as stated          -- an aim is the house's own words: stated
  end
  let staff = h where module is employees
  emit employees: band (count staff) as [none, some] marked verified     -- never a number
  withhold h where visibility is internal
  give project h as isotype
end

A preset that adds atem lines for the offers asks the model, and its plan says so; project itself never does. An unlabelled emit adds an item to the projection; a labelled one (emit employees: …) puts a value into a field the target schema names, and the schema says which fields may be filled. An item neither emitted nor withheld is left out as if withheld, by default-deny, and the plan says how many such items there are, so the author sees what the program never mentioned. The example is an illustration, not Comprise's minimal preset: Comprise emits employees only when the holotype holds people or says it has none, and always fills shape.markets; the preset says so.

In agent-free mode the coprocessor is the refusing one: a plan with model-backed nodes is refused at plan time with their count, the route and the first one's statement and line ("3 steps ask a model on the local route, the first let f = derive story (line 11); agent-free is on"). A cloud model is a model too: agent-free refuses a plan on the cloud route exactly as it refuses one on the local route, at plan time, before any broker is asked. 20. Folders and code

thym 1 reads a folder of code as a value, decompiles it into a fabulac, and compiles a fabulac back into a cshort and its catems. The fabulac, the cshort and the catem are MacroCOSM OES's (its section 18.4.7); this section says how a program reaches them.

20.1 The folder type

A folder is a directory tree read under one fixed rule, with a digest. Its value is the list of the files the rule keeps, each {path, sha256, size, binary}, where path is relative to the folder, written with forward slashes and without a leading ./. The text of a text file is read when something walks or reads it.

The rule has no options. A folder holds every regular file under its root except:

  1. any path one of whose parts begins with . (so .git/, .venv/, .env);
  2. any path one of whose parts is __pycache__ or node_modules;
  3. symbolic links, which are never followed: a link is left out.

A file is text when its bytes are UTF-8 (a byte-order mark is tolerated), hold no zero byte, and number at most 1,000,000 bytes. Any other file is binary: it is counted and listed, and it is never read as text. A text's lines are its line feeds, and one more when its last line has none. The rule uses no ignore-file semantics, on purpose: those semantics are a program, not a rule, and the rule must be the same in every runtime.

The tree digest is MacroCOSM's, computed exactly as MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.4.1, gives it: over the files that section's rule reads, in the order of their paths' UTF-8 bytes, the sha256 of each file's relative path, a zero byte, its sha256 in hexadecimal and a line feed. A binary file is among them only when its only zero bytes lie past its first 8,192 bytes. It is a fabulac's source.tree, and a fabulac is fresh while its folder's tree digest still matches. Everywhere a digest is asked for (digest repo, a plan's input row, a record's inputs and outputs, made), a folder's digest is its tree digest.

Its fields, read with a dot, and as bare names in a where over the folder:

field of a folder of a member (a text walked from it)
path the path as given relative to the folder walked
name the last part the file name
files how many files the rule kept —
bytes their total size —
lines total lines of its text files the file's lines
tree the tree digest —
commit the clone's HEAD commit, read from its files (no git is run, so it says nothing of uncommitted changes), else nothing —
origin the clone's origin remote, else nothing —
suffix — the part of the name after its last ., without the dot (py, md); "" for none
depth — how many folders stand above it inside the folder walked (0 at the top)
size — bytes
sha256 — the file's
binary — true for a file that is not text

20.2 Walking and filtering

for each t in repo gives the folder's text files as text values, in path order. Binary files are not walked; they are counted. Each member's provenance is hand-made, with source: {folder: <tree>, path}.

repo where suffix is py is a folder: the same root, only the files whose fields satisfy the condition, and a tree digest of its own over the files it kept that MacroCOSM's rule reads. The same condition may stand on a loop, for each t in repo where suffix is py. A where over a folder reads the member fields of the table above, over binary files too, so repo where binary gives the binary files. folders of d gives a list of folder: the folders directly below d that the rule keeps, in path order, each with its own fields and tree digest. count of a folder counts its text files.

thym 1

program python-files
  why: the Python files of a folder, one line each with its length in lines.

  takes repo: folder
  gives listing: short

  let rows = for each t in repo where suffix is py
    keep join [t.path, (say t.lines)] with " · "
  end
  give join rows
end

20.3 A folder as an input

A program takes a folder as takes repo: folder, and the person gives its path as the input (repo=tool/). A folder needs no catalog. The plan records the input as {name, type: folder, path, sha256: <tree digest>, files, bytes}. At run time the tree is read again, and a changed digest is the usual refusal (check input): "repo changed since it was planned: tool/ has tree 3f2a…, the plan recorded 91c0…; plan it again".

A text input is a file the person keeps, and a replay needs it present. A folder is usually a working tree that moves on, so a run snapshots every folder it takes: it writes inputs/<name>.folder.cosm, a thym/1 volume of kind folder (section 17.1) that lists every file with its digest and holds the bytes of each text file as a block scalar, as the single-file package does (section 15.3). The catalog's shape:

oes: thym/1
kind: folder
id: thym/2026-11-05-1530-a1b2.repo
name: tool/
path: tool/
tree: 91c0e3…
commit: 4b1f0c2…
origin: https://github.com/someone/tool
files:
  - {path: LICENSE, sha256: …, size: 1071}
  - {path: tool/cli.py, sha256: …, size: 4312}
  - {path: docs/logo.png, sha256: …, size: 20480, binary: true}
texts:
  - path: tool/cli.py
    text: |
      …
nodes: []

A replay reads a folder input from this snapshot and never from the disk, so it needs no repository (section 12.4). A snapshot keeps no binary file's bytes, so a replay of a run whose folder output held a binary file is refused. The cost is the text of the walked tree, once per run, and one row per binary file: a folder of about sixty files and six thousand lines takes a few hundred kilobytes per snapshot. Sharing one snapshot across the runs of a job, by its digest, is reserved for a later edition.

20.4 A folder as an output

A move may give a folder (entail f as code, section 20.7). A program that gives code: folder lands it as outputs/code/, a directory holding exactly the folder's files, written into the run's stage and renamed into place with every other output in one step: all or none (section 12.1). The trace's output row is {name: code, sha256: <tree digest>, path: outputs/code/}. Where the host copies it next follows the kind of the value: a cshort lands beside its fabulac as <name>.cshort-<YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM>/ (MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.4.7). A folder has no catalog of its own, so its made lives in the trace's record and in whatever file the move writes into it (for a cshort, the footer of its CSHORT.md).

20.5 The flavour of a decompile

A fabula's flavour is said with as, the modifier every program already has: derive t as prose gives a fabulan, derive t as verse a fabulap, and derive t as code the fabulac's reading of one file. With no as, a text derives as it always has, to a fabulan: derive t and derive t as prose are the same call. A fabula from derive <folder> or survey is a fabulac. entail f as told of a fabulan or a fabulap keeps its contract; as code and a catem's depths are refused of either (check flavour): "f is a fabulap, a poem's fabula; only a fabulac entails a catem".

thym 1

program decompile-verse
  why: a poem decompiled into its fabula, line by line, with its metre and its rhymes, and a
       question for its author wherever a reading is a guess.
  must: nothing is fetched; the poem is the person's own copy.

  takes poem: text

20.6 Decompiling a folder of code

A fabulac is built in stages, each one atomic run, so that a build that stops can take up again after the last stage that landed. The stages, and the moves that make them:

stage the move asks what it gives, and what its contract holds

Every stage's contract ends with fabula-checks and provenance, and the last program keeps fabula-checks. A reading is derive because a file read as code is a text decompiled, and the fold is derive because a folder decompiled with what is known of it is a decompile too; read keeps its meaning (section 14). gather takes a list of fabulas or of items and gives the with volume with every one of them in it. 20.8 What is never run

Nothing a program reads or makes from code is executed by Thym. survey reads syntax trees; history reads the clone's log and tags through the host's engine, which answers only each commit's id, date, author's name, subject and paths, and each tag's name, commit and date; parses and interfaces-present ask the host's syntax engine, whose answer per file is recorded as an engine call (section 12.4), so a replay, and a runtime in another language, needs no parser. Parsing is not running: nothing is imported, compiled into files on disk, or run. A cshort is viewed, never run; whether it behaves as its source did is a test that runs outside Thym, in a sandbox of its own.

21. Editing

In thym 1, edits are values. A program reads a text or a short as numbered lines, makes changes, each naming the line it is about by number and digest, and then applies all its changes at once with one deterministic move, edit, under a contract that no other line moved. A model is asked only for the wording of a replacement, inside a move that asks (reword), and under that move's contract.

21.1 Lines and changes

A line is one line of a text or a short: the line's bytes without its line break, its number (from 1, in its source), and the sha256 of its source and of itself. Its fields are number; level (the heading level, 1 to 6, of a heading written with # marks, 0 otherwise); title (a heading's words without its # marks and without a leading section number such as 3. or 3.2, nothing for other lines); and blank (a bool). A line fits wherever a string is taken.

A change is one edit: {at: <line number>, was: <sha256 of that line>, of: <sha256 of the source>, do: replace | drop | insert-before | insert-after, lines: [<new lines>]}. It is plain data, and a program makes one only with the moves below.

21.2 The moves

move reads aloud gives
lines of s "the lines of s" every line of the text or short, blank ones included, numbered
replace l with v "replace l with v" a change: l's line becomes v (a string, a line, or a short of several lines)
drop l "drop l" a change: l's line goes
insert v before l, insert v after l "insert v before l" a change: v's lines go before or after l's; insert takes exactly one of before and after
edit s with changes "edit s with the changes" s with every change applied, of the same type as s
reword l to 30 words "reword l to thirty words" asks: l said again in at most N words, of the same type as l
join xs, join xs with "…" "join the rows" a short: the members one per line, or joined by the string
say n "say n" a string of a number, a date, a word or a bool

The loops that build a list of changes are loops that collect (section 8.1), and edit is one deterministic node. A text edited is a new value: it lands in the run's outputs and goes where the person names, never over its source (never-source holds on edit, as on a reading).

21.3 Only the changed

edit's contract, only-the-changed, holds when:

  1. every change names its source: its of is the digest of the value being edited ("a change names line 3 of another text");
  2. every change's line exists there with that digest ("line 12 changed since it was read");
  3. no two replace or drop changes name one line, and inserts at one place keep their written order ("line 7 is replaced twice");
  4. the output is the input with exactly those changes: every input line no change names appears byte for byte and in order, and the output holds no line that neither the input nor a change gave.

The first three check the program's changes, and the fourth checks the implementation. All four are deterministic.

21.4 reword

reword is the one editing move that asks. Its contract is length (at most N words, and at least 80 per hundred of N), one-line (a line gives one line; an answer of several lines is repaired by joining them with a space, then checked again), nothing-added (a narrow no-invention that needs no fabula: every name, number and word outside FEED in the output is in the input) and provenance. A move that rewords may keep a contract of its own as well (section 7.5), and is asked again until it holds or its attempts run out.

21.5 Two programs

tighten is the editing program people copy. It says every line of a short that runs past thirty words again in thirty or fewer, and moves no other line:

thym 1

contract shorter(new: line, old: line)
  why: a reworded line says less in fewer words, never more.

  assert words new < words old else "the reworded line is not shorter than the line it replaces"
end

program tighten
  why: every line of a short that runs past thirty words is said again in thirty or fewer, and no
       other line moves.

  takes s: short
  gives t: short

  move tightened(l: line): line
    keeps shorter against l
    give reword l to 30 words
  end

  let changes = for each l in lines of s where words l > 30
    keep replace l with tightened l
  end
  give edit s with changes
end

Read aloud: contract shorter, a reworded line says less in fewer words. Program tighten takes s, a short, and gives t. Move tightened, of a line, keeps shorter against the line, and gives the line reworded to thirty words. Let changes be: for each line of s with more than thirty words, keep a replacement of the line with the tightened line. Give s, edited with the changes. The where words l > 30 belongs to the loop, since lines declares no where (section 4.5).

A deterministic editor, which asks nothing:

thym 1

  why: every line that still says TODO leaves the draft, and nothing else changes.

  takes draft: text
  gives clean: text

  let changes = for each l in lines of draft where "TODO" in l
    keep drop l
  end

22. The dialectic in a program

The dialectic is MacroCOSM's one way of asking a person what a source means and recording what they say (MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.6): any volume may carry its questions and sessions. In a program it is three moves of thym/1, question, record and settle, in two atomic runs with a person between them. The first run lands questions with the volume. The person answers them, in a page or through an agent. The second run records the answers and settles them into the items they are about. No run waits on a person, so "nothing partial" and replay hold as they always have, and a replay of either run needs neither the person nor the model.

22.1 question

question v takes a fabula or a volume and gives a list of question items. It asks the model, with the scaffold bank. Its rules part always runs first: it gives the opening questions of the manifest's bank for the volume's flavour or format, a question for every {pending: …} field, and one for every item marked unsure (a confidence below 0.5, or pending) that holds no pending of its own (MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.6.5). When the run asks the model, the model adds questions where the volume is unsure, ambiguous, in conflict or silent on intent, and only there. It reads only the items a person can say something about: of a fabulac its aims, uses, requirements, decisions, exclusions, behaviours, interfaces, structures and claims; of any other volume every item but its files, changes, releases, test cases and dependencies as such; and in every flavour any item marked unsure or pending. Under --rules the bank part alone runs. Each question says who raised it and is open. Its contract is addressed, no-duplicate and provenance. question gives a list, and a program lands the questions with the volume through gather asked with f.

22.2 record and the answers volume

A person's answers reach a program as an answers volume, which the page or an agent writes beside the volume it answers, <the twin's name without .cosm>.answers-<YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM>.cosm, the stamp in UTC. It is a delta: the session's record, and for each question answered, only its id, its new state and its new answers. Its catalog declares the format of the volume it answers (fabula/1 for a twin read under emf/1: an old spelling is never written), carries that volume's id, and its macrocosm: when that volume has one; its nodes: hold exactly one D:session item and, for each question answered, unknown or declined, a mapping of the question's id, its new state and its new answers, each naming the session. An address asked without a question item (a format that asks from a bank without writing question items, as Comprise OES does, or a bank asked directly) is an entry {about, answers}: the address, <item id>#<field path>, and its new answers. A skipped question shows only in the session's skipped. It is read only as a delta, never checked as a volume of that format on its own. To Thym it is a volume. A sitting written and not yet recorded is read with the volume when its questions are listed: a question it answers is not put again, and a second sitting may not answer it.

record said in f is code: it appends each answer to its question's answers (never rewriting one), sets the question's state to answered, unknown or declined, and adds the session. It refuses an answers volume whose session the volume already holds, so a set of answers is recorded once, and it checks every entry before it writes any. Its contract is addressed, only-the-asked and provenance.

22.3 settle

settle f takes a fabula or a volume and gives the same, with every answered question folded into the item it is about. It asks the model only where an answer means an item must be read again (a value about a whole item or a span of the source, a value that would replace a structure, a yes-no answer about a field that is not true or false; MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.6.4). Every other answer (a field filled from a text, a choice, a yes-no, a number, a date or a reference) is written by rules, and a call that needs no reading asks nothing. An answer that the person does not know (unknown) leaves the field {pending: "<the person's own words>"}, and a refusal to say lists the field in the item's withheld and writes provenance.state: not-disclosed. Its scaffold, direct, runs the rules part only and leaves the answers that must be read again answered and unsettled, for a run with a model. Its contract is addressed; only-the-asked (the fields that change are exactly those the answered questions are about, with their provenance and the questions' settled field); fabula-checks; and provenance: every field written carries provenance: {mode: dialectic, sources: [D:session/<id>], confidence}. In a fabula the settle run joins derived.runs, and derived's single keys stay those of the run that derived it. A volume that is not a fabula, which the program of section 22.4 does not take, a host settles by the same moves' rules: it records the answers, then settles them with no reading.

22.4 The two runs, read as a pair

The first run decompiles and lands the questions:

thym 1

program decompile-prose
  why: an essay decompiled into its fabula, with a question for its author wherever the reading
       was unsure.
  decided 2026-11-05: the questions land with the fabula; the answers come back in a second run.

  takes essay: text
  gives f: fabula

  let found = derive essay as prose
  assert found covers essay else "the fabula does not cover the essay"
  let asked = question found
  give gather asked with found
end

The person answers. The second run takes the fabula and the answers volume, and lands the settled fabula:

thym 1

program settle-the-answers
  why: what a person said is written into the fabula, each answer where its question pointed, and
       nothing else in it moves.
  decided 2026-11-05: a second run, never a run that waits; "I don't know" leaves the field open
       in the person's own words.

  takes f: fabula, said: volume
  gives g: fabula
  keeps only-the-asked against f

  let answered = record said in f
  give settle answered
end

23. Models in a run

A model-backed step runs on the model chosen for its run, and the program does not change with the choice. The same program, plan nodes, contracts, repairs, attempts and output budgets hold on every route; the route changes only the model's id, the provider, the window (and so the verdict of preflight) and the cost. There is no tier of checks per route: a local model at best effort fails more often, and is refused honestly; it is never let through.

23.1 The route

The route is chosen per run, and nowhere else: local (the default: the host's local model) or cloud (a cloud model through a broker, the host's named one or one the person names by the provider's id). A run, or any job a host starts that asks a model, that names no route runs on the local route, whatever model the host's other work uses; with no local model running it runs by rules where it has a rules form, or refuses in words, and never falls back to the cloud. On the command line it is --model local, --model cloud or --model cloud:<id> (section 11.3). A plan that asks a model on a cloud route whose gate is closed is refused at plan time, check cloud, in the gate's own words: "the cloud is not open for this run: 'local models only' is on in the broker". A plan that asks nothing runs on either route and reaches no model. Nothing reaches a cloud model that the person did not choose for that run.

23.2 What the plan shows

A plan with a node that asks carries the catalog key models before anything runs:

models: {route: cloud, provider: broker-name, model: vendor/large-model, calls: 61, at_most: 183, calls_open: [], window: 200000}

calls is the number of model requests the plan makes when every check passes the first time: a plain node that asks counts one, and a node with calls in a loop counts its asking calls times its members.at_most. at_most is calls times each node's attempts. calls_open lists the nodes whose count cannot be known at plan time: a recursive move that asks, a loop over a collection of unknown size. A command line prints it under the plan's atem: 61 model calls, at most 183, on the cloud (broker-name: vendor/large-model); on the local route, 3 model calls, at most 9, on this machine (g40-04); with an open count it adds ; and more that cannot be counted before it runs, naming the steps. The plan's root summary is the same in both versions. A plan compared by the conformance suite is compared without models, since the route is the run's choice, not the program's.

23.3 What a run records

  • made gains route and provider (section 5.3), and engine stays model:<id>, the model's own id.
  • A candidate gains route and provider (section 12.2). A candidate recorded without them replays without them, so an older recorded run replays to the same bytes.
  • The trace's environment holds the id of the model, as the route names it before the first request, the route, the provider, and the window of that model (section 12.3); where a provider answers with a more exact id of its own, that id is in engine and made.
  • A fabula a program builds records which model made it, in its catalog's derived: model, route, provider, program (the program's name) and run for the last run that derived it, and one entry in derived.runs per run that wrote into it, a settle run included (section 20.6).

23.4 Budgets

Preflight (section 12.5) is unchanged in its rule, and reads the window of the route's model. --calls N sets a ceiling on a run's model requests: the runtime counts every request, re-asks included, and the request past N refuses the run, check calls: "keep read-file t, f (line 55, for t = tool/cli.py): this run reached its ceiling of 120 model calls; nothing landed". A cloud plan whose calls_open is not empty is refused at plan time without --calls (check calls): "this plan may ask the cloud model a number of times nobody can know before it runs (tree-of calls itself and asks); give a ceiling with --calls". A local run needs no ceiling: it costs time, not money.

23.5 Agent-free

Agent-free refuses every model, local or cloud, at plan time (section 19). With --rules, the scaffolds run, on no route at all.

24. Conformance

24.1 A compiler must

  1. Read the grammar of section 4.8 for the version its line names, exactly, and refuse a version line it does not know. Compile a thym 0 program against the thym 0 view of every declaration, to the items and the plan it has always had, and refuse thym 1 syntax under a thym 0 line as "this needs thym 1" (section 4.9).
  2. Resolve moves from every manifest's moves: block and every *.moves.cosm sidecar in the registry's folders, and refuse an unknown move with the nearest name.
  3. Type every expression by section 5, refuse every type error with its line, and work out provenance.
  4. Refuse the static contract failures of section 7.1 and the other compile errors of section 9, each with its check.
  5. Enumerate into thym/1 items that compile back byte for byte (section 10), and plan into an acyclic graph of nodes by section 11.1.
  6. Return refusals as values with stage, line, check and message.

24.2 A runtime must

  1. Refuse a plan at plan time when the environment cannot run it (section 9), with the count or the size.
  2. Walk the plan in a topological order; run deterministic moves by their reference semantics; send model-backed moves to a coprocessor under their contracts; check every output against its contract before it becomes a value.
  3. Land every output or none, several outputs and folder outputs included; write the trace with every record; name the run, and for a model-backed value the route, in every output's made.
  4. Snapshot every folder a run takes, and replay a recorded run to byte-identical outputs with the replay coprocessor, reading folder inputs from their snapshots.
  5. Refuse in the person's words (section 9), keep the run's ceiling of model calls, and refuse every model, local or cloud, when agent-free.
  6. Keep the six laws.

24.3 The suite

The suite is a folder, portable together with MacroCOSM's own corpus, which holds the sample Holotype that open-house runs on; the reference implementation carries both (https://github.com/0gsd/enough):

README.md                       the layout, the inputs of each program, and what passing means
programs/<name>.thym            the programs: the three of this document first
inputs/                         the texts, twins, package, morph and context the programs run on
expected/<name>/items.cosm      the enumeration; compiles back to programs/<name>.thym byte for byte
expected/<name>/plan.cosm       the plan, compared without run ids, timestamps and prompt token counts
expected/<name>/outputs/        the outputs of deterministic runs, byte for byte
replay/<name>/<run-id>/         a recorded run: plan, trace, candidates; re-runs to the recorded outputs
bad/<slug>.thym                 programs that must be refused; the first line reads -- refused: <stage> <check> line <n>

A compiler conforms when every program in programs/ compiles, enumerates to its expected/<name>/items.cosm, compiles back byte for byte and plans to its expected/<name>/plan.cosm, and when every bad/ program is refused at the stage, check and line its first line names. A runtime conforms when every deterministic program's outputs match (run with the clock pinned to 2026-11-01T12:00:00Z, so an epoch in a recorded output stays comparable) expected/<name>/outputs/ and every replay/ run re-runs to its recorded outputs. A deterministic program whose outputs carry its run's id (every item's made, a fabula's derived.run) cannot be frozen so: it is run as its latest recording under replay/<name>/, with that run's id and clock, and its outputs are compared with the recording's byte for byte. The three programs of this document (sections 4.1, 16.2 and 18) are the suite's first three, byte for byte, and the suite's test compiles every block marked thym here.

The suite reads each file by its own version line. Its thym 0 files are those of edition 0.5.4, unchanged, but for one: bad/version.thym names thym 2, since thym 1 is a grammar. Beside them it holds thym 1 programs with their expected items, plans and outputs (among them python-files and folder-outline over a small folder of code with a binary file, aims-in-brief, drop-the-todos and a deterministic contract block); a thym 0 program whose loop folds a nested call into one node; bad/ files for every refusal sections 4.9, 7.5, 8 and 21 add, each read by the version its own line names; and recorded runs of tighten, with one re-ask a kept contract forced, and of decompile-prose.
