Ascribe OES 1: a person, described without a name

Specification of the ascribe/1 MiniCOSM · written 2026-11-02 for enough 0.5.0

Ascribe is the MiniCOSM for a person's holograph: a description of one self, comprehensive enough that a name is unnecessary. It holds what a person is drawn to and turns from, believes, worries about, has lived through, wants to know, thinks, feels, can do, understands and longs for, each item anchored to the thing it is about, under a pseudonymous handle. It is written in MacroCOSM OES (docs/MACROCOSM_OES.md), whose words (item, kind, volume, catalog, module, check, lens, open question) keep their meaning here, and what leaves a holograph leaves by a Thym program its holder runs (docs/THYM.md). Comprise describes companies; Ascribe describes people, in the same grammar, with the same envelope, so that one set of moves works on both.

Status. Normative for ascribe/1. The declaration is the manifest, defaults/cosm/manifests/ascribe.manifest.cosm; the reference reader is enough/macrocosm.py, and Ascribe's own checks are enough/ascribe.py. Where this text and the code disagree, the code wins and this text is corrected. What enough builds of Ascribe in 0.5.0 is the manifest and the reader: a holograph loads, checks and draws in the cosm viewer, and nothing in enough writes one (section 13). A rule the owner of enough settled is marked the owner's ruling; the rulings are dated 2026-11-01 and collected in section 14.

Colophon

How to read this

Must and may. Must is a rule a conforming reader or writer keeps. May is a permission. Should is advice the checks do not enforce.

The family of words. The suffix says what kind of subject; the prefix says the form. Ascribe is the second row.

the whole, private, held by its subject the verified public duplicate an outsider's guess, unverified the owner's encrypted share the style sheet
a company (Comprise) holotype isotype paratype cryptotype —
a person (Ascribe) holograph isograph — (never) cryptograph holomorph (private) · isomorph (shared)

holo- the whole; iso- an equal, verified; para- beside, by another hand; crypto- hidden, by the owner. There is no outsider's graph of a person, ever (the owner's ruling): what one person knows of another is an experience in their own holograph.

Blocks. A block marked cosm is one complete file that loads through the reference reader and passes the checks the manifest selects and Ascribe's own (python -m enough.ascribe lint), with every reference resolving inside it. A block marked yaml is a fragment and may point outside itself. A block marked text is a picture or a transcript.

1. Provenance and the name

The owner's drawings for the 0.5.0 round set two subjects side by side, each with a private whole and public projections: Comprise for companies, already built, and Ascribe for people. docs/thym-architecture.md (local) drew its first shape, with the modules left to the owner; the owner wrote the list on 2026-11-01, and the manifest landed the same evening as ascribe/1. Ascribe OES 1 is its name in prose, as Comprise OES 1 is Comprise's.

To ascribe is to set something down as belonging to someone. A holograph ascribes qualities to a self without saying whose self it is.

2. The model on one page

<holograph>/
  holograph.cosm                    the self: oes: ascribe/1, id ascribe/<hid>, a handle, pseudonyms, sample
  A/ B/ C/ E/ I/ O/ Q/ S/ U/ Y/     ten containers of the self, one kind of node each
  F/                                the forms the nodes anchor to, the polyforms that combine them, declared relations
  **/.ascribe-private               a marker: what ingest and transfer leave behind (declared, not built)
  • The holograph is the volume: a folder whose catalog, holograph.cosm, is the self. It names no one. Its id is a base32 handle-id, and its handle is a pseudonym.
  • A node is one item of the self, in one of ten kinds: affinity, belief, concern, experience, interest, opinion, quale, skill, understanding, yearning. Each kind lives in its own module, whose letter is the kind's initial.
  • A form is a thing a self can be about: anything a person can love or hate, know well or want to learn ("is there an encyclopedia article for it, or should there be?"). Every node anchors to one form, or to a polyform that combines several. Forms are shared roots, grounded in FEED where the form is one word FEED has.
  • The author shapes the rest: hierarchies, branches and flat lists by of and parts; typed links from a starter list or from relations they declare; a when, a valence and a strength where they help.
  • Nothing about the person that would identify them has a place in the schema. A check warns on what reads like it, and a filter keeps private files out of ingest and transfer.
  • The holograph stays with its holder. What leaves it, as an isograph or a cryptograph, leaves by a Thym program the holder runs (section 12).

3. The design rules

  1. No name is needed. A holograph describes a self well enough that a name adds nothing. The catalog carries a pseudonymous handle and no name.
  2. No personal data, three ways (the owner's ruling). Schema: there is no kind and no field for a name, an email address, a phone number, a postal address, a birth date, a government id or an employer's legal name. Lint: the no-pii check warns on what reads like any of them, anywhere in the volume, comments included, and never refuses (section 9). Filter: a .ascribe-private marker keeps files out of ingest and transfer (section 10).
  3. Every node is about something. A node anchors to a form or a polyform that exists (form-anchored), so that two nodes about the same thing are seen to be.
  4. One grammar for both subjects. The envelope is Comprise's, plus six fields of Ascribe's own (section 6), so that Thym's moves read a holograph as they read a Holotype.
  5. The author shapes it. Hierarchy, branches, links, dates, valence and strength are optional and the author's. The checks hold only what must hold: an anchor, a declared relation, a well-formed when, a strength in range.
  6. Nothing of another person. A reference never points into another person's holograph. People in a life appear by role ("a retired harbour pilot") or by a pseudonym the catalog declares.
  7. The holder releases. Nothing in a holograph is public by default (visibility is internal when absent), and what is released is released by a program the holder runs.

4. The holograph

4.1 The volume

◂ 1

A holograph is a folder, laid out as a Holotype's atoms are, minus the part level: one person, one volume, so the module folders sit directly beside the catalog.

Place Holds
holograph.cosm the catalog, the self; it may carry a few nodes of any module in its own nodes:
<module>/ A/ B/ C/ E/ I/ O/ Q/ S/ U/ Y/ for the ten kinds, F/ for forms, polyforms and relations; a reader reads either case and a writer writes the capital
<module folder>/**/*.cosm module files, at any depth, each a mapping with a nodes: list
.ascribe-private a marker, anywhere below the catalog (section 10)

A node is written in its own module's folder or in the catalog's nodes:; a node elsewhere fails id-module, which enough/ascribe.py holds to the folders because the generic reader places module folders only under parts.

4.2 The catalog

Key Holds
oes ascribe/1; a holograph.cosm without it is refused
macrocosm reserved (MacroCOSM 5.6)
id ascribe/<hid>: the hid is twenty-six characters of lowercase base32 (a–z, 2–7), the shape of a Comprise Connect hid
handle a pseudonymous handle: lowercase words joined by hyphens (salt-and-crumb), never a name
summary one line, optional; people appear in it by role, never by name
sample true on a fixture or a demonstration, never on a real person's holograph
visibility public, internal or restricted; internal when absent
pseudonyms the stand-in names the author uses for the people in their life ("Wren", "the Captain"), so that the no-pii check reads them as declared rather than as personal names
nodes items, when the catalog carries any

A catalog never holds the person's name, an email address, a phone number, a postal address, a birth date, a government id or an employer's legal name. A catalog name key reads as a personal name to the no-pii check (a holograph names no one); an item's name is the item's own. The reader titles a holograph by its handle.

4.3 Ids and paths

An item's id is <module>:<kind>/<slug>: A:affinity/sourdough-baking, Q:quale/starter-smell, F:form/celestial-navigation, F:polyform/slow-crafts, F:relation/learned-in. Inside its own volume an id is written bare. A path respells an id through the manifest's levels, <module>/<kind>/<slug>#<field> (A/affinity/sourdough-baking#strength), and a URI qualifies it, oes:ascribe/<hid>/<path>, the catalog id without ascribe/ standing for the database. The slug grammar and the refused path forms are MacroCOSM's (MacroCOSM 11.2), with the module segment one of A B C E I O Q S U Y F.

4.4 An example

❏ 1

A holograph small enough to be one file, every node in the catalog: two forms, a declared relation, and two nodes, one linked to the other by the declared relation.

id: ascribe/aej6l5qrka6c6btarfiy2f7vnj
handle: salt-and-crumb
summary: A baker by a working harbour, slow at most things on purpose.
sample: true
visibility: internal
pseudonyms: [Wren]
nodes:
  - id: F:form/sourdough
    type: form
    name: sourdough

    feed: {word: sourdough, pos: noun, origin: feed}
    provenance: {mode: dialectic, sources: [session-1], confidence: 1}
  - id: F:form/celestial-navigation
    type: form
    name: celestial navigation
    summary: Finding a position at sea from the sun, the moon and the stars, with a sextant, a clock and tables.
    wiki: Celestial navigation
    provenance: {mode: dialectic, sources: [session-2], confidence: 1}
  - id: F:relation/learned-in
    type: relation
    name: learned in
    summary: A skill or an understanding picked up during the experience the link points to.
    provenance: {mode: manual, sources: [self], confidence: 1}
  - id: E:experience/first-night-crossing
    type: experience
    name: A first night crossing
    summary: A crossing of the bay at night with a retired harbour pilot, steering by the stars.
    form: F:form/celestial-navigation
    when: 2019
    valence: for
    strength: 5
    provenance: {mode: dialectic, sources: [session-2], confidence: 0.9}
  - id: S:skill/taking-a-sight
    type: skill
    name: Taking a sight

    provenance: {mode: dialectic, sources: [session-2], confidence: 0.8}

The sample holograph that tests/fixtures/ascribe/sample-self/ holds is the same self at full size, in folders: every module, twenty-six items, one open question (sea glass, which FEED does not have).

5. The ten containers and the forms

5.1 The containers

Each container holds one kind, named for the singular of the module, and the letter is the kind's initial (the owner's ruling).

Letter Module Kind What it holds
A Affinities affinity what you are drawn to, and what you turn from
B Beliefs belief what you hold to be true
C Concerns concern what weighs on you
E Experiences experience what has happened to you, and what you did
I Interests interest what you want to know more about
O Opinions opinion what you think, and would say so
Q Qualia quale how things feel from the inside
S Skills skill what you can do
U Understandings understanding what you have come to understand
Y Yearnings yearning what you long for

Every kind's reference fields are form, of, parts and links, and its checked fields when, valence and strength. An affinity against something is still an affinity: valence: against says which way it points.

5.2 The forms

Module F, Forms, holds the things a self is about and how they combine. Forms are shared roots, not nodes of the self: they anchor nothing and need no form of their own.

Kind What it is Its own fields
form one thing a self can be about feed: {word, pos, origin} (origin is feed or user) when the form is one word FEED has, else {pending: "not in FEED"}; wiki: an encyclopedia article's title or URL; when
polyform several forms taken together forms: the forms it combines; links: {from, rel, to} between them; feed, wiki, when
relation a relation the author declares beyond the starter list name and summary, which give its sense; links name it by its slug (section 7)

Metadata on a form is global: every node anchored to it shares it. Metadata on a node belongs to that node alone. A form with a wiki names a public subject, and its prose is not read for personal names (section 9).

A module file, as a fragment:

# A/affinities.cosm
nodes:
  - id: A:affinity/fast-bread
    type: affinity
    name: Fast bread
    summary: Bread hurried along with commercial yeast tastes of very little.
    form: F:form/sourdough
    valence: against
    strength: 3
    provenance: {mode: dialectic, sources: [session-1], confidence: 0.8}

6. The envelope

◂ 1

The envelope is Comprise's, so that Thym's moves work on both subjects; confidence and sources live in provenance, as they do in Comprise outside its product tree. Six fields are Ascribe's: form, of, parts, when, valence and strength.

Field Holds
id, type the item's id and its kind
name the item's name, never the person's
summary one line about the item
status active, dormant, planned or retired (once true of the person, no longer: a belief given up)
visibility public, internal or restricted; internal when absent
provenance mode, sources, confidence (a number from 0 to 1: how sure the person is) and state (known, the default, pending, not-disclosed, stale); sources name a dialectic session (session-3), a text the author wrote (notes.md#L3-L7), or self for what they wrote down directly
pending open questions on the item
links typed links (section 7)
span where in a text the node was read: {lines: "12-18"}, or {start, end} of up to sixty characters each
form what the node is about: one F:form/<slug> or F:polyform/<slug>; every node of the ten kinds carries one
of, parts the node this one is a child of, and the node's children in order: a hierarchy, a branch or a flat list, as the author likes
when a date (2014-06-03), a year (2014) or an era word (childhood, 2010s, university); absent, the node is not on the timeline
valence for, against or mixed
strength a whole number from 1 to 5
made {move, run, step, engine}: the Thym move that made the item, or {move: none} for a hand-made one (THYM.md 5.3)

Required: id, type, name, summary, provenance. The provenance modes are Comprise's list (dialectic, net-scan, system-scan, integration, structured-intake, manual, derived, asserted, archetype, catalogue, directory, accounting). An open question is {pending: "<the question>"}, answered by the person; the sentinels are none and self; unknown fields are allowed and kept. valence and a FEED grounding's origin are closed lists.

7. Links and relations

◂ 2

A typed link is {rel, to}, as in MacroCOSM 9.4. Its rel is one of the starter relations or a relation the holograph declares:

is-a  part-of  about  because  before  after  with  against  like  from  toward  see

before and after order nodes in time without dates. A declared relation is an F:relation/<slug> with a name and a one-line summary giving its sense; a link names it as rel: <slug>, or by its id. A relation that repeats a starter word is refused: use the starter word, or give the relation a name of its own.

The manifest keeps the starter list as links.starter and leaves the closed links.rels out on purpose: a closed list there would make the generic reader refuse every relation an author declares. Ascribe's own check, relation-declared, closes the list instead, so that a typo never becomes a relation.

References take three forms: a bare id (F:form/sourdough), naming an item of the same holograph; a typed link; and an open question. An unresolved reference is an error. A reference never names another person's holograph.

8. The dialectic

The manifest's dialectic: block holds the opening questions a guided process, or an agent, asks to help a person fill a holograph in (the owner's ruling). They are plain words, in no one's voice. What the person says is written as nodes with provenance {mode: dialectic, sources: [session-<n>], confidence: <how sure the person is>}. In 0.5.0 the questions are all that is built; the process that asks them is a later round.

  • A Affinities. What do you go back to, again and again? What can you not stand, even when everyone around you loves it? Which place or kind of work feels most like yours? What did you love as a child that you still love?
  • B Beliefs. What do you believe that most people around you do not? What did you once believe and no longer do? What would you hold to even if it cost you something? What do you take on trust?
  • C Concerns. What keeps you awake at night? What do you worry about for the people close to you? Which problem do you wish more people noticed? If you could fix one thing, what would it be?
  • E Experiences. What happened that changed the direction of your life? Which day would you live again? What have you done that you are proud of? Which stretch of years feels like a chapter of its own?
  • I Interests. What could you read about for hours? What have you looked up lately just because you wanted to know? What would you study if you had a year to spare? What do you notice that others walk past?
  • O Opinions. What do you think most people get wrong? What is overrated, and what is underrated? Which argument would you gladly start at dinner? What have you changed your mind about lately?
  • Q Qualia. Which sensation do you remember most exactly? What does your favourite weather feel like? Which sound, smell or taste takes you straight back somewhere? What does it feel like when your work is going well?
  • S Skills. What can you do without thinking about it? What do people ask you to help with? What did you learn the hard way? What are you getting better at right now?
  • U Understandings. What do you understand now that you did not ten years ago? What could you explain to a curious stranger? Which idea changed how you see things once it clicked? What do you know from experience that no book taught you?
  • Y Yearnings. What do you wish you had time for? What would you try if you knew you could not fail? Where would you go, given the chance? What do you miss?
  • F Forms. Which words would you put on a list of things you care about? What would you look up first in an encyclopedia? Which subject deserves an article that nobody has written yet? Which things belong together for you, though others keep them apart?

9. Checks

◂ 2

9.1 The generic checks

The manifest selects load and the envelope checks duplicate-id, id-grammar, id-module, type, type-prefix, identity-min, provenance, status, visibility, ref-grammar, ref-unresolved, closed-list, pending-shape and catalog-key (MacroCOSM 12.2). enough/ascribe.py holds two of them to what the generic reader cannot read from the manifest: id-module to the module folders (section 4.1), and catalog-key to the hid, the handle and the shape of pseudonyms.

9.2 Ascribe's own

Run by enough/ascribe.py (lint_holograph) after the generic ones, in one report. A generic reader does not run them.

Check Severity Catches
form-anchored error a node of the ten kinds with no form, a form that names no item, or one that names something other than a form or a polyform; a form that is an open question is a note
relation-declared error a link whose rel is neither a starter word nor a declared relation; a declared relation that repeats a starter word
when-shape error a when that is not a date, a year or an era word
strength-range error a strength that is not a whole number from 1 to 5
no-pii warning, never an error what reads like personal data (section 9.3)
private-marker note what a .ascribe-private marker would keep out of ingest and transfer (section 10)

The manifest's checks.severity may move a severity; no-pii may be lowered to a note and never raised above a warning, while the schema and the filter still hold (the owner's ruling).

9.3 What reads like personal data

◂ 1

no-pii reads every string of the catalog and of every item, every key, and every comment in every file of the volume. It warns on:

What How it is recognised
an email address a local part, an @ and a dotted domain
a birth date born, birth, birthday, date of birth or dob within a few words of a day of a month (a year alone is a when, not a birth date); and a node dated to the day whose name, summary or form speaks of a birth
an id-like run of digits the three-two-four shape of a social-security number, a card's groups of four, or eight digits or more standing alone
a phone number international with a plus, a bracketed area code, three-three-four groups, or a national number after a trunk zero; seven to fifteen digits in all
a street address a number and a capitalised street name (17 Quillfeather Lane), the French, Spanish and Italian order, the German -straße, or a post-office box
a field for personal data a key named for one: email, phone, address, birthday, ssn, passport, employer, first-name, surname and their kin; a catalog name
a personal name in a prose field (a summary, and an experience's name), two adjacent capitalised words neither of which is all capitals, a place word, a month or day, a sentence's opening word, or a declared pseudonym

A stretch one pattern has claimed is not claimed again, so one email address is one warning. A form with a wiki names a public subject, and its prose is not read for names. Every warning ends with the same advice: people appear by role, or by a pseudonym you declare.

10. The private filter

◂ 4

The manifest declares what ingest and transfer leave out of a holograph, the way Canon leaves a Holotype's private side behind. The rule is declared and checked; the code that applies it is a later round.

  • Marker: .ascribe-private.
  • Drops: every file and folder beside a marker, with everything below them; and every node with visibility: restricted.
  • Keeps: everything else, unchanged. A reference to a dropped node keeps its id and gains no name.

private-marker reports, as a note, what each marker would drop: the files, and how many nodes they hold, or "the whole holograph" when the catalog itself is beside a marker. It drops nothing.

11. The command line

python -m enough.ascribe lint <folder | holograph.cosm> [--json]

It loads the holograph through the reference reader, runs the generic checks and Ascribe's own, prints each problem as <severity> <check> <item> (<file>): <message>, and ends with one line: ascribe/1: <n> items, <e> errors, <w> warnings, <k> notes, <q> open questions. It exits 0 when there is no error (warnings and notes do not fail it) and 1 otherwise. It reads only the folder it is given and writes nothing. No server route, viewer or tool calls it in 0.5.0.

On the fixtures in tests/fixtures/ascribe/:

sample-self                   ascribe/1: 26 items, 0 errors, 0 warnings, 0 notes, 1 open questions      exit 0
bad/pii                       ascribe/1: 8 items, 0 errors, 7 warnings, 0 notes, 0 open questions       exit 0
bad/unanchored                ascribe/1: 3 items, 1 errors, 0 warnings, 0 notes, 0 open questions       exit 1
bad/undeclared-relation       ascribe/1: 3 items, 1 errors, 0 warnings, 0 notes, 0 open questions       exit 1

bad/pii carries one of each pattern, invented, and every one is a warning: a phone number, a birth date, a personal name, an email address, an id-like run of digits, an employer field and a street address.

12. Projections, by reference

◂ 2

  • An isograph is the holograph projected by a Thym program its holder runs: the dial, for a person (THYM.md 18 writes the company's). It is discoverable on an index like a profile. Verified means only what an Ascribe-Connect could check, that one real person holds this key and this graph has one holder; everything else is stated.
  • A cryptograph is the holograph's sealed share for a chosen party: an encrypted Thym package (THYM.md 15.3), with the key shown once and stored nowhere, and, on an index, only consented match tokens, hashes of the characteristics the holder chose to make matchable.
  • A holomorph is the morph measured from what the person writes with Ascribe active, and an isomorph is one the holder chose to share, released from the holograph by a program as an isograph is (THYM.md 13.4).
  • There is no paratype of a person: no outsider's graph, ever.

A later round may bridge Comprise and Ascribe: an employer's seek for a role shaped like an isograph, answered by a candidate's isograph, both speaking Thym. Nothing in 0.5.0 builds it; the common envelope makes it possible.

13. What is not built

◂ 1

  • The application module: the process that asks the dialectic's questions and writes nodes, and any editor for a holograph. In 0.5.0 a holograph is written by hand; enough reads it.
  • Ascribe-Connect: the service that would vouch that one real person holds a holograph.
  • Isographs on an index, and the projection programs that make them.
  • Consented match tokens, and an index that serves them.
  • The private filter's code: the rule is declared (section 10) and nothing applies it.
  • The bridge to Comprise (section 12).

What is built: the manifest, the reader's support (a holograph loads, checks and draws in the cosm viewer, titled by its handle, with the lenses self, graph and the generic map; timeline is a table the reader keeps and does not draw), enough/ascribe.py and its command, and the fixtures with their tests (tests/test_ascribe.py).

14. Decisions in this version

◂ 1

  1. Ten containers, one kind each (the owner's ruling): A B C E I O Q S U Y, the kind the singular of the module's name and the letter its initial.
  2. Module F for forms, polyforms and declared relations (the owner's ruling). Every node anchors to a form.
  3. The catalog is the self (the owner's ruling): holograph.cosm, with the module folders beside it; a handle, pseudonyms and a sample flag, and no name.
  4. when, with before and after (the owner's ruling): a date, a year or an era word, optional; order without dates by the two starter relations.
  5. valence and strength (the owner's ruling): for, against or mixed; a whole number from 1 to 5.
  6. The dialectic's questions live in the manifest (the owner's ruling); the process that asks them is later.
  7. No personal data by schema, lint and filter; the lint warns and never refuses (the owner's ruling), while the schema and the filter still hold.
  8. Ascribe OES 1 is ascribe/1 (the owner's ruling).
  9. Comprise's envelope, so that one set of Thym moves works on both subjects.
  10. links.starter, not a closed links.rels, so that declared relations pass the generic reader and relation-declared closes the list.
  11. No outsider's graph of a person, ever (the owner's ruling, docs/thym-architecture.md): crypto- means encrypted, and there is no paratype of a person.
