Ascribe

Edition 0.6.0 · revised 2026-11-06 · cosmoes.org home: https://ascribe.cosmoes.org/

This edition describes the ascribe/1 MiniCOSM: the declaration every holograph carries.

A person, described without a name: Ascribe is the MiniCOSM for a person's holograph, a description of one self comprehensive enough that a name is unnecessary. It holds what a person is drawn to and turns from, believes, worries about, has lived through, wants to know, thinks, feels, can do, understands and longs for, each item anchored to the thing it is about, under a pseudonymous handle.

Ascribe is one of the two MiniCOSMs, the domain OESes with a specification of their own; Comprise OES (https://comprise.cosmoes.org/), for companies, is the other. Both are written in MacroCOSM OES (https://macro.cosmoes.org/), an OES: the shared grammar and its core, which holds the formats built into every reader, the densities and the dialectic; its words (item, kind, volume, catalog, module, check, lens, open question, question, session) keep their meaning here. Thym (https://thym.cosmoes.org/) is the transcognition language that connects OESes: an isograph leaves a holograph by a Thym program its holder runs. Comprise describes companies and Ascribe describes people, in the same grammar and with the same envelope, so that one set of moves works on both.

Status. Normative for ascribe/1. The declaration is the manifest, ascribe.manifest.cosm. The reference implementation is enough (https://github.com/0gsd/enough): its MacroCOSM reader loads a holograph and runs the checks the manifest selects, its Ascribe module runs Ascribe's own, and its cosm viewer draws the holograph. Where an implementation and this text disagree, one of them is in error; the Changes appendix (section 17) records which was corrected.

Colophon

How to read this

Must and may. Must is a rule a conforming reader or writer keeps. May is a permission. Should is advice the checks do not enforce.

The family of words. The suffix says what kind of subject; the prefix says the form. Ascribe is the second row.

the whole, private, held by its subject the verified public duplicate an outsider's guess, unverified the owner's encrypted share the style sheet
a company (Comprise) holotype isotype paratype cryptotype —
a person (Ascribe) holograph isograph — (never) cryptograph holomorph (private) · isomorph (shared)

holo- the whole; iso- an equal, verified; para- beside, by another hand; crypto- hidden, by the owner. There is no outsider's graph of a person, ever: what one person knows of another is an experience in their own holograph.

The holder of a holograph is its subject, who keeps it: the one person who answers its questions, runs its programs and releases what leaves it.

Blocks. A block marked cosm is one complete file that loads through a conforming reader and passes the checks the manifest selects and Ascribe's own (section 10), with every reference resolving inside it. A block marked json is one complete document of the format it names. A block marked yaml is a fragment and may point outside itself. A block marked text is a picture or a transcript.

1. Provenance and the name

Ascribe was drawn beside Comprise: two subjects set side by side, each with a private whole and public projections, Comprise for companies and Ascribe for people. The architecture drawn for Thym gave Ascribe its first shape, with the modules left to the owner, who wrote the list of ten; the manifest followed as ascribe/1 (section 17). Ascribe is its name in prose, as Comprise OES is Comprise's; ascribe/1 is its declaration, as comprise/1 is Comprise's.

To ascribe is to set something down as belonging to someone. A holograph ascribes qualities to a self without saying whose self it is.

Ascribe is stewarded by Verroes (Graham Smith). Its canonical edition is published at https://ascribe.cosmoes.org/ (see the colophon); each revision is an edition, and the Changes appendix (section 17) records what each edition changed.

2. The model on one page

<holograph>/
  holograph.cosm                    the self: oes: ascribe/1, id ascribe/<hid>, a handle, pseudonyms, sample,
                                    the releases it has made, its morph volumes
  A/ B/ C/ E/ I/ O/ Q/ S/ U/ Y/     ten containers of the self, one kind of node each
  F/                                the forms the nodes anchor to, the polyforms that combine them, declared relations
  D/                                the dialectic's questions and sessions: the holder's, and never released
  **/.ascribe-private               a marker: what ingest, packages, transfer and projections leave behind (section 11)
<holograph>.morph.cosm              beside the folder: the holder's morphs, a thym/1 volume (section 14.5)
  • The holograph is the volume: a folder whose catalog, holograph.cosm, is the self. It names no one. Its id is a base32 handle-id, and its handle is a pseudonym.
  • A node is one item of the self, in one of ten kinds: affinity, belief, concern, experience, interest, opinion, quale, skill, understanding, yearning. Each kind lives in its own module, whose letter is the kind's initial.
  • A form is a thing a self can be about: anything a person can love or hate, know well or want to learn ("is there an encyclopedia article for it, or should there be?"). Every node anchors to one form, or to a polyform that combines several. Forms are shared roots, grounded in FEED where the form is one word FEED has.
  • The author shapes the rest: hierarchies, branches and flat lists by of and parts; typed links from a starter list or from relations they declare; a when, a valence and a strength where they help.
  • A holograph is filled in by the dialectic, the core's way of asking a person and recording what they say (section 8), and by reading the holder's own texts, each node so read a candidate until the holder accepts it (section 9).
  • Nothing about the person that would identify them has a place in the schema. A check warns on what reads like it, and a filter keeps private files and restricted nodes out of everything that leaves (section 11).
  • The holograph stays with its holder. What leaves it, as an isograph, a cryptograph, match tokens or an isomorph, leaves by the holder's act: an isograph by a Thym program the holder runs, the others made by the implementation (section 14); each release is recorded in the holograph (section 13).

3. The design rules

  1. No name is needed. A holograph describes a self well enough that a name adds nothing. The catalog carries a pseudonymous handle and no name.
  2. No personal data, three ways. Schema: there is no kind and no field for a name, an email address, a phone number, a postal address, a birth date, a government id or an employer's legal name. Lint: the no-pii check warns on what reads like any of them, anywhere in the volume, comments included, and never refuses inside the holograph (section 10); at a projection it is an error, and a release is refused while it stands (section 14). Filter: a .ascribe-private marker and visibility: restricted keep files and nodes out of everything that leaves (section 11).
  3. Every node is about something. A node anchors to a form or a polyform that exists (form-anchored), so that two nodes about the same thing are seen to be.
  4. One grammar for both subjects. The envelope is Comprise's, plus six fields of Ascribe's own (section 6), so that Thym's moves read a holograph as they read a Holotype.
  5. The author shapes it. Hierarchy, branches, links, dates, valence and strength are optional and the author's. The checks hold only what must hold: an anchor, a declared relation, a well-formed when, a strength in range.
  6. Nothing of another person. A reference never points into another person's holograph. People in a life appear by role ("a retired harbour pilot") or by a pseudonym the catalog declares.
  7. The holder releases. Nothing in a holograph is public by default (visibility is internal when absent), what is released is released by a program the holder runs, and every release is recorded in the holograph.
  8. Nothing without the holder's word. An answer, a candidate read from a text, a release: each becomes part of the holograph, or leaves it, only by the holder's own act.

4. The holograph

4.1 The volume

A holograph is a folder, laid out as a Holotype's atoms are, minus the part level: one person, one volume, so the module folders sit directly beside the catalog.

Place Holds
holograph.cosm the catalog, the self; it may carry a few nodes of any module in its own nodes:
<module>/ A/ B/ C/ E/ I/ O/ Q/ S/ U/ Y/ for the ten kinds, F/ for forms, polyforms and relations, D/ for the dialectic's questions and sessions; a reader reads either case and a writer writes the capital
<module folder>/**/*.cosm module files, at any depth, each a mapping with a nodes: list
.ascribe-private a marker, anywhere below the catalog (section 11)

A node is written in its own module's folder or in the catalog's nodes:; a node elsewhere fails id-module, which an Ascribe reader holds to the module folders because the generic reader places module folders only under parts (section 10.1). Every .cosm file below the catalog's folder is read as a module file, so a file of another format, a morph volume included, is kept beside the folder and never inside it.

4.2 The catalog

Key Holds
oes ascribe/1; a holograph.cosm without it is refused
macrocosm reserved (MacroCOSM OES, its section 5.6)
id ascribe/<hid>: the hid is twenty-six characters of lowercase base32 (a–z, 2–7), the shape of a Comprise Connect hid
handle a pseudonymous handle: lowercase words joined by hyphens (salt-and-crumb), never a name
summary one line, optional; people appear in it by role, never by name
sample true on a fixture or a demonstration, never on a real person's holograph
visibility public, internal or restricted; internal when absent
pseudonyms the stand-in names the author uses for the people in their life ("Wren", "the Captain"), so that the no-pii check reads them as declared rather than as personal names
releases optional: the record of every release the holder has made, oldest first (section 13); absent, none has been made
morphs optional: the file names of the holder's morph volumes, each kept beside the holograph's folder (section 14.5)
nodes items, when the catalog carries any

A catalog never holds the person's name, an email address, a phone number, a postal address, a birth date, a government id or an employer's legal name. A catalog name key reads as a personal name to the no-pii check (a holograph names no one); an item's name is the item's own. The reader titles a holograph by its handle. A holograph written before releases and morphs were catalog keys reads unchanged, and a reader that does not know the two keeps them as unknown catalog keys.

4.3 Ids and paths

An item's id is <module>:<kind>/<slug>: A:affinity/sourdough-baking, Q:quale/starter-smell, F:form/celestial-navigation, F:polyform/slow-crafts, F:relation/learned-in, D:question/ascribe-a1, D:session/session-3. Inside its own volume an id is written bare. A path respells an id through the manifest's levels, <module>/<kind>/<slug>#<field> (A/affinity/sourdough-baking#strength), and a URI qualifies it, oes:ascribe/<hid>/<path>, the catalog id without ascribe/ standing for the database. The slug grammar and the refused path forms are MacroCOSM's (MacroCOSM OES, its section 11.2), with the module segment one of A B C E I O Q S U Y F D.

4.4 An example

A holograph small enough to be one file, every node in the catalog: two forms, a declared relation, and two nodes, one linked to the other by the declared relation.

5. The ten containers and the forms

5.1 The containers

Each container holds one kind, named for the singular of the module, and the letter is the kind's initial.

Letter Module Kind What it holds
A Affinities affinity what you are drawn to, and what you turn from
B Beliefs belief what you hold to be true
C Concerns concern what weighs on you
E Experiences experience what has happened to you, and what you did
I Interests interest what you want to know more about
O Opinions opinion what you think, and would say so
Q Qualia quale how things feel from the inside
S Skills skill what you can do
U Understandings understanding what you have come to understand
Y Yearnings yearning what you long for

Every kind's reference fields are form, of, parts and links, and its checked fields when, valence and strength. An affinity against something is still an affinity: valence: against says which way it points.

Two more modules sit beside the containers and are not the self: F, the forms (section 5.2), and D, the dialectic's questions and sessions, which the core declares and every format inherits (section 8). Ascribe's own check form-anchored holds only the ten kinds, never an item of D.

5.2 The forms

Module F, Forms, holds the things a self is about and how they combine. Forms are shared roots, not nodes of the self: they anchor nothing and need no form of their own.

Kind What it is Its own fields
form one thing a self can be about feed: {word, pos, origin} (origin is feed or user) when the form is one word FEED has, else {pending: "not in FEED"}; wiki: an encyclopedia article's title or URL; when
polyform several forms taken together forms: the forms it combines; links: {from, rel, to} between them; feed, wiki, when
relation a relation the author declares beyond the starter list name and summary, which give its sense; links name it by its slug (section 7)

Metadata on a form is global: every node anchored to it shares it. Metadata on a node belongs to that node alone. A form with a wiki names a public subject, and its prose is not read for personal names (section 10).

A module file, as a fragment:

# A/affinities.cosm
nodes:
  - id: A:affinity/fast-bread
    type: affinity
    name: Fast bread
    summary: Bread hurried along with commercial yeast tastes of very little.
    form: F:form/sourdough
    valence: against
    strength: 3
    provenance: {mode: dialectic, sources: [session-1], confidence: 0.8}

6. The envelope

The envelope is Comprise's, so that Thym's moves work on both subjects; confidence and sources live in provenance, as they do in Comprise outside its product tree. Six fields are Ascribe's: form, of, parts, when, valence and strength.

Field Holds
id, type the item's id and its kind
name the item's name, never the person's
summary one line about the item
status active, dormant, planned or retired (once true of the person, no longer: a belief given up)
visibility public, internal or restricted; internal when absent
provenance mode, sources, confidence (a number from 0 to 1: how sure the person is) and state (known, the default, pending, not-disclosed, stale); sources name a dialectic session (D:session/session-3, or the earlier spelling session-3), a text the author wrote (notes.md#L3-L7, or notes.md#sha256:<digest> for a candidate read from it, section 9), or self for what they wrote down directly
pending open questions on the item
links typed links (section 7)
span where in a text the node was read: {lines: "12-18"}, or {start, end} of up to sixty characters each
form what the node is about: one F:form/<slug> or F:polyform/<slug>; every node of the ten kinds carries one
of, parts the node this one is a child of, and the node's children in order: a hierarchy, a branch or a flat list, as the author likes
when a date (2014-06-03), a year (2014) or an era word (childhood, 2010s, university); absent, the node is not on the timeline
valence for, against or mixed
strength a whole number from 1 to 5
made {move, run, step, engine}: the Thym move that made the item, or {move: none} for a hand-made one (Thym, its section 5.3)

Required: id, type, name, summary, provenance. The provenance modes are Comprise's list (dialectic, net-scan, system-scan, integration, structured-intake, manual, derived, asserted, archetype, catalogue, directory, accounting). An open question is {pending: "<the question>"}, answered by the person; the sentinels are none and self; unknown fields are allowed and kept. valence and a FEED grounding's origin are closed lists. Two fields the dialectic writes are kept like any unknown field: withheld, the fields the holder declined to answer (section 8.5), and, inside provenance, answered, the session and confidence of each field an answer filled.

7. Links and relations

A typed link is {rel, to}, as in MacroCOSM OES, its section 9.4. Its rel is one of the starter relations or a relation the holograph declares:

is-a  part-of  about  because  before  after  with  against  like  from  toward  see

before and after order nodes in time without dates. A declared relation is an F:relation/<slug> with a name and a one-line summary giving its sense; a link names it as rel: <slug>, or by its id. A relation that repeats a starter word is refused: use the starter word, or give the relation a name of its own.

The manifest keeps the starter list as links.starter and leaves the closed links.rels out on purpose: a closed list there would make the generic reader refuse every relation an author declares. Ascribe's own check, relation-declared, closes the list instead, so that a typo never becomes a relation.

References take three forms: a bare id (F:form/sourdough), naming an item of the same holograph; a typed link; and an open question. An unresolved reference is an error. A reference never names another person's holograph.

8. The dialectic, applied

The dialectic is the core's one way of asking a person what something means and recording what they say: questions and sessions in module D, two atomic runs with a person between them, and the Thym moves question, record and settle (MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.6; Thym, its section 22). This section says how a holograph uses it. Every rule of the core holds; Ascribe adds what a holograph needs.

8.1 The bank

A manifest's dialectic: key holds banks of opening questions. Ascribe's manifest holds one, read as the bank named ascribe: four questions for each module of the self and four for the forms, forty-four in all, in plain words and in no one's voice. Each question is an entry about its module, asked of the holograph once (about: <letter>), with kind: text, to: holder and why: missing; entry ids are A1, A2 and on, by position, so a question raised from the first says raised.from: ascribe/A1.

  • A Affinities. What do you go back to, again and again? What can you not stand, even when everyone around you loves it? Which place or kind of work feels most like yours? What did you love as a child that you still love?
  • B Beliefs. What do you believe that most people around you do not? What did you once believe and no longer do? What would you hold to even if it cost you something? What do you take on trust?
  • C Concerns. What keeps you awake at night? What do you worry about for the people close to you? Which problem do you wish more people noticed? If you could fix one thing, what would it be?
  • E Experiences. What happened that changed the direction of your life? Which day would you live again? What have you done that you are proud of? Which stretch of years feels like a chapter of its own?
  • I Interests. What could you read about for hours? What have you looked up lately just because you wanted to know? What would you study if you had a year to spare? What do you notice that others walk past?
  • O Opinions. What do you think most people get wrong? What is overrated, and what is underrated? Which argument would you gladly start at dinner? What have you changed your mind about lately?
  • Q Qualia. Which sensation do you remember most exactly? What does your favourite weather feel like? Which sound, smell or taste takes you straight back somewhere? What does it feel like when your work is going well?
  • S Skills. What can you do without thinking about it? What do people ask you to help with? What did you learn the hard way? What are you getting better at right now?
  • U Understandings. What do you understand now that you did not ten years ago? What could you explain to a curious stranger? Which idea changed how you see things once it clicked? What do you know from experience that no book taught you?
  • Y Yearnings. What do you wish you had time for? What would you try if you knew you could not fail? Where would you go, given the chance? What do you miss?
  • F Forms. Which words would you put on a list of things you care about? What would you look up first in an encyclopedia? Which subject deserves an article that nobody has written yet? Which things belong together for you, though others keep them apart?

Besides the bank, questions are raised by rule, as the core raises them: one for each {pending: …} in the holograph, and one for each node whose provenance.state is pending or whose confidence is below 0.5 (why: unsure).

8.2 Choosing and putting a question

Every question in a holograph is to: holder: no one else answers for a self. A question is put in one of three ways.

Way How The session records
a person working alone the holder picks a module, or takes the next open question, and answers in their own time through: person
an agent a readvisor puts one or two questions at a time in conversation, and records the answers the holder gives through: agent, with the agent's model
a Thym program the holder runs a program whose question step lands questions; the holder answers; a second run's settle step folds the answers in through: person or agent, as the answers were given

Which bank questions come first is the asker's choice: a writer should offer the modules with the fewest nodes first and never put a question whose entry the holder has declined (section 8.5) unless the holder asks for it. One open question stands for each bank entry and each item or field at a time (the core's no-duplicate). A session's with names the holder by the role word holder and describes them by nothing else.

8.3 What an answer writes

An answer to a bank question writes a node in the container the question belongs to, or, for a question of the forms, a form. settle writes it, from the answer, after record has kept the holder's words verbatim on the question:

  • name: a few words of the holder's own, naming the node; summary: the answer, one line, people in it by role or by a declared pseudonym;
  • form: the form the answer is about, found as section 8.4 says;
  • provenance: {mode: dialectic, sources: [D:session/<id>], confidence: <how sure the person is>}, the confidence the holder gave with the answer, and 1 when they gave none and stated it plainly.

An answer to a question about a field (<node>#when, <node>#strength) fills that field; the node keeps its mode, provenance.sources gains the session, and provenance.answered gains the field with its session and confidence. A write that would add a check error is not made, and a why: counter question is raised instead.

The earlier spelling of a session source, session-<n> (sources: [session-1]), stays valid: it names D:session/session-<n> whether or not the holograph holds that record. A writer writes D:session/<id> and a D:session item for every new session, and a new session's number skips every number a source already cites.

A question, its session and an answer, as a fragment:

# D/session-3.cosm
nodes:
  - id: D:session/session-3
    type: session
    name: An evening session
    summary: Three questions on what the holder goes back to.
    at: 2026-11-05T19:30:00Z
    with: {to: holder, who: the holder}
    through: agent
    model: g40-04
    asked: [D:question/ascribe-a1]
    answered: [D:question/ascribe-a1]
  - id: D:question/ascribe-a1
    type: question
    name: What do you go back to?
    summary: The first opening question of the affinities.
    about: A
    asks: What do you go back to, again and again?
    why: missing
    kind: text
    to: holder
    raised: {by: bank, from: ascribe/A1, at: 2026-11-05T19:30:00Z, run: 2026-11-05-1930-7e1a}
    state: answered
    answers:
      - {at: 2026-11-05T19:34:00Z, by: holder, says: "Sourdough. I keep a starter going and bake twice a week.", value: "Baking sourdough from a starter kept going for years.", confidence: 1, session: D:session/session-3}
    settled: {at: 2026-11-05T19:40:00Z, run: 2026-11-05-1940-c4d2, wrote: [A:affinity/sourdough-baking]}

8.4 Form discovery and follow-up questions

A node needs a form. settle looks for one in this order: a form or polyform of the holograph the answer is plainly about; a new form for one word FEED has, feed: {word, pos, origin: feed}; and, for anything else, a new form whose feed is {pending: "not in FEED"}, named in the holder's words. A word not in FEED is an open question, never an invented form: no writer grounds it in a sense FEED does not give, and the holder may answer it later with a definition or an encyclopedia article (wiki). The form is found from the holder's own words for what the answer is about, or from a thing the answer names whole, never from a word guessed out of a sentence. Where the answer names no thing at all, the node is not written, and a follow-up asks what it is about: a question about the bank question (about: D:question/<its id>, raised from settle), whose answer settles both.

After a node is written, a writer may raise follow-up questions about its optional fields, each to: holder, why: missing, about <node>#<field>:

Field Asks kind
form What is this about, in a word or two? text
when When was this, roughly: a date, a year, or a time of life? date
valence Does this draw you, put you off, or both? choice: for, against, mixed
strength How strongly, from 1 to 5? number, range 1 to 5

A follow-up is never put twice about one field, and a field the holder left empty stays empty: the author shapes the holograph (section 3).

8.5 Not knowing, and not saying

The holder says The question The holograph
"I don't know" unknown, the answer value: null and their words in says on a field, the field stays {pending: "<their words>"}; on a bank question, nothing is written
they would rather not say declined, the answer declined: true on a field, the node gains withheld: [<field>] and provenance.state: not-disclosed; on a bank question, nothing is written, and the bank does not put that entry again unless the holder asks
nothing; they skip it stays open; the session lists it as skipped nothing

A refusal is never asked again in other words, and nothing a holder declined is inferred from anything else.

8.6 The dialectic stays with the holder

Module D holds the holder's own words. A holograph's D items are the holder's and never leave it: every projection, package and ingest leaves module D out, and only a transfer of the whole holograph into the holder's own keeping (a backup, a second machine) carries it (section 11).

9. Reading a person's own texts

A holder may have their own writing read for what it says of them: letters, a journal, essays, notes. The text is decompiled to a fabulan, the fabula of narrative and nonfiction prose (MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.4), and from the fabulan a reading proposes candidate nodes of the ten kinds: what the text shows its author drawn to, believing, worried by, living through, wanting to know, thinking, feeling, able to do, understanding and longing for.

Only the holder's own texts. A reading is begun only by the holder, over texts the holder names as their own. A text about the holder written by anyone else is never read into a holograph: that would be an outsider's graph of a person.

A candidate is an ordinary node of its kind, proposed by a reading and not yet the holder's, written with:

Field Holds
provenance {mode: derived, sources: ["<text>#sha256:<the text's digest>"], confidence: <the reading's>, state: pending}
span where in the text it was read: {lines: "12-18"}, or {start, end} of up to sixty characters each
form a form of the holograph, a new form found as section 8.4 says, or {pending: "<what it seems to be about>"}
made the move, run and engine that proposed it (Thym, its section 5.3)

Its name and summary say what the text shows in the reading's words, and name people only by role or by a declared pseudonym; the text's own names never enter the holograph. A candidate is proposed only from what the text says (the core's no-invention), and each carries the span that shows it.

The holder decides. Each candidate gets a question about it, about: <the candidate's id>, why: unsure, to: holder, kind: choice, options: [yes, no, partly], asking whether it is so:

Answer settle writes
yes the candidate becomes a node of the self: provenance.state is removed, sources gain D:session/<id>, confidence is the holder's
partly as yes, then a follow-up text question about the candidate's summary, so the holder says it their way
no the candidate is removed; its question is settled with wrote: [], keeps the answer, and is not raised again
"I don't know", a refusal, or nothing the candidate stays a candidate

A candidate is not part of the self until the holder says so. Every lens draws it as pending, no-pii reads it as it reads any item, and no projection, package or match token ever carries one (section 14). Nothing is written into a holograph by a reading except its candidates and their questions, and nothing becomes the holder's except by their answer.

The step that proposes candidates asks the model; it is the move propose, declared in Ascribe's manifest: propose f with h takes the text's fabulan and, with it, the holograph, and gives the candidates and their questions, which the program lands with gather (Thym, its section 6.3). Its contract is no-invention and provenance, the move itself holds every candidate to Ascribe's own checks (section 10), and its run lands every candidate and question or none.

10. Checks

10.1 The generic checks

The manifest selects load and the envelope checks duplicate-id, id-grammar, id-module, type, type-prefix, identity-min, provenance, status, visibility, ref-grammar, ref-unresolved, closed-list, pending-shape and catalog-key (MacroCOSM OES, its section 12.2). An Ascribe reader holds two of them to what the generic reader cannot read from the manifest: id-module to the module folders (section 4.1), and catalog-key to the hid, the handle and the shape of pseudonyms. A holograph that carries module D also passes the dialectic's checks (MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.6).

10.2 Ascribe's own

An Ascribe reader runs them after the generic ones, in one report. A generic reader does not run them.

Check Severity Catches
form-anchored error a node of the ten kinds with no form, a form that names no item, or one that names something other than a form or a polyform; a form that is an open question is a note
relation-declared error a link whose rel is neither a starter word nor a declared relation; a declared relation that repeats a starter word
when-shape error a when that is not a date, a year or an era word
strength-range error a strength that is not a whole number from 1 to 5
release-shape error a releases entry that lacks a field section 13 requires, holds a field section 13 does not name, or whose as, epoch or digests are not in their shape; a morphs entry that is not a bare file name ending .morph.cosm
no-pii warning, never an error in the holograph what reads like personal data (section 10.3)
private-marker note what a .ascribe-private marker would keep out of everything that leaves (section 11)

The manifest's checks.severity may move a severity; no-pii may be lowered to a note and never raised above a warning in the holograph, while the schema and the filter still hold. At a projection no-pii is an error whatever the manifest says (section 14.1).

10.3 What reads like personal data

no-pii reads every string of the catalog and of every item, every key, and every comment in every file of the volume. It warns on:

What How it is recognised
an email address a local part, an @ and a dotted domain
a birth date born, birth, birthday, date of birth or dob within a few words of a day of a month (a year alone is a when, not a birth date); and a node dated to the day whose name, summary or form speaks of a birth
an id-like run of digits the three-two-four shape of a social-security number, a card's groups of four, or eight digits or more standing alone
a phone number international with a plus, a bracketed area code, three-three-four groups, or a national number after a trunk zero; seven to fifteen digits in all
a street address a number and a capitalised street name (17 Quillfeather Lane), the French, Spanish and Italian order, the German -straße, or a post-office box
a field for personal data a key named for one: email, phone, address, birthday, ssn, passport, employer, first-name, surname and their kin; a catalog name
a personal name in a prose field (a summary, and an experience's name), two adjacent capitalised words neither of which is all capitals, a place word, a month or day, a sentence's opening word, or a declared pseudonym

A stretch one pattern has claimed is not claimed again, so one email address is one warning. A form with a wiki names a public subject, and its prose is not read for names. Every warning ends with the same advice: people appear by role, or by a pseudonym you declare.

11. The private filter

The manifest declares what leaves a holograph and what stays, the way the home file system leaves a Holotype's private side behind; this section is the rule, and an implementation applies it.

Where it applies. To everything that takes any part of a holograph off the holder's own copy:

Use What it is
ingest another volume, a library or an index copies the holograph, or part of it, in
package a Thym package carries it, sealed or not, a cryptograph included (Thym, its section 15)
transfer the holograph, or part of it, is copied off the holder's machine, a backup included
projection a program projects it: an isograph, match tokens, an isomorph (section 14)

What it withholds.

  • Marker: .ascribe-private. Every file and folder beside a marker, with everything below them, the marker included. A marker beside the catalog withholds the whole holograph: nothing leaves.
  • Restricted: every node, form, polyform, relation and dialectic item with visibility: restricted, wherever it is written. A kept file that holds one is written out again without it, as its nodes: in the COSM YAML subset, and without its comments, since a comment cannot be tied to the node it was beside.
  • Module D, in every use but a transfer of the whole holograph into the holder's own keeping (section 8.6).
  • Candidates, in every use: every candidate (section 9) and every question about one, the holder's own keeping included, since a candidate is not part of the self until the holder says yes.
  • The catalog's releases:, in every use: it is the holder's own account of what left (section 13).
  • What it cannot read: a symbolic link, and a .cosm file that does not parse, since either may hold what must be withheld.

What it never drops. Everything else, unchanged and byte for byte when its file holds nothing it withholds. A reference to a withheld item keeps its id and gains no name; the filter never rewrites a kept item, never adds a placeholder or a count for what it withheld, and never drops the catalog unless a marker sits beside it.

A pure function of the folder. The filter reads the holograph's folder and nothing else (no setting, no history, no other volume) and gives the files that leave and their bytes; the same folder gives the same answer every time. It runs before anything else in each use, so that a program, a check or a model never sees what it withholds.

private-marker reports, as a note, what each marker would withhold: the files, and how many nodes they hold, or "the whole holograph" when the catalog itself is beside a marker. It withholds nothing.

12. Lenses

A holograph is drawn by the lenses its manifest declares and by MacroCOSM's generic ones (MacroCOSM OES, its section 14). A reader applies visibility before it draws anything; the private filter does not apply, since a lens draws for the holder on the holder's own copy.

Lens Kind Draws
self a mindmap the self at a glance: a branch for each of the ten modules and a leaf for each node
graph a flowchart every typed link between the nodes and the forms, coloured by valence; a form ([ ]), a polyform {{ }}, a relation [/ /]
timeline a table, not a drawing every node that carries a when, one row each (when, id, name, valence, strength), dates and years in order and era words after them
map a flowchart (the generic lens) every module, D included, with every reference field, every typed link and every parts and of
tree a flowchart (the generic lens) the nodes joined by parts, when any node carries parts

The text tree (MacroCOSM OES, its section 14.2) lists the holograph module by module and item by item, as text. In the reader's order a holograph has the lenses self, graph, map and, when its nodes carry parts, tree; timeline and the text tree are drawn by their own renderers. A candidate (section 9) is drawn like any node, with its pending state shown.

13. Releases

A release is one act of the holder's by which something left the holograph: an isograph, a cryptograph, a list of match tokens or an isomorph (section 14). Every release is recorded in the holograph, in the catalog's releases: list, oldest first. The record is the holder's own and private: the private filter withholds it in every use (section 11). It is what Thym's released check reads: nothing is in a package's graph.cosm without a release recorded in the holograph (Thym, its section 15). It is the shape of a Holotype's isotype release in Comprise OES, its section 11.3, kept in a file because a holograph has no database.

Field Holds Required
epoch the epoch the release carries: a quarter (2026-Q4), a month (2026-11) or a week (2026-W45), never the day of an edit yes
as what left: isograph, cryptograph, tokens or isomorph yes
program {name, sha256}: the program that made it, and the sha256 of its .thym bytes yes
trace {run, sha256}: the run's id, and the sha256 of its trace file as the run wrote it, which stays on the holder's machine yes
at when the release was made: a timestamp, kept in the holograph and never in what left yes
sha256 the sha256 of the bytes that left: the isograph's canonical JSON, the sealed file, the token list or the morph volume yes
items the ids of the holograph's items that left, sorted, and for an isomorph the morph's id (M:morph/<slug>); all for a cryptograph of the whole holograph yes
for {path, sha256}: the text a package's excerpt was released for when the release is a package's graph.cosm

A record never holds what was withheld: no id of a withheld item, no count of them and no reason. It never holds a key, a token's key or the party a cryptograph was handed to. A record is appended and never rewritten; a release withdrawn later stays recorded, since what left cannot be recalled.

A release, as a fragment of the catalog:

releases:
  - epoch: 2026-Q4
    as: isograph
    program: {name: open-self, sha256: '2222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222'}
    trace: {run: 2026-11-05-1415-ab12, sha256: '3333333333333333333333333333333333333333333333333333333333333333'}
    at: 2026-11-05T14:15:00Z
    sha256: '4444444444444444444444444444444444444444444444444444444444444444'
    items: [A:affinity/fast-bread, F:form/sourdough]

A holograph with no releases has released nothing, and every holograph written under ascribe/1 reads as such until its holder releases something.

14. Projections: the isograph, the cryptograph, match tokens, morphs

What leaves a holograph leaves by its holder's act, after the private filter (section 11), and is recorded as a release (section 13). In this edition an isograph is made by a Thym program the holder runs (Thym, its section 18); a cryptograph, an isomorph and match tokens are made by the implementation at the holder's act, and Thym moves for them are reserved for a later edition. Every projection keeps four rules:

  1. Default-deny. Only what this section names may appear; anything else is refused, never filtered.
  2. Withhold, never falsify. What does not leave leaves no mark: no placeholder, no gap, no count.
  3. Nothing of the dialectic and nothing pending. No D item, no candidate (section 9), no node whose provenance.state is anything but known, and nothing below confidence 0.5.
  4. No personal data. no-pii runs over what the filter keeps and over the projection itself, at the severity of an error, and a release is refused while any finding stands.

There is no paratype of a person: no outsider's graph, ever.

14.1 The isograph

An isograph (ascribe-isograph/0) is a person's public projection: a separate document with a fixed schema, computed from the holograph by rules, one rule per field, and narrowed by the holder's program, as a company's isotype is in Comprise OES, its section 11.3. It is an entailment made by rule: the holograph entails its isograph, and what is projected holds by construction (MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.1). It is the dial for a person (Thym, its section 18, writes the company's): project h as isograph, under a program that only chooses, and can keep more back but never add a row, a word or a model's entailment. A field added to the model later never flows into it by itself.

{isograph, hid, handle, epoch, sample, program, run, self}
self[]:  kind, form, valence, strength, when, marking
Field Derived from
isograph ascribe-isograph/0
hid the catalog's id without ascribe/
handle the catalog's handle: the pseudonym, never a name
epoch the release's epoch (section 13)
sample true when the catalog says so; absent otherwise
program as the release records it: {name, sha256}
run the id of the run that made it; the release record carries its trace's digest (section 13)
self[].kind the node's kind, one of the ten
self[].form the FEED word of the node's form (its feed.word, where feed.origin is feed); for a polyform, the sorted list of its forms' words, at most eight, every one so grounded
self[].valence the node's valence, when it has one
self[].strength a band of the node's strength: slight (1 or 2), moderate (3), strong (4 or 5)
self[].when the decade of a date or a year (2010s), or an era word already in that shape; any other era word is left out
self[].marking stated

Which nodes may appear. A node of the ten kinds whose visibility is public, whose status is absent or active, whose provenance is known at confidence 0.5 or more, that the filter keeps, and whose form, or every form of whose polyform, is grounded in FEED with origin feed. Nothing else of a holograph may appear: no node's name, summary, links, of, parts, span or provenance, no id, no form named only by an encyclopedia article or in the holder's own words, no relation, no pseudonym, and not the catalog's summary. Free text never leaves, since free text can identify.

Its rules.

  • Assertions, not measurements. No number of anything; the only digits are in the epoch, a decade, the hid, the run's id and the digests.
  • Bands. strength leaves only as its band, and when only as its decade.
  • Sorted. self is sorted by the order of the modules (A B C E I O Q S U Y), then by form, valence, strength and when, so that file order leaks nothing. Two nodes that give the same entry give it once, and nothing counts them. At most twenty entries of each kind; at most 64 kilobytes.
  • One per epoch. A holder releases at most one isograph in an epoch; one who releases more often chooses a shorter epoch.
  • Stated and verified. Every entry is marked stated: the holder's own word. verified is reserved for what a registry can attest of the holder, and of nothing in the self: that one real person holds the key this isograph is signed with, and that this holograph has one holder. Ascribe lists nothing as derivable, so Thym's verified-only-where-derived refuses every mark … as verified in a program over a holograph, and an isograph made by its holder alone marks everything stated. A reader refuses an isograph that carries verified.
  • Canonical JSON. The document is JSON with its keys sorted, no space between tokens and UTF-8 without a byte-order mark; the release's sha256 is over those bytes.

The registry itself, the signing of an isograph and the key it is signed with, an index of isographs, and further fields a holder may open one at a time (each opt-in and marked stated or verified) are reserved for a later edition. An isograph is the holder's to keep or hand on; nothing in this edition publishes one.

The isograph of the holograph of section 4.4, were its node A:affinity/fast-bread of section 5.2 added and marked visibility: public (the two other nodes anchor to a form FEED does not have, and stay):

{"epoch":"2026-Q4","handle":"salt-and-crumb","hid":"aej6l5qrka6c6btarfiy2f7vnj","isograph":"ascribe-isograph/0","program":{"name":"open-self","sha256":"2222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222222"},"run":"2026-11-05-1415-ab12","sample":true,"self":[{"form":"sourdough","kind":"affinity","marking":"stated","strength":"moderate","valence":"against"}]}

A package's graph.cosm (Thym, its section 15) is an isograph made for one text: the same keys, written in the COSM YAML subset, its self narrowed to what the program emitted for that text, and recorded as a release as: isograph with for naming the text.

14.2 The cryptograph

A cryptograph is the holder's encrypted share: a Thym package in its encrypted form (Thym, its section 15.3) of the holograph or of a projection of it, handed with its key to one chosen party. It is, for a person, the sealed package of Comprise OES, its section 11.5.

  • Two kinds. The whole holograph: one holograph row per file the filter keeps, at its own path, module D left out. A projection: projection (projection.json, an ascribe-isograph/0 document), program (program.thym) and trace (trace.cosm, the run's trace as the run wrote it). Nothing else is a cryptograph: no key, no morph's evidence, nothing the filter withholds.
  • The seal. The cipher of Thym's encrypted form under a fresh data key for the package; its catalog's hid is a random id of its own, never the holograph's, so that a cryptograph cannot be joined to the holder's isograph.
  • The key is shown once, stored nowhere, and handed by the holder to the party of their choosing. A lost key is a lost cryptograph.
  • The notice says what it is and names no one.

Hosting a cryptograph for others to fetch is reserved for a later edition, with the registry.

14.3 Match tokens

A match token lets a search find a cryptograph without reading it: a consented, salted digest of one chosen form, {about, token}, token being the lowercase hex HMAC-SHA256 of about, a line feed and the value, under a key made for one cryptograph and never its package key.

about The value
one of the ten kinds: affinity, belief, concern, experience, interest, opinion, quale, skill, understanding, yearning the slug of the node's form, and, when the node carries a valence, a space and the valence word: sourdough against
form the slug of a form the holder chose, whatever nodes anchor to it

A token is made only for a form grounded in FEED with origin feed, and only for a node the isograph's rules let appear (section 14.1), so that a value is a public word whose meaning anyone can work out. Making tokens is the holder's act and is a release (as: tokens): the holder chooses each form, and nothing is made a token by default. The list is {tokens: ascribe-tokens/0, of: <the cryptograph's hid>, epoch, entries: [{about, token}]}, its entries sorted by token. A cryptograph handed over directly has none. Serving tokens, and who holds the key a search needs, are reserved for a later edition, with the registry.

14.4 The bridge to Comprise

A bridge between a company's Holotype and a person's holograph, an employer's seek for a role shaped like an isograph and answered by a candidate's isograph, both speaking Thym, is reserved for a later edition; the common envelope makes it possible.

14.5 Morphs

A holomorph is the holder's morph: the ten scales of Thym's morphs measured from the holder's own texts, as a departure from the null morph, sharpened over a corpus, with any scale the holder pins by hand winning (Thym, its section 13.4). It is an M:morph item in a thym/1 morph volume kept beside the holograph's folder, never inside it (section 4.1): <holograph folder>.morph.cosm for the first, and any name ending .morph.cosm for more, each named in the catalog's morphs: list. Its derived.from holds the digests of the holder's own texts only, as section 9 reads them; a text by anyone else is never measured into it.

An isomorph is a holomorph the holder chose to share: the same shape, marked shared, released by a program and recorded as a release as: isomorph. It leaves with its scales, toward, pinned and confidence, and never with its evidence or derived.from, which quote and point to the holder's texts. A holder may keep several, one for letters and one for work.

15. The command line

enough, the reference implementation, offers one command that checks a holograph. It is given here as the example of how the checks are run, not as a rule of the format:

python -m enough.ascribe lint <folder | holograph.cosm> [--json]

It loads the holograph through enough's MacroCOSM reader, runs the generic checks and Ascribe's own, prints each problem as <severity> <check> <item> (<file>): <message>, and ends with one line: ascribe/1: <n> items, <e> errors, <w> warnings, <k> notes, <q> open questions. It exits 0 when there is no error (warnings and notes do not fail it) and 1 otherwise. It reads only the folder it is given and writes nothing.

On enough's fixtures:

sample-self                   ascribe/1: 26 items, 0 errors, 0 warnings, 0 notes, 1 open questions      exit 0
bad/pii                       ascribe/1: 8 items, 0 errors, 7 warnings, 0 notes, 0 open questions       exit 0
bad/unanchored                ascribe/1: 3 items, 1 errors, 0 warnings, 0 notes, 0 open questions       exit 1
bad/undeclared-relation       ascribe/1: 3 items, 1 errors, 0 warnings, 0 notes, 0 open questions       exit 1

bad/pii carries one of each pattern, invented, and every one is a warning: a phone number, a birth date, a personal name, an email address, an id-like run of digits, an employer field and a street address.

16. Conformance

16.1 A reader must

  1. Read every holograph written under ascribe/1, from any edition of this text, and keep every unknown field and catalog key, releases and morphs included where it does not know them.
  2. Read a session source in both spellings, D:session/<id> and session-<n>, as one session.
  3. Run the generic checks the manifest selects and Ascribe's own (section 10), in one report, with no-pii never above a warning inside the holograph.
  4. Apply visibility before drawing or showing anything (section 12).
  5. Refuse an isograph that carries a field section 14.1 does not name, or the marking verified.

16.2 A writer must

  1. Never write a name, an email address, a phone number, a postal address, a birth date, a government id or an employer's legal name, in any field, key or comment; write people by role or by a pseudonym the catalog declares.
  2. Write a node only from the holder's own word: an answer, an accepted candidate, or what the holder wrote down (section 8, section 9); write an open question, never a guess, and never a form FEED does not have as if it did.
  3. Read only the holder's own texts into a holograph, never a text about them by anyone else.
  4. Apply the private filter (section 11) to every ingest, package, transfer and projection, before anything else reads the holograph.
  5. Refuse a projection while no-pii finds anything in what the filter keeps or in the projection (section 14).
  6. Keep module D, candidates and every node not known at confidence 0.5 or more out of every projection, package and ingest.
  7. Record every release in the catalog's releases: before anything leaves, append records and never rewrite one, and never record what was withheld (section 13).
  8. Keep a morph volume beside the holograph's folder, never inside it (section 14.5).

16.3 Where an implementation and this text disagree

Where an implementation and this text disagree, one of them is in error. The disagreement is settled by correcting the one that is wrong, and the Changes appendix (section 17) records which was corrected, with the section it touched.
