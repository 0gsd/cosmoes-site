The OES specifications

Edition 0.5.4 · revised 2026-11-03 · cosmoes.org home: https://cosmoes.org/

An OES, an organizational enumeration system, describes a domain by enumerating everything in it. It is written as plain-text files that are at once a file system and a relational database, made to be rendered. This page says what that is and how the specifications published at cosmoes.org fit together; each has a home of its own, listed after this text.

The short version

An OES describes its subject as a file system that is also a relational database: every volume (a company's Holotype, a person's holograph, a text's twin, a library's catalog) is a folder of plain-text files or one such file, every module a set of tables, every item a row with a path. Written down once, as a shared grammar, the shape describes a company, a person, a text or a document the same way. The shared grammar is MacroCOSM. A grammar specialized for one domain is a MiniCOSM: Comprise OES, for companies, is the first; Ascribe, for a person, has a specification of its own. Thym is the language of the programs that move meaning between volumes. One organization's additions on top of a MiniCOSM are a MicroCOSM, reserved for a later edition.

1. What an OES is

An OES, an organizational enumeration system, is a system that organizes a domain by enumerating everything in it, as a file system that is also a relational database, made to be rendered.

  • Enumerating. It lists what there is, item by item, each with its sources. What is unknown is an open question with the name of the role that can answer it, never a guess.
  • A file system. Plain text files in folders, on the subject's own machine. Anyone can read them without any application, move them, keep them in version control, and leave with everything.
  • A relational database. Every item has a primary key (its id), its fields are columns, its references are foreign keys, and checks are constraints. The same files can be indexed and queried.
  • Made to be rendered. The description exists to be turned into other forms for other readers: a 3D scene, a summary, an export for an agent, a public projection. A renderer can only show what the description holds, so the description is held to account.

2. Three scales

COSM is the family's name. It is not an acronym: it was Context Ontology Schema and Markup, and before that OPI; both are history. It comes from kosmos, order and world. .cosm is the family's file extension.

Scale What it is Specified by
MacroCOSM the whole language: the shared grammar every MiniCOSM is written in MacroCOSM OES
MiniCOSM an OES for one domain, declared by a manifest: its volume layout, modules, kinds, fields, references, checks and renderers Comprise OES, Ascribe, Thym's thym/1; the rest in MacroCOSM OES, its section 18
MicroCOSM one organization's extension on top of a MiniCOSM: the extra kinds, fields, checks or renderers it needs reserved for a later edition (section 5)

The names borrow an old idea, from Greek philosophy to the Renaissance: that a small world mirrors the order of the great one. Here each smaller scale keeps the order of the larger and adds to it.

3. What MacroCOSM gives every MiniCOSM

Every MiniCOSM inherits all of it, unchanged. The right-hand column gives the section of MacroCOSM OES (https://macro.cosmoes.org/) that defines each part; the examples are Comprise OES's.

Part What it is Section of MacroCOSM OES
Paths, and a URI form every part, module, kind, item and field has an address that respells its id as a location, mozilla-foundation/P/offering/creative-futures; qualified with the MiniCOSM and the volume's database name it is a URI, oes:<minicosm>/<database>/<path> 11
Items, fields and references a row with a stable id and a kind (P:offering/ledger); its fields are columns; a reference is an id written in a field (owner: O:role/Founder), and every reference has two ends 7 and 9
The envelope the columns every item has: id, kind, name, summary, status, visibility, provenance (how it is known, from what, how surely), open questions and typed links; a MiniCOSM adds its own 8
Open questions a value nobody has given yet, {pending: "…"}, written where the value would go, with who can answer it; never a guess 10
Checks constraints on the whole volume, run on every write; a write that breaks one is undone 12
Saved and signed versions an immutable copy of the files at a moment, with a manifest of hashes; a copy the checks allowed, signed by the roles that govern it 15
The private/public split two formats, not one with a setting: the complete private description (the Holotype), kept by its subject, and its small public projection (the isotype), default-deny, released and signed by the subject 16
Renderers anything that turns a volume into another form; renderers only read, and show a reader only what that reader may see 14
The manifest the machine-readable declaration of a MiniCOSM, one .cosm file: what a program needs to read, check and render its volumes 13

Holotype and isotype are taxonomy's words, the one specimen that defines a species and a duplicate of it; they name the two halves of the split in every MiniCOSM, not only Comprise's.

4. What a MiniCOSM declares

A MiniCOSM is declared by its manifest: one .cosm file, outside every volume, that says

  1. its name and version (comprise, 1), and the declaration a volume uses to name it (oes: comprise/1), with any legacy forms it accepts;
  2. its volume layout: a folder or a single file, the catalog at its root, and the places of its parts and modules;
  3. its modules and kinds, and for each kind its fields: at least the reference fields, which the checks follow;
  4. its reference forms: the id forms, the relationship kinds, the closed lists;
  5. its checks, by id, with their severities and what the verdict means;
  6. its renderers and lenses;
  7. its public projection, when it has one: the fixed fields of the isotype, and the rule that derives each one.

A volume names its MiniCOSM with one line in its catalog and never embeds the manifest. A reader that does not know the MiniCOSM or the version a volume declares refuses it with a plain error.

5. What a MicroCOSM may change

A MicroCOSM is one organization's extension of a MiniCOSM. MacroCOSM OES, in its section 17, states these rules as the grammar's; how a volume says that it uses a MicroCOSM, and how a reader loads one, are reserved for a later edition.

It may add: kinds, in any module; fields, on any kind; checks, including stricter versions of the MiniCOSM's own; renderers.

It may never: remove anything; rename anything; loosen anything (a closed list, a required field, a check's severity); change what a path or an id means; weaken the split, by adding to the public projection or opening anything by default. Only the MiniCOSM, in a new version of its projection format, can add a public field, and only as one the subject opts into.

What a MicroCOSM adds carries its name, <microcosm>.<kind> and <microcosm>.<field>, so a later version of the MiniCOSM can never collide with it. A volume written with a MicroCOSM remains a valid volume of its MiniCOSM: a reader that does not know the MicroCOSM keeps its fields and ignores its checks.

6. The specifications and how they relate

Four specifications are published at cosmoes.org, each at its own home.

  • MacroCOSM OES (https://macro.cosmoes.org/) is the grammar every MiniCOSM is written in: the parts in section 3, the file format, and what a conforming reader and writer must do. It is the grammar Comprise OES worked out for companies, read out of it so that nothing in it says "company"; its section 18 registers the MiniCOSMs.
  • Thym (https://thym.cosmoes.org/) is the language of programs over MacroCOSM volumes. Everything a reader does with a volume is generic; everything that moves meaning from one telling to another (a text to its twin, a document to its one-line atems, a Holotype to its public isotype) is a Thym move. A program says what it is for and what it must preserve, and its contracts are checked; programs, morphs, contexts, plans, traces and packages are themselves volumes of the thym/1 MiniCOSM.
  • Ascribe (https://ascribe.cosmoes.org/) is the MiniCOSM for a person: a holograph, the description of one self comprehensive enough that a name is unnecessary, kept private by its subject, with no kind and no field for a name, an address or a government id. What leaves a holograph leaves by a Thym program its holder runs.
  • Comprise OES (https://comprise.cosmoes.org/) is the MiniCOSM for companies, and the first. A company is a set of concerns (a branded face, a subsidiary, a joint venture) joined by relationships, each concern with eight modules, C · O · M · P · R · I · S · E. Its volume is the Holotype, one folder describing one company at a moment; its public projection is the isotype. Its declaration is oes: comprise/1.

Four more MiniCOSMs are registered in MacroCOSM OES, its section 18, and defined there, with no home of their own: cosm (one whole's product tree, outside any Holotype), canon (a library: its files, and what has been derived from them), emf (one text, taken apart into its words, the things it names, its claims and its events) and doc (one document, taken apart into items and compiled back byte for byte). doc is DocOES (MacroCOSM OES, its section 18.5), the form these pages are built in at three densities (atem, short, fabula).

Comprise OES and Ascribe are written in MacroCOSM; Thym is written over it. A shared vocabulary of public forms ("chair", "M4 screw", "invoice": defined once, cited by every MiniCOSM that needs them) is reserved for a later edition.

8. Editions

An edition is a published state of one of these texts. Every text here, this page included, is at edition 0.5.4; the status line says so. An edition is the text's, not the format's: a format's version (thym/1, ascribe/1, comprise/1, the grammar version MacroCOSM OES names) is a line in the files a program reads, and each text's status line names the one it describes. A text may be revised at any time; the canonical edition of each is the page at its home, a copy an application ships or syncs is a copy of an edition published here, and the source and history of every page are at https://github.com/0gsd/cosmoes-site.

9. Implementations

Two products are the reference implementations. enough (https://github.com/0gsd/enough) implements MacroCOSM OES, Thym and Ascribe and reads every registered MiniCOSM: it loads, checks and draws their volumes, compiles and runs Thym programs, and builds documents with DocOES. Comprise implements Comprise OES: it writes and reads Holotypes, runs their checks, saves and signs versions, and projects and releases isotypes. Where an implementation and a text disagree, one of them is in error, and the text's Changes appendix records which was corrected.

10. Open formats

This site exists so that the formats are open. Every format these texts describe is documented here completely enough that a second reader, a second writer, a second vault or a second index can be built from the text alone: the layer is larger than any one product. The files a format describes belong to their subject: plain text, on the subject's own machine, readable without any application, movable and complete, so that a subject who leaves takes everything. The format itself is never sold, and neither is the way out of it: no product and no account stands between a subject and their own files.
